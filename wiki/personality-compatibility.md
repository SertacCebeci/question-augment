# Personality compatibility

> Whether a user trusts and delegates to an assistant depends less on the
> assistant's style in isolation than on whether that style matches the
> user's own conversational leaning.

## Definition / Mechanism

Restricting to the two trait-linked assistant conditions (extraverted
"Titan" vs. conscientious "Europa"), the study scored each of 24
participants on a compatibility index — z(Extraversion) − z(Conscientiousness)
— and regressed the Titan-minus-Europa difference in trust-and-delegation
ratings on it. The slope was significant: b=0.648, F(1,22)=6.38, p=.019,
partial η²=.225. Participants relatively more extraverted than
conscientious rated Titan higher than Europa on trust and delegation, and
participants relatively more conscientious rated Europa higher — the
opposite of a single best assistant.[^c1]

The same direction held for the overall post-interaction composite but was
weaker and not significant on its own: b=0.383, F(1,22)=2.98, p=.098,
partial η²=.119. The authors read this as compatibility being specifically
a trust/delegation effect, not a general satisfaction effect.[^c2]

## Why it matters

This is a second, distinct axis from [[assistant-personality-style]]'s
task-fit: even within one task, the same assistant style is not equally
trusted by every user. It names *whom to match the style to* as a variable
separate from *which task the style suits*.

## Limits

The compatibility slope is fit on 22 degrees of freedom (24 participants,
one regression) from a single lab study with prompted, not measured,
assistant "traits"; the authors themselves note the overall-composite
version of the same effect does not reach significance (p=.098), so the
result should be read as specific to trust/delegation, not general
satisfaction, until replicated at larger scale.
