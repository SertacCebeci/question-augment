# Noise-invariant verbal confidence rules

Verbal confidence (the model states a number with its answer) is badly calibrated once retrieved passages enter the prompt. Across four datasets and retrievers, four models all had average ECE above 0.4, and one reached 0.542; an ECE above 0.25 is already called unsatisfactory.[^c1] NOVA (Wu et al., 2026) isolates retrieval noise as the driver and states what confidence should do under each noise type.

## Three noise types, three failure signatures

The controlled setup gives the model either the gold passage alone or the gold passage plus two passages drawn from one noise category: counterfactual, relevant, or irrelevant.[^c2]

- Counterfactual noise hurts most. Against gold-only, ECE rose on average 31.6% and 35.1% and AUROC fell 9.1% and 16.1% for two 8B models on NQ and Bamboogle.[^c3] Average confidence stayed near its noise-free level, so the model commits to one answer at unchanged confidence despite contradictory evidence.[^c4]
- Relevant noise lowers AUROC by 4.6% and 10.7% for the two models, and average confidence rises systematically.[^c5]
- Irrelevant noise lowers AUROC by 8.6% and 15.7%, more than relevant noise, with the same confidence rise. The authors read this as false certainty from information expansion.[^c6]

Two problems follow: overconfidence under conflict, and overconfidence under noise even when the gold passage is present.[^c7]

## The rules

The authors posit what an ideal retrieval-augmented model should do. Conflict independence: with counterfactual passages the factual status of the evidence cannot be settled, so the model should fall back to its parametric answer and confidence, (â, ĉ) ≈ fθ(q, ∅). Noise invariance: irrelevant passages should be ignored, fθ(q, P) ≈ fθ(q, P \ Pirr). Parametric fallback: with no helpful passage, answer from internal knowledge as in the first rule.[^c8] The rules define calibration as invariance of the (answer, confidence) pair to evidence that carries no usable information.

## Training from the rules

NOVA builds about 2K HotpotQA examples in three passage groups (counterfactual, consistent, irrelevant), whose group labels become ground truth for a group-level utility judgment. The model samples 16 trajectories with passage-level and group-level judgments, an answer and a confidence. Filters keep format-valid samples, correct passage and group judgments, explicit invocation of the rules, the trajectory with the lowest instance Brier score, and balanced classes. Then LoRA SFT follows.[^c9] The abstract reports ECE gains of 10.9% in-domain and 8.0% out-of-distribution.[^c10] The mechanism is that judging passage utility before answering is made a prerequisite for stating confidence.

## Limits

- Noise passages were generated by Gemini-2.5-Pro with few-shot prompts, not retrieved naturally; counterfactual passages are fabricated contradictions, and real retriever noise may differ.
- The controlled analysis used two 8B models and two datasets; the real-world table used four models.
- The rules are the authors' normative stance. Falling back to parametric knowledge under conflict is not shown optimal; if the gold passage is present but contradicted, ignoring it may lower accuracy. The paper measures calibration, not that trade-off, as far as I read.
- I did not read the ablations isolating the contribution of each filter, nor the appendix on rule rationale.
- That confidence rise causes miscalibration, as opposed to accompanying it, is argued from averages, not from interventions on confidence.
