# Ask4VG

> Before a vision-language model answers a medical question, Ask4VG asks
> several rewrites of it under four visual conditions, scores each rewrite by
> how invariant its answer is to the image being missing or wrong, and picks
> the rewrite whose answer depends most on the actual image.

## Definition / Mechanism

For an image-question pair $(I, q)$, a frozen VLM is queried under the
original image, a perturbed image, a blank image, and a mismatched image,
producing answers $y^o, y^p, y^b, y^m$. Visual sensitivity, question-only
bias, and mismatch invariance are computed from these four answers, and
combined into a weak hallucination-risk proxy
$\hat H(I,q) = \alpha(1-S_{vis}) + \beta S_{prior} + \gamma S_{mis} + \delta(1-S_{cons})$.
A regression-plus-pairwise-ranking model $R_\theta$ is trained to predict
$\hat H$ from image/question embeddings and the answer differences, without
any human hallucination label.[^c1]

At inference, a small candidate set of rewrites is generated by a rewriting
prompt that is told to preserve clinical intent; each candidate is scored by
$-R_\theta(I,q') + \lambda_u U(q') + \lambda_s \mathrm{Sim}(q,q') - \lambda_d D_{deg}(q')$
— predicted risk traded off against answerability, intent-preservation, and
a penalty for degenerate rewrites — and the arg-max is asked to the VLM. No
weights on the base model change.[^c2]

On VQA-RAD with Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct, risk-only reranking lowers the
held-out risk proxy from 0.658 (original questions) to 0.623 and raises
exact-match accuracy from 0.337 to 0.356, while rewriting only 20.7% of
questions; unconditional prompt-only rewriting (no risk feedback) instead
*raises* the proxy to 0.696.[^c3]

A 300-sample external check on PMC-VQA, using the VQA-RAD-trained
estimator unchanged, shows the same direction: prompt rewriting raises risk
from 0.6483 to 0.6590, while risk-guided reranking lowers it to 0.6200 and
raises accuracy from 0.3767 to 0.3933.[^c4]

## Why it matters

This is a pre-generation, question-side intervention on stall 2 (which
formulation of a question to ask): instead of scoring candidate questions by
[[expected-information-gain|expected information gain]] about a hidden state, it scores them by predicted
answer-invariance to the *evidence itself* — a check that the question, once
answered, will have actually used the image rather than a language prior.

## Transfer

| | Home field | Here | Survives | Does not |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| counterfactual perturbation as a risk signal | causal inference / explainability (perturb-and-compare attribution) | reworded to "swap the image, not the question, and see if the answer moves" | the perturb-and-compare test itself, used as weak supervision | any causal identification guarantee — $\hat H$ is a correlational proxy, calibrated only against the weak target it was fit to (Pearson 0.816), not against expert-labelled hallucination |

## Limits

The risk estimator is validated only against its own weak, formula-defined
proxy $\hat H$, not against clinician-judged hallucination; a model could
score low risk while still being wrong in ways the four counterfactual
probes do not expose. Both the effect and the estimator come from one
frozen 2B VLM (Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct) on one primary dataset (VQA-RAD,
1,793 pairs); the PMC-VQA check is 300 samples and explicitly called a
sanity check, not a cross-dataset benchmark, by the source. Effect sizes
are modest (accuracy +0.019 and +0.017 across the two datasets); the paper
itself frames the work as a pilot.[^c6]
