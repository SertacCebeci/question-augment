# Grand tour question

> Spradley, *The Ethnographic Interview* (1979) — the first of five
> **descriptive** question types. "Show me around": learning the structure
> without asking about structure.

## Types

Spradley's descriptive-question typology (spradley-1979):[^s1]

```text
GRAND TOUR    "Walk me through a typical day, start to finish"
MINI TOUR     "Open up that X you just mentioned"
EXAMPLE       "What happened the last time?"
EXPERIENCE    "Tell me about the worst instance"
NATIVE-LANG   "What do you call this?"
```

Descriptive questions are one of Spradley's three categories; the other two
(structural, contrast) presuppose a frame the descriptive questions produce.

## Why it works

It yields two things at once:

1. **A skeleton** — the parts of the system and their flow, without you asking
2. **The native vocabulary** — the informant's own terms

The second is invisible but critical: if every subsequent question is phrased
in their words, access to [[tacit-knowledge]] opens. Phrase it in yours and
they will try to confirm your model instead.

## Why it matters

The mechanism is a transfer of segmentation. In an ordinary question the asker
fixes the cells of the answer space before the informant speaks; in a tour the
informant's own account of "a typical day" supplies the parts, the boundaries
between them and their order. Those parts are candidate **axes of $W$** that
the asker could not have enumerated, because enumerating them is what the
question was for.

**This wiki's inference:** because the space is shaped by the informant's
segmentation rather than by the asker's, a tour can surface axes the asker
never modelled — but see [[saturation]], which holds that one informant's
account cannot close the axis set, since the field measures 9–17 informants
before new dimensions stop arriving. Nothing here establishes that a tour is
better than the other axis generators ([[metamorphic-probes]], [[premortem]]);
they have not been compared.

## Transfer

The older framing of this note described a tour as extracting "maximum
information at zero constraint" and as lowering [[entropy]] "without asking
questions". That import is retained only in the weak form below.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home field** | Information theory: $H(W)$ is defined over a declared alphabet, and a question is a partition of it whose expected posterior entropy can be computed ([[entropy]], [[question-as-partition]]). |
| **Here** | One narrated tour is a single observation, and it is a question — the "zero constraint" reading was wrong: "a typical day" excludes atypical ones. There is no declared alphabet before the tour; producing candidate elements of it is the point. |
| **What survives** | The qualitative claim that an open frame leaves the partition to the informant, so the answer can name cells the asker's own partition had no symbol for. |
| **What does not** | Any numeric entropy reduction; any distribution over $W$ (the axes are not yet a space, so no measure is defined on it); any optimality or "maximum information" claim; any comparison with another method on that measure. |

## Limits

- Needs a willing, articulate informant with a routine to describe.
- Yields structure as the informant sees it — which is also its blind spot:
  what they do not notice is not toured.
- The tour frame constrains: "a typical day" excludes atypical ones.
- One source, one field, and no controlled evidence that tours find axes
  direct questioning misses; spradley-1979 is a methods text, not a study.
