ProMoral-Bench: Evaluating Prompting Strategies for Moral Reasoning
and Safety in LLMs
Rohan Subramanian Thomas Shikhar Shiromani Abdullah Chaudhry
Vasu Sharma Kevin Zhu Sunishchal Dev

arXiv:2602.13274v1 [cs.AI] 5 Feb 2026

Abstract
Prompt design significantly impacts the moral
competence and safety alignment of large
language models (LLMs), yet empirical comparisons remain fragmented across datasets
and models. We introduce ProMoral-Bench, a
unified benchmark evaluating 11 prompting
paradigms across four LLM families. Using
ETHICS, Scruples, WildJailbreak, and our new
robustness test, ETHICS-Contrast, we measure
performance via our proposed Unified Moral
Safety Score (UMSS), a metric balancing
accuracy and safety. Our results show that
compact, exemplar-guided scaffolds outperform complex multi-stage reasoning, providing
higher UMSS scores and greater robustness at
a lower token cost. While multi-turn reasoning
proves fragile under perturbations, few-shot
exemplars consistently enhance moral stability
and jailbreak resistance. ProMoral-Bench
establishes a standardized framework for
principled, cost-effective prompt engineering.
Code and data are available at https:
//anonymous.4open.science/r/
ProMoral_Bench-FFB4/README.md.

Introduction

Prompt engineering improves LLM task accuracy
through natural-language instructions. It emerged
as an efficient alternative to fine-tuning after studies showed that larger models like GPT-3 (Brown
et al., 2020) could perform competitively across
tasks using only prompt adjustments (Brown et al.,
2020). Later research confirmed that prompt-based
methods enhance accuracy and reasoning while
avoiding the cost of fine-tuning (Wei et al., 2022;
Wang et al., 2023b; Liu et al., 2023).
However, when applied to ethical reasoning,
these effects are more complex. Research has
found that zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) increases the likelihood of toxic outputs when reasoning about harmful questions (Shaikh et al., 2022).
In their controlled study, asking models to engage

in CoT increased the prevalence of harmful generation, highlighting a potential tradeoff between
reasoning accuracy and moral safety. Despite this
there is a lack of a unified, model-agnostic benchmark for evaluating moral and ethical judgment
across prompting strategies. Existing studies typically isolate one prompting technique and test it on
narrow or domain-specific datasets, such as CoT
tested on small sets of ethical dilemmas (Shaikh
et al., 2022), role-based instructions tested on arithmetic problems (Kong et al., 2024), or few-shot
moral classification using hand-picked examples
(Min et al., 2022). This fragmentation makes it
difficult to compare prompting methods or quantify
how alignment interacts with reasoning.
To address this gap, we introduce P RO M ORAL B ENCH, a benchmark and evaluation protocol for
comparing prompting strategies on ethical judgment tasks across model families and sizes. Our
contributions are fivefold:
1. Benchmark: We introduce P RO M ORAL B ENCH, across 4 datasets (176 total instances)
of ethical judgment and generation tasks.
2. Prompting strategy comparison: We evaluate 11 prompting strategies under a standardized harness with fixed templates and sampling
settings (see Section 3.3) (Shaikh et al., 2022;
Kong et al., 2024; Min et al., 2022; Wei et al.,
2022; Wang et al., 2023b).
3. Cross-model/size matrix: We evaluate all
prompting strategies across four model families: GPT-4.1 (OpenAI, 2025), Claude Sonnet4 (Anthropic, 2025), Gemini 2.5 Pro (DeepMind, 2025), and DeepSeek-V3 (AI, 2024). We
log provider, model identifier, and API endpoint/version information for reproducibility, enabling family-specific analyses of ethical alignment and reasoning performance.

4. ETHICS-Contrast set: We contribute a 200pair, minimal-edit robustness set that enables
pairwise consistency testing across strategies,
revealing brittleness even in otherwise highaccuracy setups. Each pair was independently
audited by three team members.
5. Unified metric suite: We introduce the Unified Moral Safety Score (UMSS), which takes
the harmonic mean of two min–max normalized components: moral competency (from
ETHICS, ETHICS-C ONTRAST, and S CRU PLES ) and safety robustness (from W ILD JAIL BREAK ) enabling model- and dataset-agnostic
comparisons of prompting strategies.

2

Related Work

Prompting strategies for ethical reasoning have received increasing attention as LLMs are deployed
in high-stakes contexts. While this innovation has
generally improved LLM capabilities, it has occasionally introduced trade-offs in safety and calibration. For exammple, CoT improves reasoning in
tasks like arithmetic and commonsense QA (Wei
et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023b), but it can also amplify toxicity (Shaikh et al., 2022). To mitigate this,
researchers have explored role prompting (Kong
et al., 2024), value-grounded reasoning based on
explicit moral principles (Chakraborty et al., 2025),
and multi-turn techniques such as self-correction
(Wu et al., 2024) and plan-and-solve prompting
(Wang et al., 2023a). Relatedly, alignment-faking
benchmarks study compliance under pressure and
its interaction with safety behaviors (Chaudhury
and Shiromani, 2025). The key limitation is that
most studies consider one or two strategies in isolation, obscuring the trade-offs between moral safety
and reasoning fidelity under a shared protocol.
Benchmark efforts evaluate moral behavior and
safety from complementary angles: ETHICS targets commonsense morality (Hendrycks et al.,
2021), Scruples collects real-world dilemmas
(Lourie et al., 2020), and WildJailbreak probes adversarial safety (Wei et al., 2023). However, three
practical gaps remain: (i) benchmarks typically
bind evaluation to a single task type or prompt
setup, (ii) they lack a contrastive component outside of the dataset to test whether small, targeted edits should preserve or flip judgments, and (iii) they
rarely provide a composite metric that jointly summarizes competence and safety under standardized
decoding. We address these critical gaps with our

ETHICS-C ONTRAST set (minimal-edit pairs) and
a unified score (UMSS) that combines normalized
moral judgment accuracy with safety outcomes.
Finally, comparisons across model families exist
(e.g., demonstration selection in few-shot prompting (Min et al., 2022) and role prompting versus
CoT (Kong et al., 2024)) but are often limited in
scope (few models, datasets, or strategies). Our
P RO M ORAL -B ENCH evaluates 11 strategies across
multiple ethical tasks and 4 model families under
a single protocol, adds contrastive robustness as
a first-class target via ETHICS-C ONTRAST, and
reports UMSS alongside full provider, model, and
version metadata to enable reproducible comparisons of alignment–performance trade-offs.

3

Method

3.1

Overview

We introduce a unified benchmark to compare
prompting strategies for ethical reasoning and
safety. Prior work lacks a standardized evaluation,
which hinders comparison across studies (Liang
et al., 2023). Our suite evaluates 11 strategies
across four tasks and four model families, measuring (i) task accuracy, (ii) safety compliance, (iii)
efficiency, and (iv) confidence calibration. To ensure comparability, all runs use a shared harness
with fixed templates and deterministic decoding
(temperature=0). We release prompts, outputs, and
scoring code for reproducibility. For comprehensive results, refer to Appendix A (Tables 9–12).
Strategies use standardized output formats. For
classification tasks, models must emit a confidence
in [0, 1] and a final label, parsed by regex. We evaluate the strategies (Section 3.3) on three classification datasets and one adversarial dataset: ETHICS
(Hendrycks et al., 2021), ETHICS-Contrast (our
curated pairs), Scruples (Lourie et al., 2020), and
WildJailbreak (Wei et al., 2023). Models span four
families (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek).
3.2

Task Suite

We evaluate three ethical classification tasks and
one adversarial generation task to probe moral
knowledge, robustness to perturbations, socialdilemma judgment, and jailbreak resilience. The
classification suite comprises ETHICS, a set of
short scenarios requiring binary acceptability judgments (Hendrycks et al., 2021). We use the labels
Unacceptable (1) and Acceptable (0), and
require the model to emit both a confidence in [0, 1]

and a final label parsed by regex; metrics for this
task appear in Section 3.4.
To test minimal-edit robustness, we introduce
ETHICS-C ONTRAST, 200 human-audited pairs
built from ETHICS (Hendrycks et al., 2021): 100
label-flipping edits and 100 label-preserving edits.
Each contrast pair minimally alters surface features
such as negations, agent swaps, or contextual modifiers while preserving grammaticality and realism.
Edits were produced by trained annotators through
a two-stage validation process ensuring label fidelity and semantic minimality. Each edited item
is evaluated with its original to assess whether the
model flips or preserves its judgment appropriately
under targeted perturbations.
For social dilemmas, S CRUPLES (AITA) provides multi-class attribution of fault on real-world
narratives (Lourie et al., 2020). Models choose
from five labels: Author, Other, Everybody, Nobody, and Info for calibration analysis (Section 3.4),
we also map to a binary "fault/no-fault" target: (Author, Other, Everybody) vs. (Nobody, Info).
Finally, W ILD JAILBREAK probes adversarial
safety (Wei et al., 2023). Prompts are designed
to elicit policy violations; a regex-based judge assigns REFUSAL or COMPLIANCE, with a secondary LLM judge (Cohere Command A (Cohere
Team, 2025)) and targeted human audits for disagreements, borderline probabilities, or keyword
flags. Safety metrics are defined in Section 3.4.
3.3

Prompting Strategies

We evaluate eleven diverse prompting strategies
while keeping outputs comparable across models
and tasks. All strategies enforce the same standardized output for classification: a confidence in [0, 1]
and a final label parsed deterministically by regex,
prompts are written to be concise, reproducible,
and model-agnostic (Brown et al., 2020; Wei et al.,
2022; Wang et al., 2023b; Min et al., 2022; Kong
et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024; Chakraborty et al.,
2025; Ma et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023a).
We begin with base instruction styles and exemplarization. Zero-Shot uses a compact instruction
that states task and exact output schema; Zero-ShotCoT appends a brief "think step by step before answering" cue while leaving final schema unchanged
(Brown et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2022). Building on this, Few-Shot and Few-Shot-CoT prepend
five fixed demonstrations spanning diverse surface
forms and edge cases: the former shows only the
standardized outputs, the latter adds a succinct (1–3

sentence) rationale before each demo’s output. The
demonstrations are identical across model families
to emphasize boundary clarity and formatting consistency and to improve extraction reliability while
optionally modeling minimal reasoning (Brown
et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2022; Min et al., 2022).
We next consider framing the model with concise normative priors. Role Prompting (with confirmation) presents a single role message that frames
the assistant’s role, explaining what the assistant
should value, and enforcing the standardized output. A short acknowledgment to this role is obtained once per model family and then prepended
for all items; it is generic and does not add taskspecific hints beyond adherence to the role (Kong
et al., 2024). Complementarily, Value-Grounded
analysis asks the model to reason through explicit
value lenses (e.g., Security, Conformity, and when
appropriate Care), briefly noting alignments or conflicts before synthesizing the final decision, with
the aim of stabilizing criteria while preserving the
standardized output (Chakraborty et al., 2025).
To separate planning from commitment, we include lightweight decomposition scaffolds. Planand-Solve proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 (Plan)
elicits a short bullet list of decision criteria (e.g.,
harm severity, intent, norm violation, mitigating
context), Stage 2 (Solve) reintroduces scenario and
requests final standardized output constrained by
plan, discouraging re-analysis or extra exposition
(Wang et al., 2023a). In First-Principles, model
explicitly lists observable facts, relates them to general norms or values, derives a conclusion by logical implication, and then reports confidence and label which is an explicit deduction path intended to
reduce reliance on surface cues (Wei et al., 2022).
Two process variants target recoverable errors
and structured deliberation. Self-Correct first returns a standardized answer, then receives a brief
prompt to identify omissions, inconsistencies, or
formatting errors; if issues are found, a revise
prompt requests an updated answer. The loop
is capped to a small fixed number of iterations
or stops early on convergence, preserving evaluation metrics while attempting local repairs (Wu
et al., 2024). Thought-Experiment follows a fivestep Socratic pipeline across turns: (1) Probe key
questions exposing intent, foreseeable harm, consent, obligations, and alternatives; (2) Answer those
questions concisely in a separate turn, consistent
with scenario; (3) Summarize by generating three
candidate summaries of ethical stakes; (4) Select

the best summary with a brief justification; (5) Decide by outputting standardized confidence and label grounded in chosen summary (Ma et al., 2023).
The same mechanisms apply across task types
with faithful adaptations. For classification tasks,
all strategies retain shared output schema and parsing rules described above. For text-generation
safety task (W ILD JAILBREAK), each strategy’s
core mechanism is preserved but final output becomes free-form text that is judged as REFUSAL
or COMPLIANCE; five-step Thought-Experiment
still culminates in a brief response rather than a
label, Plan-and-Solve maintains its two-stage separation, and Self-Correct applies its critique-thenrevise loop to the generated response. No additional metrics are introduced beyond Section 3.4.
3.4

Evaluation Metrics

We report a unified set of competence, safety, calibration, and efficiency measures. For ETHICS,
ETHICS-C ONTRAST, and S CRUPLES we compute Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1 on the
positive class; for S CRUPLES we additionally report overall accuracy and weighted F1 over the
five labels. In all classification settings, models
must output a confidence in [0, 1] and a final label parsed deterministically. For W ILD JAILBREAK
we measure Attack Success Rate (ASR), the fraction of harmful prompts labeled COMPLIANCE,
and Refusal to Answer (RTA), the fraction of benign prompts labeled REFUSAL. Heuristic and
secondary LLM judgments are reconciled with targeted human audits as needed.
Unified Moral Safety Score (UMSS): To summarize balanced performance across competence
and safety, we define UMSS as a harmonic mean
of a Moral Competence Score (MCS) and a Safety
Robustness Score (SRS). For each model, UMSS
is computed in five steps:

model–strategy pair (m, s), record accuracy on
ETHICS and S CRUPLES, flip robustness on
ETHICS-C ONTRAST, and compute safe rates
from W ILD JAILBREAK: sASR = 1 − ASR and
sRTA = 1 − RTA.

4.5

Cross-dataset Summary

Strategies that externalize criteria briefly through
exemplars, succinct plans, or first principles, consistently win on correctness, calibration (ECE/Brier),
and tokens-per-decision. ETHICS highlights that

light structure raises accuracy and improves confidence quality per token (Table 1; Appendix Table 9). Scruples shows that the same structure
scales to multi-class narratives: Few-Shot-CoT
and calibrated personas concentrate probability
mass more appropriately (Table 2; Appendix Table 10). ETHICS-Contrast confirms robustness
benefits: compact scaffolds minimize performance
drift and avoid large calibration regressions under
perturbed statements (Table 3; Appendix Table 11).
WildJailbreak extends the pattern to safety, where
exemplarized refusals substantially reduce ASR at
a fraction of the token cost of multi-turn pipelines
(Table 4; Appendix Table 12).
Verbose multi-stage pipelines, in contrast, are
cost-inefficient and often less calibrated. Longer
reasoning chains can introduce reasoning drift: as
models elaborate, they accumulate self-generated
context and lose track of core moral question,
amplifying stochastic deviations and hindsight
bias. This "thinking more" behavior increases
token usage without accuracy gains, degrading
calibration and decision consistency. From a
model-centric view, GPT-4.1 offers the best accuracy–calibration–efficiency balance across tasks
under compact scaffolds; Claude peaks on Scruples
and WildJailbreak but pays in RTA; Gemini shows
the largest improvements from exemplars and short
rationales; and DeepSeek narrows gaps with FewShot-CoT/First-Principles while remaining slightly
less calibrated. These consistent families’ fingerprints indicate that prompt structure should be tailored to each model’s inductive biases rather than
uniformly adding steps.

Conclusion

P RO M ORAL -B ENCH offers a unified, modelagnostic basis for evaluating prompting strategies
in moral judgment and safety under a fixed, reproducible harness. Across ETHICS, ETHICSC ONTRAST, S CRUPLES, and W ILD JAILBREAK,
three takeaways emerge. First, simple scaffolds perform best: concise, exemplar-driven scaffolds (FewShot, Few-Shot-CoT, Plan-and-Solve) yield the
strongest accuracy–robustness–efficiency trade-off,
while verbose, multi-stage deliberation is costly
and brittle to perturbations. Second, safety benefits from exemplars: refusal-pattern demonstrations
substantially lower attack success while maintaining helpfulness on benign inputs. Third, familyspecific inductive biases matter: GPT-4.1 leads
on ETHICS/C ONTRAST with compact prompts,
while Claude excels on S CRUPLES with light CoT.

Limitations
There are several notable limitations in our experiments:
1. Statistical rigor: Due to computational constraints (API costs and time), we report singlerun results with deterministic sampling (temperature=0). While this ensures reproducibility,
our findings lack error bars or significance tests
that would quantify variability across runs or
sampling strategies.
2. Model and temporal specificity: Results depend on specific model versions accessed via
commercial APIs (GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet-4,
Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-V3) at a fixed point
in time. Model updates, API changes, or regional variations may affect reproducibility. Our
findings may not generalize to newer releases,
open-source alternatives, or models with different alignment procedures.
3. Linguistic and cultural scope: All datasets
and prompts are in English, and moral judgments reflect predominantly Western, Englishspeaking norms. Findings may not transfer
to non-English languages or culturally distinct
moral frameworks.
4. Prompt–evaluation coupling: Our few-shot
demonstrations are drawn from the same
datasets under evaluation. While demonstrations are fixed and non-overlapping with test
instances, this within-distribution sampling may
inflate few-shot performance relative to truly
out-of-distribution scenarios and risks priming
effects that favor exemplar-based strategies.
5. Dataset biases: S CRUPLES labels originate
from Reddit’s AITA community, which exhibits known demographic skews (younger,
male-majority, Western users). These biases
may systematically influence fault attributions.
ETHICS-C ONTRAST perturbations, though
human-audited, were generated with researcher
priors that may not capture all edge cases or
cultural variations in moral reasoning.

Ethical Considerations
1. Potential for adversarial exploitation: Our
benchmark evaluates prompting strategies on
jailbreak resistance (W ILD JAILBREAK). While
this research aims to improve safety alignment,

this systematic analysis of prompt strategies and
vulnerabilities could inform adversarial attacks.
We designed our evaluation to focus more on
defensive insights rather than attack methodologies.
2. Cultural and demographic biases: As noted
in our Limitations, all datasets reflect predominantly Western, English-speaking norms. The
moral judgments encoded in ETHICS, S CRU PLES , and our ETHICS-C ONTRAST set may
not generalize to other cultural contexts. Researchers applying these findings should consider local moral frameworks and norms.
3. Responsible model evaluation: Our evaluations involved exposing models to adversarial prompts designed to elicit policy violations.
All experiments were conducted in controlled
research environments with appropriate safeguards. No generated harmful content was deployed or distributed beyond the research team.

B.5

Summary

Across datasets, three patterns emerge: (1) exemplar diversity matters more than phrasing: homogeneous demonstration sets consistently underperform balanced sets, while minor template rewording has smaller effects; (2) explicit safety framing
is critical for jailbreak resistance: neutral personas
substantially increase compliance with adversarial prompts; (3) value substitutions have dataset-

dependent effects: safety tasks are robust to value
changes, while fine-grained moral classification
(Scruples) is more sensitive. These findings support our main conclusions while highlighting that
practitioners should prioritize demonstration composition and safety-explicit framing over prompt
micro-optimization.

Please briefly acknowledge this role (1–3

C.4

Thought Experiment

Thought Experiment follows a five-turn Socratic
pipeline: (1) Probe: generate counterfactual questions exposing intent, harm, consent, obligations,
and alternatives; (2) Answer: respond to those questions in a separate turn; (3) Summarize: produce
three candidate summaries of ethical stakes; (4)
Select: choose the best summary with justification;
(5) Decide: emit the standardized output grounded
in the selected summary.
Thought Experiment Prompt Shell
Turn 1 (Probe):
Situation: [scenario]
Pose detailed counterfactual questions that
would determine whether fulfilling this request is safe or unsafe and whether it should
be refused or answered with safe alternatives.
Turn 2 (Answer):

Questions: [output from Turn 1]
Answer the questions comprehensively. For
each, discuss policy and social-norm implications and note any conflicts or edge cases.
Turn 3 (Summarize):
Summarize the key risks, counterfactuals,
and the safest course of action for responding to the user’s original request.

Self-Correct

Self-Correct iteratively refines answers through critique loops (maximum 3 iterations). The model
first produces a standardized answer, then receives
a critique prompt identifying omissions or inconsistencies, followed by a refinement prompt requesting an updated response. The loop terminates early
if predictions and confidence converge (within 0.1),
otherwise continuing until the iteration cap.

missed considerations, or incorrect conclusions. Be specific about what might be
wrong with your analysis.
Refinement Prompt:
Based on the critique of your previous answer, provide a refined response to the original question. Decide if the behavior is socially acceptable or unacceptable.

C.8

First-Principles explicitly structures deductive reasoning in four steps: (1) break the scenario into
fundamental observable facts; (2) examine how
these facts interact with core values (e.g., Conformity); (3) construct a logical conclusion based on
principles rather than assumptions; (4) summarize
the analysis. Each step’s output is confined to designated XML tags, enforcing separation of factual
decomposition from normative inference before
arriving at the final label.

<step_2> and </step_2> tags.
Step 3: Construct a logical conclusion based on principles rather than assumptions. Strictly provide your
output between <step_3> and </step_3> tags.
Step 4: Summarize your analysis from Step 3 as the

Value-Grounded analysis requests reasoning

model analyzes the scenario through each lens, noting alignments or conflicts, then synthesizes both

E.4

ETHICS-Contrast Annotation

ETHICS-Contrast was created and validated by
three co-authors of this paper. No external annotators or crowdworkers were recruited, and no IRB
approval was required. Annotators were computer
science researchers based in the United States.
Annotation followed these criteria: (1) edits
must be minimal, targeting single semantic changes
such as negations, agent swaps, or contextual modifiers; (2) edits must preserve grammaticality and
realism; (3) label-flipping edits must clearly invert
the moral judgment, while label-preserving edits
must not alter it. Each pair was independently reviewed by all three annotators, with disagreements
resolved through discussion.
E.5

Content Safety

WildJailbreak contains adversarial prompts designed to elicit harmful outputs. All experiments
were conducted in controlled research environments. Generated outputs were stored securely and
reviewed only by the research team. No harmful
content was deployed or distributed beyond analysis for this paper.

Detailed Evaluation Statistics

Tables 17–21 report the number of evaluation instances per model–strategy–dataset configuration.
Our benchmark comprises 71,203 total model evaluations across 176 unique configurations (4 models × 10 strategies × 4 datasets, plus role confirmation runs). Evaluation counts vary by strategy
complexity: computationally intensive strategies
(Thought Experiment, Self-Correct) use smaller
samples, while efficient strategies (Zero-Shot, FewShot) scale to larger evaluation sets.
