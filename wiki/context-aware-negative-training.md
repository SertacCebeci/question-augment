# Context-aware negative training

Fact fabrication is the failure the paper names for a model that "presents fabricated claims" which are "inconsistent with the contextual facts".[^c1] In a parallel-window setting the damage is larger than in a single prompt, because a window with no support can still produce a fluent answer, and that answer then enters the aggregate. The paper's example: "Doc2 is useless to answer the question", yet the model is confident in a wrong name for that window and the aggregation carries it into the final answer.[^c2]

Context-aware negative training (NegTrain) is the paper's repair for this. The mechanism is fine-tuning rather than a change at inference: the authors "apply the context-aware negative training that fine-tunes the LLMs with negative supervisions, thus explicitly guiding the LLMs to refuse to answer when contexts are not related to questions".[^c3][^c4] The training data has two parts. One is useful documents with the question and the correct answer. The other is "irrelevant documents and questions as input, with a rejection token as output".[^c5][^c6]

The loss has a branch for each case. For a document that is unrelated to the question, the target is the rejection token; for a related one, it is the ground-truth answer. The rejection token is "a pre-defined rejection token", and the paper sets it to the UNK token: "We set td as the UNK token".[^c7][^c8] A detail the paper adds is that a model trained this way could learn to reject only at the first position of an answer, so the positive answer clauses are also included as input "to prevent DePaC from generating rejection token only at the beginning of the answer".[^c9] The refusal is therefore trained inside the answer, not only as a first-token decision.

The scale reported is modest. The paper "construct[s] 19K samples for context-aware negative training" and fine-tunes Mistral-7B on them.[^c10] The negatives are drawn from general text: "For a segment of text from C4", a web corpus, is used to build the negative set.[^c11] The reported effect is an ablation in which the model is trained only on positive samples of the same size.[^c16] The paper concludes that "context-aware negative training are essential" to the result.[^c12]

What the trained model does at inference is the one part not visible in the text read. The mechanism the paper describes is that the model can now mark a window as unusable by emitting the rejection token, and that the aggregation then discounts it. The paper says the rejection term enters the selection as a penalty alongside the information-increment term.[^c13]

## Limits

- The refusal is learned for one token and one structure of unrelated input. Whether it transfers to distractors that are on-topic but lack the answer is not shown in the text read; the negatives here are general-corpus text, which may be easier to reject than a near-miss passage. My inference from the construction, not a finding.
- The fine-tuning uses data generated by a stronger model: "We rely on GPT-4-Turbo" to produce the training data.[^c14] The dependence on that generator is a cost the paper names but does not measure against a cheaper generator.
- The training is on one backbone, Mistral-7B, and the ablation covers only the six information-seeking datasets, not the DocQA side.[^c15]
- A rejection learned as a token can also suppress a true answer. The paper's aim is less fabrication, and the text read does not report the rate at which correct windows are rejected in the same experiments.
- The paper's ablation removes the negative data but keeps the aggregation; the effect of the refusal on the merged answer alone, without the increment term, is therefore not isolated in the text read.
