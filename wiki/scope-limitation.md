# Scope limitation

> Before an evidence shortfall enters the [[modified-opinion-matrix]] it is
> classified by *who caused it*, and the class changes the response: a
> shortfall is not a limitation at all if an alternative procedure closes it;
> one imposed by the party under examination triggers a request, an
> escalation, and — where practicable — leaving rather than disclaiming.

## Definition / Mechanism

**Three origins.** ISA 705 (Revised), A8 names the inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence "a limitation on the scope of the audit" and gives three sources: circumstances beyond the entity's control; circumstances relating to the nature or timing of the auditor's work; and limitations imposed by management.[^c1]

The examples separate them: records destroyed or seized (A10); appointment too late to observe the inventory count (A11); management preventing the auditor from observing the count or requesting external confirmations (A12).[^c2]

**The alternative-procedure test.** An inability to perform a specific procedure "does not constitute a limitation on the scope of the audit" if sufficient appropriate evidence can be obtained by alternative procedures (A9); only when this fails do the matrix's cells apply.[^c3]

**The imposed case has its own ladder.** Only the third origin class — the management-imposed limitation — carries a prescribed sequence. If, after accepting the engagement, management imposes a limitation likely to require a qualified opinion or a disclaimer, the auditor shall request that management remove it (para. 11); on refusal, shall communicate the matter to those charged with governance and determine whether alternative procedures are possible (para. 12).[^c4]

If evidence still cannot be obtained and the possible effects could be material and pervasive "so that a qualification of the opinion would be inadequate to communicate the gravity of the situation", the auditor shall withdraw from the audit where practicable and possible under law, and disclaim only if withdrawal before the report is not practicable or possible (para. 13(b)).[^c5]

Before withdrawing, the auditor shall communicate to governance any misstatements identified that would have modified the opinion (para. 14); practicality of withdrawal depends on how far the engagement has progressed (A13).[^c6]

The imposed origin also feeds two other judgements: A9 says limitations imposed by management "may have other implications for the audit, such as for the auditor's assessment of fraud risks and consideration of engagement continuance".[^c7]

**The US standard chooses the other rung.** AS 3105 treats restrictions "whether imposed by the client or by circumstances" as one class whose consequence depends on "the importance of the omitted procedure(s)" (.05–.06), and for client-imposed restrictions that significantly limit the scope, "ordinarily the auditor should disclaim an opinion" (.07) — the disclaimer, where ISA 705 prefers withdrawal.[^c8]

## Known as

| Field | Name there | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| IAASB (ISA 705 Revised) | inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence; limitation on the scope of the audit (A8 gives them as the same) | exact |
| PCAOB (AS 3105) | restrictions on the scope of the audit; client-imposed restrictions | close — same object, but the origin does not change the ladder: client-imposed → ordinarily disclaim, no withdrawal step |

[^c9]

Not this concept: the **disclaimer of opinion** is an *outcome* of a scope limitation and belongs to [[modified-opinion-matrix]]; a **management-imposed limitation** is one of the three origin classes (A8(c)), a member of the taxonomy, not a name for it; **sufficient appropriate audit evidence** (ISA 500) is the standard the shortfall is measured against and is not read here.

## Why it matters

On stall 10, [[abstention]] prices "I don't know" by the asker's cost of an empty answer and asks nothing about *why* the answer is empty. ISA 705 makes the origin of the shortfall a variable with three consequences a derived procedure lacks: (1) no non-answer is admissible until an alternative route to the same evidence has been tried and failed (c3); (2) when the shortfall was imposed by the party the question is about, the standard's first responses are a request and an escalation to a third party, not a hedge (c4); and (3) at the top of that ladder the prescribed act is to stop being the answerer — withdrawal — with "I don't know" as the fallback, because a [[scoped-exception-commitment|qualified answer]] under an imposed shortfall understates its gravity (c5). On stall 4 the same taxonomy says a source that blocks the answerer's evidence is a different object from a source that lacks it, and that the block itself is evidence for a separate judgement (fraud risk, continuance — c7).

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| In its home field | an engagement with a contract (ISA 210), a management that controls the records, a governance body distinct from management to escalate to, and a legal option of withdrawal |
| Here | an answerer whose evidence is blocked by the asker, by the thing asked about, or by its own reach; usually no governance body and no engagement to leave |
| What survives | the origin taxonomy; the alternative-procedure test as a precondition on any non-answer; the ranking that an *imposed* shortfall is answered by request and escalation before by a hedge; the reading of the block itself as evidence |
| What does not | withdrawal — an answerer to a single question has nothing to withdraw from, so the top rung collapses into the disclaimer, which is exactly the PCAOB's rung (c8); the governance escalation needs a third party the setting may not have |

[^c10]

## Limits

- The two standards disagree on the top rung (withdraw vs disclaim) and neither reports an outcome; which rung better serves a user is not measured in either source.
- The origin classes are given by examples (A10–A12), not by a decision rule; a shortfall caused jointly by timing and by management is not assigned.
- The claim that an imposed shortfall is evidence for a separate judgement is A9's "may have other implications" — a licence, not a finding.
- `[?]` The AS 3105 quotations (c8, and the PCAOB row of c9) were checked against the live PCAOB page only; `pcaob-2017` has no archived text extract, so those claims are unverified until it is archived.
- Only ISA 705's text was read; nothing from ISA 500's text is claimed here.
