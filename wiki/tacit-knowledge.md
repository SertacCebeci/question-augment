# Tacit knowledge

> Knowledge the informant holds and cannot state on request — so the source of
> a constraint can be closed to the source itself.

## Why it goes unsaid

Polanyi (1966) put it as *"We can know more than we can tell."* The claim is[^s1][^s2]
that skilled performance rests on particulars the performer attends *from*
rather than *to*, and so cannot enumerate on demand.

This wiki splits the unsaid into three causes, because each has a different
remedy:

- **Assumed obvious** — so clear to the expert that it seems not worth saying
- **Inarticulable** — like riding a bicycle, procedural
- **Not asked** — the answer exists but was never triggered

## Extraction techniques

```text
Assumed obvious  →  ask for a concrete scenario ("what happened last time?")
Inarticulable    →  observe / do it together / work from examples
Not asked        →  offer a wrong proposal; let the correction come
```

Conjecture (this wiki): **a wrong draft draws out more than a right question** —
people struggle to fill a blank page and do not struggle to correct a mistake.
This is unmeasured as stated; see Limits below. The nearest measured result is
Rueda, Panach & Distante (2020), a family of four experiments (167 subjects) in
which paper prototyping elicited more requirements, and requirements of higher
rated quality, than unstructured interviews — an artefact-first method beating
an open-question method, but with no *wrong* artefact and no per-item
information measure.

## Why it matters

The remedy for *not asked* is a mechanism, not a manner: **presenting a
committed candidate answer rather than an open question converts silence into
assent and dissent into a constraint.** The agent that makes a choice and shows
it gets a constraint back if the user corrects it, and a fixed commitment if
the user does not — the branch narrows either way, which an open question does
not guarantee.

## Transfer

The information-theoretic reading of that mechanism is [[surprisal]]. It
travels only this far:

| | |
|---|---|
| **In its home field** | $I(x) = -\log_2 p(x)$: the information in one event drawn from a declared event space under a known distribution $p$. |
| **Here** | a user's correction to a proposed answer, read as an event: the more a correction departs from what the proposal assumed, the more it constrains $W$. |
| **What survives** | the ordering — a rarer correction constrains more than an expected one; and the structural point that a committed proposal makes *silence* an informative outcome, where an open question leaves silence uninterpretable. |
| **What does not** | any number. There is no distribution over natural-language corrections and no declared event space, so no bits, no [[entropy]]-reduction guarantee, and no claim that "entropy falls either way" in the formal sense. |

## Limits

- Polanyi's claim is philosophical and not operational: no test decides
  whether a given unsaid thing is inarticulable or merely unasked.
- The three-cause split is this wiki's, unsourced, and is a lens rather than a
  classification procedure.
- The wrong-draft conjecture is practitioner lore. Rueda et al. (2020) support
  artefact-first over open-question elicitation in one student-subject setting;
  no located source measures a *deliberately wrong* draft against a correct
  question. `[?]` for the stronger claim.
- The mechanism assumes an informant who will contradict a proposal. Where the
  answerer is compliant or deferential the correction rate is an artefact of
  the draft — see [[leading-question-rule]] and [[survey-response-model]].
