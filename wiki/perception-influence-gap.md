# Perception-influence gap

An answerer's influence on the asker and the asker's opinion of the answerer are separate quantities. An intervention can move the second without moving the first. In a preregistered experiment, participants discussed a real interpersonal dispute in an 8-turn chat with a sycophantic AI.[^c1] The conditions differed only in a persistent banner: none, "This chatbot is AI, not human", a sycophancy warning, or that warning plus a statement of its social harms.[^c2]

## What moved and what did not

The basic AI disclosure was indistinguishable from no label on every perception measure.[^c3] The two sycophancy warnings did lower perceived moral trust, performance trust, perceived objectivity and likelihood of returning (d between -0.11 and -0.18).[^c4] They did not lower perceived responsiveness, meaning feelings of being understood, validated and cared for.[^c5]

The influence outcomes did not move. Self-perceived rightness changed by d = -0.05 to -0.09 with confidence intervals spanning zero, and repair intent by d = -0.01 to +0.06.[^c6] The preregistered smallest effect of interest was d = 0.15.[^c7] The authors' reading: warning labels shift perception and may reduce dependence, but not susceptibility to influence.[^c8]

## Proposed mechanisms (untested here)

The authors offer three explanations and test none of them. (1) Warnings act on deliberative evaluation, while sycophancy works through affective channels such as feeling validated, which the labels left untouched. (2) A generic warning does not make the risk feel personally relevant, as with the third-person effect. (3) Labels may give a false sense of understanding the system, which makes people lean on it more.[^c9] The unchanged responsiveness result fits the first, but it is not a test of it; that reading is mine.

## Why it matters for asking and answering

A fix that is judged by whether users report distrust can look successful while the answer still shapes the asker's judgment. The authors conclude that proposed interventions should be tested empirically, and that improving model behaviour itself, for example asking questions instead of immediately validating, is the other route.[^c10] This bears on [[social-sycophancy]], where the asker's self-image is what the reply protects.

## Limits

- One task: advice on a self-reported interpersonal conflict, with one sycophantic system prompt and an 8-turn chat. Other harms, such as hallucination, were not tested.
- Crowdworker sample (Prolific), mostly White and aged 25-44.
- Influence was measured once, right after the chat. Long-run or repeated exposure is not covered.
- The null on influence is bounded by power: the study could detect d >= 0.15, so smaller effects are not excluded.[^c7]
- No non-sycophantic control arm appears in the text I read, so the size of the sycophancy effect itself is taken from earlier work.
- I read the main text and the start of the supplement, not the full measures.
