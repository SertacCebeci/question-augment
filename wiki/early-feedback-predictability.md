# Early feedback and predictability

Most feedback waits for a closed unit, but some does not. Duncan's early annotations, from "a limited corpus of two dyadic dialogues", classed a substantial share of vocal backchannels and nods as "prior to the end of the unit".[^a1][^a2] Early feedback is the case where a listener responds inside the unit the speaker is still building, which the feedback-relevance account of [[feedback-relevance-space]] does not license by closure.

Howes and Eshghi's account is that the listener predicts the rest. Early feedback "indicates that the speaker's truncated semantic unit was locally predictable enough to be already understood without having been fully articulated".[^a3] The listener "has made some prediction about the upcoming linguistic material", and so can respond before the input is complete.[^a4] The paper's corpus finding is consistent with this: early feedback "often occurs at points at which the remainder of the semantic information unit is already predictable".[^a5]

My reading of the Dynamic Syntax account, whose detailed section I did not read in full: a parser holding a predicted continuation can treat the feedback as already grounding the material to come. The conclusion states the same reading: feedback at a non-FRS "gets interpreted as if it had done so" when the feedback is early and "the (rest of the) informational unit is predictable".[^a6]

**The measure.** In the BNC sample, the early cases are "the 81 acknowledgements and 7 CRs that were categorised as early".[^a7] The paper's prediction for them is that the speaker need not finish: "there should be no need for the speaker to complete their utterance".[^a8] I did not find the outcome of that prediction in the text read here, so I do not report it.

For clarification requests the paper expects a different shape. Early CRs "are likely to be formulated as a compound contribution", directly continuing the truncated utterance, and, unlike early backchannels, "actually articulating the predicted material as a" question.[^a9][^a11] The transfer to asking is this (my inference): when the answerer can predict the rest of the question, a clarifying question can be started before the asker has finished, and it then names the material the listener already expected.

## Limits

- Predictability is judged by the analysts from the surrounding context. The paper does not measure it, and the early categories are coded by hand over a small sample.
- Early CRs are very few. The paper says "there are simply not enough cases of early and late CRs to draw any strong conclusions", and its explanations focus on backchannels for that reason.[^a10]
- Duncan's early proportions rest on two dyadic dialogues; the paper's own sample is one British English corpus.
- The idea that a parser can "already understand" an unfinished unit is a claim about the model. Whether a human listener's feedback before completion reflects understanding or only expectation is not separated here. My inference.
