# Soft dependency retention

The mechanism is a way of reducing [[false-credit-propagation]] without throwing away partial credit. Each criterion is treated as a latent binary event. The local judge's score is taken as an observation, not as part of the model: "treated as observed input to the graphical aggregation model rather than as a variable generated by"[^c1] the graph. Dependencies are typed edges from a licensing criterion (parent) to a licensed one (child), with prerequisite edges for graded support and activation edges for applicability.[^c2]

The discount is a retention factor. Each edge (j, i) carries a value λ in [0, 1], which "specifies how much child-event probability is retained when parent event Cj is unsupported."[^c3] Concretely, the child's score is multiplied by a retention factor for each parent that is unsupported. The paper's wording is that "the local score pi is multiplicatively discounted by" each unsupported parent.[^c4] A supported parent imposes no discount. The result is monotone: "events can only suppress, not inflate, child-event probabilities."[^c5]

The default values encode the intended difference between edge types. Weak prerequisites keep 0.6, strong prerequisites 0.2, and activation edges 0.0. The paper's reading is that "Thus activation edges impose the strongest" suppression, strong prerequisites intermediate, and weak prerequisites the weakest.[^c6] Under an activation edge an unlicensed child is effectively switched off, so a penalty attached to an absent context cannot count (my inference from the update with a retention of zero).

Direct summation over the joint of these events is exponential in the number of criteria.[^c19] The reward is therefore computed on a linear-time approximation: marginals are updated in topological order, with each non-root criterion's probability multiplied by a factor per parent. The authors state the scope of this update: "This update is exact for nodes with at most one parent, and for multi-parent nodes whose parents are"[^c7] mutually independent. Otherwise "it is a mean-field approximation to the"[^c8] joint of the parents. The reward is the "normalized expected signed utility" over these marginals.[^c9]

The reported effect is a trade-off. Against flat aggregation, the authors' diagnostic shows lower leakage with less loss of licensed utility than a hard gate: "Hard gating achieves slightly lower leakage,"[^c10] "but preserves much less licensed utility than GEAR."[^c11] Stronger suppression pushes leakage down but costs task score: "further reduces leakage but degrades task performance, indicating that moderate soft suppression"[^c12] gives the best trade-off in their ablation. The sensitivity study reports that "Increasing suppression strength consistently reduces" leakage across settings.[^c13]

## What survives the transfer

The formalism is a conditional Bayesian network with monotone conditional probability tables, borrowed from probabilistic graphical models: "GEAR defines a conditional Bayesian network over"[^c20] latent criterion events. What survives is the monotonicity and the topological update. What does not survive automatically is the probabilistic reading of judge scores. The paper says the scores "need not" be calibrated (see Limits), so the marginals are a bookkeeping device for suppression, not calibrated probabilities of criteria being met (my inference).

## Limits

- The retention values are fixed by edge type, not learned or calibrated for each task: "GEAR uses fixed type-level retention factors"[^c14] and "and bounded soft judge scores, which need not"[^c15] be calibrated probabilities. The numbers 0.6, 0.2 and 0.0 are the authors' defaults; their sensitivity analysis varies suppression strength but does not show these values are optimal elsewhere.
- The update is a mean-field approximation for correlated parents: "the topological update becomes a meanfield approximation when multiple parent events"[^c16] are correlated.
- Only monotone relations are modelled. "It models monotone prerequisite and activation relations, where an unsupported parent"[^c17] can only suppress. Compensatory, alternative and mutually exclusive relations are outside the model.
- The graph is the weak point: "Incorrect or missing edges may suppress"[^c18] valid utility and leave false credit uncorrected. The paper's audit covers the graph only partly: "human audit evaluates the precision and type accuracy of retained edges, but does not measure the"[^c21] recall of omitted dependencies.
- The evidence is three benchmarks and "with two policy backbones show that GEAR"[^c22] improves; the headline gain, "of up to 15.5% over flat aggregation."[^c23] holds for those settings only.
