# Modified-opinion matrix

> The one deployed protocol that tells a professional answerer *which* kind
> of non-commitment to emit derives it from two judgements, not from one
> uncertainty score: is the shortfall in the **evidence** or in the
> **finding**, and is its effect **confined** or **pervasive**? The four cells
> hold three outputs — and one of them is a full commitment with a named hole.

## Definition / Mechanism

**The two variables.** ISA 705 (Revised), para. 2 establishes three modified opinions — qualified, adverse, disclaimer — and makes the choice depend on (a) the nature of the matter: whether the statements *are* materially misstated, or, for an [[scope-limitation|inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence]], *may be*; and (b) the auditor's judgement about the pervasiveness of the effects or possible effects.[^c1]

**The table (A1).**

| Nature of the matter | Material but not pervasive | Material and pervasive |
|---|---|---|
| Statements are materially misstated (evidence obtained, finding negative) | Qualified opinion | Adverse opinion |
| Inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence | Qualified opinion | Disclaimer of opinion |

[^c2]

**Pervasive** is defined (para. 5(a)) by three tests, any one sufficing: the effects are not confined to specific elements, accounts or items; or if so confined, represent or could represent a substantial proportion of the statements; or, for disclosures, are fundamental to users' understanding.[^c3]

**A fourth route to the empty cell.** Para. 10 requires a disclaimer, in "extremely rare circumstances involving multiple uncertainties", when evidence on each uncertainty *was* obtained but their potential interaction and cumulative effect make it not possible to form an opinion — a disclaimer with no evidence shortfall at all.[^c4]

**Three sentences, not three levels.** A qualified opinion says *except for the effects of* the described matter the statements present fairly — *except for the possible effects* when the cause is an evidence shortfall (para. 17); an adverse opinion says they *do not* present fairly (para. 18); a disclaimer says the auditor *does not express an opinion* and rewrites "have been audited" to "was engaged to audit" (para. 19).[^c5]

The reasons are mandatory: a modification from an evidence shortfall must state the reasons for the inability (para. 24).[^c6]

Hedge phrasings are banned by name: "with the foregoing explanation" or "subject to" are not to be used in the opinion "as these are not sufficiently clear or forceful" (A20).[^c7]

**The evidence/finding line is a prohibition, not a gradient.** The US standard states it from the other side: a disclaimer "should not be expressed because the auditor believes, on the basis of his or her audit, that there are material departures" — an answerer that *found* the statements wrong may not say "I don't know" (PCAOB AS 3105, .45).[^c8]

## Known as

| Field | Name there | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| IAASB (ISA 705 Revised) | modified opinion — the family; the table in A1 | exact |
| PCAOB (AS 3105, from SAS 58 / AU 508) | departures from unqualified opinions | close — same three outputs, but the scope-limitation choice is keyed on "the importance of the omitted procedure(s)" (.06) with no nature × pervasiveness table |

[^c9]

Not this concept: **qualified**, **adverse** and **disclaimer of opinion** are the function's values, not names for it. Two neighbours this note points at without sourcing them: **materiality** (ISA 320, not read here), the threshold below which no matter enters the table; **emphasis of matter** (ISA 706, not read here), which flags a matter without modifying the opinion. Both are logged as gaps.

## Why it matters

On stall 10 the wiki's non-answer is a single act priced against a single score ([[abstention]]). ISA 705 says the non-answer has a *shape*, computed from two judgements the score does not carry. First, "I don't know" (disclaimer) and "no" (adverse) are distinguished by *where the defect lies* — in the answerer's evidence or in the thing asked about — and the second may never wear the first's words (c8). Second, a full commitment with a scoped exception is a first-class output whose wording records which kind of exception it is (c5). Third, the empty answer has two routes — pervasive evidence shortfall, or evidence on every part whose *interaction* cannot be evaluated (c4) — so good evidence on each part does not entail an answer on the whole.

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| In its home field | a fixed assertion set (the financial statements) authored by management, audited against a named reporting framework, with a quantitative materiality threshold fixed before the work and a legal reporting duty |
| Here | an answerer's reply to a question; the "statements" are the asker's premise and the answer's own content, and there is no authored assertion set to audit against |
| What survives | the two-variable derivation (evidence vs finding; confined vs pervasive); the scoped-exception form as a distinct output; the requirement that the reasons for a non-answer be stated; the prohibition on hedge words in place of a named form |
| What does not | materiality — no analogue of a pre-declared quantitative threshold exists for a question; "pervasive" rests on the auditor's judgement with three verbal tests and no metric, so the second variable arrives as a judgement, not a computation; the withdrawal option (ISA 705 para. 13) presupposes an engagement one can leave |

[^c10]

## Limits

- The table is normative. Nothing in ISA 705 measures how often auditors choose the cell the standard implies or whether users read the three forms differently; that the matrix improves on a single score is this note's inference from its structure, not a finding.
- The PCAOB claims (AS 3105 .45 and .06; c8, c9) are checked against the live standard page only — no archived extract exists yet, so they stand [?] unverified until one is archived. The ISA 705 half of the note does not depend on them.
- "Pervasive" is judgement with three tests, one of magnitude ("substantial proportion") and one audience-relative ("fundamental to users' understanding"); reading it as *extent* is faithful to test (i) only.
- The evidence/finding split presupposes the answerer can tell an evidence shortfall from a negative finding. In audit this is fixed by procedure (ISA 500, not read here); for an answerer with no procedure it is an unverified self-report.
- Materiality (ISA 320) and emphasis of matter (ISA 706) are unread neighbours; the Transfer row's "what does not" takes materiality to be a threshold declared before the work, which this note does not source. Both are gaps.
- Only the standards' text was read; no empirical literature on modified opinions enters this note.
