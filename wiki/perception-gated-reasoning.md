# Perception-gated reasoning

An answer verified only at its end does not show how it was reached. The PEARL paper states the gap as "RLVR for VLMs verifies only the final textual output, critically neglecting the foundational step of visual perception."[^c1] Its remedy adds a second object to each reasoning question: a set of perception sub-questions about the image, which the paper calls a perception checklist. The checklist is built from the question's own source pair, "task-aligned perception checklist derived directly from corresponding QA pairs."[^c2] Its answers are kept short on purpose: "their answers are typically concise and high-quality verifiable (e.g., a number or label)."[^c3] Short answers are what let a rule-based verifier score each probe.

The probes then act as a gate on the reasoning. At each training step the model answers the probes "without intermediate reasoning", several times over a group of samples, and "answer those probes" is the perception rollout.[^c4] The mean probe reward over the group, written R̄p, is the gate. If it is zero, the instance is skipped for that step: "perform the following reasoning rollouts and moves to next" the next training step.[^c10] If it is positive, the reasoning rollouts go ahead. The authors put the effect in two lines: "the outcome of this check acts as a fidelity gate"[^c5], and "if the model fails the perception check, the reasoning update is halted."[^c6] The stated purpose is that "it prevents the model from reinforcing spurious reasoning chains,"[^c7] so a correct final answer built on a misread image is no longer rewarded for its reasoning.

The same signal is also used softly. The paper treats the mean probe reward "as a soft reliability prior that" measures whether a reasoning trace is anchored in the image.[^c8] The group-normalised reasoning advantage is multiplied by min(R̄p, 0.5), so a weakly perceived trace is down-weighted rather than dropped.

What the mechanism is, in one line: a commitment (here, a policy update) is conditioned on passing short, verifiable checks of the premises it uses. The paper's own summary is that the model learns "to reason correctly only when it has proven it can see correctly."[^c9] On the answerer's side of the wiki's map this is the commitment step: the reasoning is credited only after the evidence it depends on has been checked in a form that can be scored.

My reading, not the paper's claim: the structure transfers to a human asker or answerer, since the gate is a rule about when to commit and not a fact about VLM weights. What does not transfer is the apparatus. The gate is a training-time filter on a model's updates, the probes are generated by an external model (the paper says "We use GPT-4.1 [20] for perception" checklist construction[^c11]), and nothing in the text tests a person who checks evidence before reasoning.

## Limits

- The evidence is RL training of Qwen2.5-VL-3B and -7B on ViRL39K, scored on math and visual reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, LogicVista, WeMath, DynaMath). There is no human asker and no open-ended question. The headline gain is "a +9.7% improvement over the baseline and +6.6%"[^c12] over GRPO on MathVerse; the gain is a benchmark number, not a measure of better questions.
- The gate is lenient. R̄p is zero only when every probe answer in the group is wrong, so one correct probe answer by one sample lets the reasoning update through. That follows from the definition; the slices I read do not report how often the gate halts training.
- The probes are anchored to the original QA pair, and the paper concedes the limit: "only exploits information referenced by the original QA"[^c13] pair, so the checklist may miss visual detail the question never mentions.
- The step-wise ablation is one cumulative roadmap on the 7B model, not a factorial test. The checklist alone brings "a clear average gain"[^c14], and the gate is added as one of the last steps, so its isolated effect is not reported separately. The probe-only setting "does not consistently outperform Reasoning-Only"[^c15], so the probe signal alone is not the whole story.
- The efficiency claim is about early stopping. The text says "the early stopping mechanism effectively reduces training time"[^c16], measured against DAPO on 16 GPUs in one comparison.
- The premise, that outcome-only RLVR is blind to perception, rests on a diagnostic on one benchmark, WeMath, which the paper calls "a preliminary diagnostic analysis."[^c17]
