# Erotetic inference

Ordinary logic lets only declarative sentences be premises and conclusions. Wiśniewski points to a class of thought processes where the conclusion is a question: the premises may be declaratives, declaratives plus a question, or a question alone.[^c1] The everyday case is arriving at a question from what one has already accepted.[^c2] His worked opening example takes two accepted sentences about Plato's theory of ideas and asks what question follows from them.

He also separates two things. "The arising of a question" names a relation between a question and the background it arises from. "A question arising" would suggest a property of the question itself.[^c3] So whether a question is well posed depends on the background of accepted sentences, not on the question alone. The chapter's notes mention a related term, "evocation", which his earlier work called weak generation.[^c4]

## Limits

The archived text is only the publisher's abstract, the opening of the chapter and its reference notes. It gives no formal definition of "arising", no soundness conditions and no measurements. Everything beyond the three claims above, including the formal account of when a question follows from premises, is unread. The claim that a question's legitimacy is background-relative is my reading of the relation-versus-property remark, not a quoted result.
