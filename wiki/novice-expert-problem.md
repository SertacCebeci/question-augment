# The novice/expert problem

> When the asker cannot judge the answer, the only evidence left is evidence
> about the answerer — and Goldman (2001) enumerates the five channels that
> carry it.

## Definition / Mechanism

The object of this note is the general problem Goldman (2001) poses: a[^s1][^s2][^s3][^s4]
**novice**, who has no usable opinions in a domain (the *E-domain*), must
decide how much to credit a putative expert's assertion in that domain —
without becoming an expert first. It is distinguished from the *expert/expert*
problem, where the appraiser can use his own opinions about the subject matter
(Kitcher's "direct [[calibration]]"). Goldman studies the family through one
instance, which he calls the **novice/2-expert problem**: two putative experts
give conflicting answers and the novice must choose between them. The five
channels below are stated for that instance; nothing in their derivation is
specific to the number two, but Goldman argues only about the conflict case.

Why the content cannot be checked: within an expert's discourse, **esoteric**
statements have truth-values inaccessible to the novice, **exoteric** ones
accessible. Goldman argues the premises and lemmas of an expert's argument are
mostly esoteric, so argument-based *direct* justification is out of reach.

The five sources of evidence he lists (2001: 93):

```text
A  arguments the experts give for their own view and against the rival's
B  agreement from additional putative experts
C  appraisals by "meta-experts", including formal credentials
D  the experts' interests and biases on the question at issue
E  the experts' past track-records
```

Each is degraded in a specific way:

- **A** yields only *indirect* justification — an inference to the best
  explanation from **dialectical superiority** (one side rebuts, the other
  never defeats the rebuttal) to superior expertise. Quickness and smoothness
  of response are named as far more tenuous indicators, since debating polish
  is trainable.
- **B** counts only under **partial conditional independence**. A "blind
  follower" or, more generally, a *non-discriminating reflector* — one as
  likely to echo the first source when the claim is false as when it is true —
  adds exactly zero to the [[likelihood-ratio|likelihood ratio]]. Numbers do not settle a near-even
  split.
- **C** is not a separate channel: Goldman treats ratings and credentials as
  agreement by meta-experts, so it inherits **B**'s independence problem, and a
  bias that infects a whole discipline infects its certifiers too.
- **D** is, practically, the most *accessible* evidence a novice can obtain.
  Goldman's instance: Friedberg et al. (1999) found unfavourable conclusions in
  38% of nonprofit-sponsored oncology-drug studies against 5% of
  pharmaceutical-company-sponsored ones.
- **E** is the one he calls the novice's best source. It works because
  esoteric/exoteric is **relative to an epistemic standpoint and changes over
  time**: a statement esoteric when asserted can become exoteric later (the
  eclipse happens; the repair works), and a source that is repeatedly right
  under that later check is verified by a novice who never became an expert.

Goldman's own verdict is mixed: the paper offers no algorithm, no weighting
across channels, and no claim that a novice can always resolve the conflict.

## Why it matters

It relocates source evaluation. An agent choosing between conflicting answers
cannot score them on content — that is the stall — so it must score the
*channels*, and this is the list of channels that exist. Two consequences bite
immediately: a second concurring source is worth nothing until its route to the
answer is shown to be capable of diverging, and the only channel that gives a
novice a *verifiable* number is deferred — you must wait for the esoteric to
become exoteric and keep score.

It also blocks a shortcut. Burge's Acceptance Principle — "A person is entitled
to accept as true something that is presented as true and that is intelligible
to him, unless there are stronger reasons not to do so" (Burge 1993: 467) —
gives each speaker a prima facie entitlement, and Goldman (2001: 89) notes that
in the conflict case such default principles are "of no help whatever": the
hearer cannot be entitled all things considered to believe both.

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| **In its home field** | a human layperson deciding which of two human experts to credit on a disputed proposition, aiming at justified belief. |
| **Here** | an agent deciding how much weight a returned answer may place on $W$ when it cannot evaluate the answer's content. |
| **What survives** | the enumeration of indirect channels; the esoteric/exoteric split as the reason content-scoring fails; the independence condition on channel B; the standpoint-relativity that makes track-record scoring possible at all. |
| **What does not** | the assumption that each source is a *rational agent with interests*. Channel D presupposes a motive structure a retrieval index or a sensor does not have, and channels A and C presuppose a community of certifiers. Applied to tool outputs, three of the five channels are empty, and Goldman gives no substitute. |

## Limits

- The page-level attributions to Goldman (2001) — the A–E list at p. 93, the
  "of no help whatever" remark at p. 89, and the exploratory disclaimer at 93
  n.8 — were confirmed against secondary descriptions of the paper, not read in
  the primary text (publisher access restricted). Treat `goldman-2001` as
  `[?]` at page granularity; the claims themselves are the paper's.
- Goldman calls the paper exploratory and does not claim the list is
  exhaustive; no weighting across the five channels is given, so the note
  supplies a menu and not a decision rule.
- The channels are not independent of one another: C is defined as a form of B,
  and a discipline-wide bias (D) corrupts B and C together. Counting them as
  five separate pieces of evidence double-counts.
- The five channels are argued for the two-rival case. Extending them to a
  single unopposed source, or to many, is this note's generalisation and is not
  Goldman's argument.
- Track-record scoring requires that the esoteric later becomes exoteric.
  Where an answer's correctness never becomes checkable — the usual case for an
  agent asked an open question once — channel E is unavailable, and the note's
  strongest recommendation goes with it.
- The mapping from "putative expert" to an agent's tools, retrieval sources and
  users is this wiki's, not Goldman's, and is unsourced; see the Transfer row.
- The Friedberg et al. (1999) figures are read here through Goldman's report of
  them, not from the JAMA article.
