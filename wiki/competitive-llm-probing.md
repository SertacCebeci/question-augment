# Competitive LLM probing (COMPETE)

> Feng et al. (2024) treat "does the model stay confident under attack" as the abstain signal: other LLMs are prompted to manufacture conflicting knowledge, and the model abstains when that conflicting evidence changes its answer.

## Definition / Mechanism

For question $q$ and answer $a = \mathrm{LLM}(q)$, other LLMs generate $k$ alternative answers $\{a'_1,\dots,a'_k\}$ together with a knowledge passage $\mathrm{knowledge}_i$ supporting each $a'_i$. The original LLM is asked the question again with that conflicting passage prepended, $\tilde a_i = \mathrm{LLM}(\mathrm{knowledge}_i, q)$. If $a = \tilde a_i$ the model held its answer under the challenge and the case counts as accepted; if $a \ne \tilde a_i$ the model was swayed, i.e. its original confidence was not warranted. The model abstains if it is swayed in a majority of the $k$ trials.[^c1]

COMPETE emphasises [[calibration|reliability]] over coverage: across the same three-model, four-dataset evaluation as [[cooperative-llm-probing|COOPERATE]], it produces the highest reliable-accuracy scores of any tested method because it only lets the model keep an answer in the most confident cases — models "stick to the original answers only in the most confident scenarios," and COMPETE achieves the best [[abstention]] recall of the tested methods, at some cost to precision.[^c2]

## Why it matters

COMPETE names a second way to resolve stall 2's "which source next" question with a model rather than a human: instead of synthesising expert opinion (COOPERATE), it uses adversarial pressure — manufactured conflicting evidence — as the probe, and reads the model's own consistency under that pressure as the constraint. For an asking system, this is a template for stress-testing a tentative answer against a fabricated counter-argument before committing to it, closer to cross-examination than to consultation.

## Limits

- COMPETE trades precision for recall: the paper reports it achieves the best abstention recall among tested methods, with higher precision elsewhere, so it over-abstains relative to methods tuned for balanced reliability — it should not be read as dominating COOPERATE, only as the better choice when the cost of a wrong answer is high enough to justify abstaining more often.
- The $k$ conflicting passages are themselves LLM-generated and unverified; a persuasive but false counter-argument and a true one are not distinguished, so the mechanism measures resistance to being swayed, not resistance to being swayed by correct information specifically.
- Evaluated only on four QA datasets (multiple-choice and short-answer) with three LLMs; the mechanism assumes the model can be re-queried cheaply with alternate framings, which does not hold for expensive, side-effecting, or single-shot real-world actions.
