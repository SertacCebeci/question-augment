# Appraisal-gated evidence synthesis

> Compression into one answer happens in two filters, not one: first a fixed
> rubric prunes the evidence pool, then the user's own stated objectives prune
> what is left — and the answer only ever cites what a database query, not a
> language model, can verify.

## Definition / Mechanism

DeepER-Med (Wang et al. (2026)) frames a deep-research[^s1]
answer as a pipeline of five stages: intent decomposition, retrieval,
appraisal, interpretation, synthesis. The two stages that do the compressing
— gathered evidence into one answer — filter on different things, in a fixed
order.

**Appraisal filter.** Every item retrieved for a sub-question is screened
"according to predefined criteria emphasizing contextual relevance,
evidentiary strength, and methodological quality" — a fixed,
question-independent rubric applied before the user's own objectives enter
at all. This is the stage the paper calls transparent and inspectable: what a
downstream reader can audit is which item passed which criterion, not only
the final prose.

**Constraint filter.** What survives appraisal is narrowed a second time,
against something the rubric does not encode: "background information
embedded in the query, together with user-provided research objectives, is
treated as an explicit constraint that narrows the scope of candidate
evidence produced by the agentic collaborative network" The order is
fixed — quality first, fit-to-purpose second — so an item can be
well-supported and still be dropped for not answering *this* question, but a
poorly-supported item is never let through for fitting the question well.

**Verified synthesis.** The surviving set for each sub-question is
re-interpreted and merged so that "DeepER-Med produces a concise final
answer accompanied by a detailed analytical report, with both outputs
explicitly grounded in the supporting evidence" The one hard guarantee
at this step: "references are retrieved via direct queries to the source
database API rather than generated by LLMs, ensuring that the reference list
remains verifiable and free from hallucination" Compression here is not
a model summarizing its own memory — every citation the answer ends with
resolves to a database record fetched independently of the sentence that
cites it.

## Why it matters

Most distillation mechanisms on this stall (the sufficient statistic, rate
distortion, the information bottleneck) specify *how much* of the input to
keep. This one specifies *by what, and in what order* — a fixed rubric, then
a per-query constraint — and adds a structural fix for one specific failure
mode of LLM-mediated synthesis, a citation that resolves to nothing, by
routing every reference through retrieval rather than generation.

## Limits

The paper's ablations bear on upstream intent decomposition, not on the two
filters described here: it reports no experiment that removes the appraisal
criteria or the constraint filter and measures the resulting accuracy, so the
two-filter ordering is a description of the built system, not an isolated,
measured effect. Expert review found the failure mode the filters do not
prevent: the system sometimes "assembled multiple relevant studies without
fully establishing biological relationships among them, leading to responses
that resembled parallel summaries rather than integrated explanations" —
appraisal and constraint chose defensible evidence, but synthesis still
occasionally failed to fuse it into one answer rather than several
paraphrased side by side. Other cases showed "misalignment with question
constraints or insufficient attention to temporal relevance", i.e. the
constraint filter is applied imperfectly, not just occasionally bypassed.
Finally the mechanism assumes a queryable, verifiable evidence database
(biomedical literature APIs); nothing in the paper measures whether
appraisal-then-constraint ordering, or the verified-citation guarantee,
survives in a domain without one.
