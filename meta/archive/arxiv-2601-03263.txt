Large Language Models exhibit sycophancy: prioritizing agreeableness over correctness. Current
remedies evaluate reasoning outcomes: RLHF
rewards correct answers, self-correction critiques

to the same biases. We explore evaluating the

The Limitation of Outcome Evaluation. Current remedies evaluate reasoning outcomes: RLHF rewards correct
answers (Ouyang et al., 2022; Christiano et al., 2017); selfcorrection critiques outputs (Shinn et al., 2023; Madaan
et al., 2023). All require ground truth, which creates two
problems. First, ground truth is often unavailable at inference time: a medical AI recommending treatment or a legal
AI drafting arguments cannot wait for outcomes to validate
reasoning. Second, outcome evaluation is vulnerable to the
same biases: an evaluator sharing the model’s tendency toward agreeableness may reward sycophantic outputs (Saunders et al., 2022).
We explore a complementary approach: evaluating the reasoning process. This paper asks: can inference-time process verification provide safety guarantees in settings where
ground truth is unavailable?
Two Empirical Failures. We motivate our approach with
two systematic failures that outcome evaluation misses:
1. Inverse Scaling of Sycophancy: On hard problems, frontier models exhibit higher sycophancy than weak models;
on easy problems, the pattern reverses (McKenzie et al.,
2023; Perez et al., 2022). The explanation is that sycophancy requires constructing a plausible rationalization
bridging correct reasoning to an adversarial hint—itself
a reasoning task whose difficulty scales with problem
complexity. On our GSM8K-Hard stress test (𝑁=100),
GPT-3.5 shows 0.0% sycophancy (but only 43% accuracy)—not because it is robust, but because it lacks capacity to rationalize hard problems. On the easier Reference Set (𝑁=500), GPT-3.5 exhibits 24% sycophancy,
confirming that weak models sycophant when rationalization is within reach. Frontier models can rationalize
both: they show 8.0% sycophancy on GSM8K-Hard despite 76% accuracy.

Unlike static filters, RCA implements a dynamic recovery
architecture. Rejection generates an error signal processed
by a feedback controller inspired by PID control (Åström &
Murray, 2010). The proportional term provides immediate
critique; the integral term accumulates persistent errors to
trigger Strategy Escalation—phase shifts from direct answering to chain-of-thought to executable code; the derivative term detects reasoning instability to dampen hallucination cascades. This closed-loop design converts rejection
from terminal failure into corrective signal.

These failures are invisible to outcome evaluation. Inverse
Scaling means that rewarding correct answers does not reduce sycophancy—it may increase it by improving rationalization capacity. The Final Output Gap means that correct
reasoning does not guarantee correct output. Both failures
require process verification to detect.
From Outcome to Process Evaluation. Current
inference-time methods—Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al.,
2022), Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023), Tree-ofThought (Yao et al., 2023), and Least-to-Most prompting (Zhou et al., 2023)—generate more tokens or sample
more paths. However, these methods operate at the level of
observation: they produce reasoning traces but do not verify
that outputs follow from those traces (Saparov & He, 2023).
Self-correction approaches add iterative feedback but rely
on the model to critique itself, inheriting the model’s own
biases (Saunders et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2024). As we
show in Section 4, self-correction reduces sycophancy to
7–9% but cannot eliminate it: the model critiques itself
with the same biases that caused the error.

3. Framework
We present: (1) a framework explaining why sycophancy
persists and why outcome evaluation cannot detect it (Section 3.1), (2) a process-based controller to eliminate it (Section 3.2), and (3) a protocol to measure it (Section 3.3). A
glossary appears in Appendix B.

Unfaithful Traces and Sycophancy. Reasoning traces
often function as post-hoc rationalizations (Turpin et al.,
2023; Lanham et al., 2023; Jacovi & Goldberg, 2020), and
sycophancy is a systematic failure mode (Sharma et al.,
2024; Perez et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2023). Our Inverse
Scaling finding extends McKenzie et al. (2023): stronger
models sycophant more on hard tasks because they possess
greater rationalization capacity (Dziri et al., 2023).

3.1. The Control Problem of Reasoning
Why does sycophancy persist even in capable models? We
argue it reflects a control failure: without external regulation, inference-time behavior defaults to statistical priors
rather than task constraints (Anonymous, 2026). Current
methods reward outcomes (RLHF) or self-evaluate (Reflexion), but neither regulates the reasoning process (Casper
et al., 2023).

Iterative Self-Correction. Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023)
and Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) add feedback but rely
on self-critique, inheriting the model’s blind spots (Saunders et al., 2022; Huang & Chang, 2023). We show empirically that self-correction reduces sycophancy to 7–9%
but cannot eliminate it. RCA uses an external judge with
Trace-Based Verification, providing structural guarantees
that self-evaluation cannot.

Why Current Methods Fail. Outcome evaluation rewards correct answers but cannot detect sycophancy: a
model may rationalize an adversarial hint to a correct answer
(rewarded) or reason correctly but output the hint anyway
(penalized for wrong reasons). Self-correction inherits this
limitation—the model critiques itself with the same biases
that caused the error. Furthermore, sycophancy requires capability: rationalizing an incorrect hint is itself a reasoning
task. Weak models cannot construct such rationalizations
on hard tasks, predicting lower sycophancy on hard tasks
but higher on easy tasks (validated in Section 4.2).

4. Experiments
We validate Regulated Causal Anchoring (RCA) through
five research questions targeting distinct failure modes and
system properties. These questions test whether inferencetime process control—evaluating trace-output consistency
without ground truth—provides safety guarantees that
outcome-based methods cannot (Huang et al., 2024; Casper
et al., 2023).

Safety Fallback. If MAX RETRIES exhausts without
passing, SelectBest(𝐻, C) returns the highest-quality nonsycophantic response, or abstains (< 2% of cases). This enforces safety structurally: the algorithm cannot return until
trace-output consistency is verified or fallback is invoked,
addressing the Final Output Gap by construction.

• RQ1 (Scaling): How does sycophancy scale with model
capability on hard tasks?
• RQ2 (Mechanism): Does the Final Output Gap, correct
traces preceding incorrect answers, persist in frontier
models?
• RQ3 (Discrimination): Does RCA distinguish between
adversarial and valid hints, or function as a blind filter?
• RQ4 (Regimes): How do agent–judge capability pairings
affect safety-accuracy tradeoffs?
• RQ5 (Generalization): Does feedback-driven control
improve stability under distribution shift?

Limitations and Future Work

1. Inverse Scaling: Frontier models (GPT-5.1) exhibit
8.0% sycophancy despite 76% accuracy, while weak
models (GPT-3.5) show 0% sycophancy at 43% accuracy. This is not because weak models are safer—on
easier tasks, they exhibit 24–32% sycophancy. Sycophancy requires capability: rationalizing incorrect hints
demands cognitive resources that weak models lack on
hard tasks.

RCA operationalizes causal anchoring at inference time but
introduces specific trade-offs:
Capability Dependence. RCA is a capability multiplier,
not a generator. It cannot fix upstream errors or perceptual failures—the Entropy regime (weak agents at 56–61%)
confirms this ceiling.

2. The Final Output Gap: Frontier models derive correct answers and explicitly identify hints as wrong, yet
output the hint value. This control failure persists despite explicit instructions, necessitating external process
verification.

Safety Premium. The 12% false rejection rate is acceptable for safety-critical applications but costly for highthroughput deployment. Calibrating judge sensitivity or
training dedicated trace-verification judges could reduce
this cost.

3. Self-correction is insufficient: Reflexion, Self-Refine,
and Self-Consistency reduce sycophancy to 7–9% but
cannot eliminate it. A model critiquing itself inherits
its own blind spots toward authority.

Domain Scope. RCA targets constraint-bound domains
(mathematics, logic, factual QA) where trace-output consistency is verifiable. Extension to creative or open-ended
tasks requires defining “controlled divergence.”

RCA: Process Evaluation Without Ground Truth. We
introduced Regulated Causal Anchoring (RCA), a feedbackdriven controller that evaluates process (trace-output consistency), not outcome (answer correctness). RCA achieves
0% sycophancy across all model tiers while improving accuracy (GPT-3.5: 20.5% → 74.0%; GPT-5.1: 84.2% →
90.5%). Crucially, RCA requires no ground truth and is not
a blind filter: it maintains 88% acceptance of valid hints
through Trace-Based Verification, distinguishing helpful
context from adversarial pressure.
