# Change talk evocation

> In counselling research the class of the next answer is measurably a
> function of the form of the previous question. Opposition is an output of
> the interview, not a property of the person being asked.

## Definition / Mechanism

Utterances on both sides are coded into classes. The asker's turns split into[^s1][^s2][^s3][^s4][^s5][^s6]
**MI-consistent** (open question, simple and complex reflection, affirmation,
asking permission) and **MI-inconsistent** (confront, warn, direct, advise
without permission). The answerer's turns split into **change talk**
(statements toward change) and **sustain talk** (statements for the status
quo). Miller & Rose (2009) name the resulting causal chain the *technical
hypothesis*: asker's utterance class → answerer's language class → outcome.

The first link has experimental support, not only correlational.

- Patterson & Forgatch (1985) coded videotaped family-therapy sessions for
  sequential dependency: therapist *teach* and *confront* raised the
  probability of the client's next turn being non-compliant; *facilitate* and
  *support* lowered it. They then manipulated the therapist's behaviour in
  single-subject ABAB reversals and the client's non-compliance rose and fell
  with the phase.
- Glynn & Moyers (2010) ran an ABAB analogue with 9 clinicians and 47
  undergraduate drinkers, alternating a change-talk-evocation phase with a
  functional-analysis phase. Percent change talk was higher in the evocation
  phase, $t(46) = 6.56$, $p < .001$, $d = 1.19$.
- Miller, Benefield & Tonigan (1993) randomised 42 problem drinkers to a
  directive-confrontational or a client-centred delivery of the *same*
  feedback. The confrontational arm produced more client resistance, and
  in-session resistance predicted drinking one year later.

The second link is weaker. Amrhein et al. (2003) found that the *strength* of
commitment language late in the session, not its frequency, tracked
abstinence. Magill et al.'s meta-analysis (2018; k = 58 reports, 36 studies,
40 effect sizes, N = 3,025) confirmed the counsellor→client-language paths
but found change talk non-significant as an independent predictor of outcome,
while sustain talk predicted worse outcome ($r \approx .19$).

## Known as

Miller & Rollnick's earlier editions called the answerer's opposition
**resistance**, a term that located it in the person. After the evidence that
it moves with the interviewer's phase, the construct was split into *sustain
talk* (about the topic) and *discord* (about the relationship). The rename is
the finding.

## Why it matters

1. **The answerer's disagreement is partly the asker's product.** A
   procedure that reads "the user pushed back" as evidence about the world is
   reading its own phrasing. Push-back is only a constraint if the preceding
   turn was held constant, and no field except this one measures that.
2. **Reflection is a question form.** The highest-yield MI-consistent move is
   not an interrogative at all: a complex reflection states the asker's
   current model back and lets the answerer correct it. This is the same move
   [[tacit-knowledge]] recommends as offering a wrong draft, arrived at from
   a different direction and with sequential-dependency evidence behind it.
3. **Utterance class, not topic, is the controllable.** The manipulations
   above hold the content fixed (same feedback, same drinking topic) and vary
   only the form. That isolates formulation from selection: choosing the axis
   does not determine what comes back.

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home field** | behaviour-change counselling; an ambivalent person, the target of inquiry is their own conduct, the desired direction is fixed in advance |
| **Here** | an agent asking a user or expert to constrain an underspecified task |
| **What survives** | the sequential-dependency claim and its ABAB evidence — the class of the reply is a controllable function of the class of the previous turn; resistance treated as an interview artefact to be measured, not a datum; reflection-for-correction as a question form |
| **What does not** | the outcome half of the chain. MI is **directional**: it deliberately reinforces one class of answers because the target variable is the respondent's own motivation. An agent seeking a fact about a system has no analogous preferred class, and importing evocation there is a [[leading-question-rule|leading-question]] bias — exactly what [[maximum-entropy-principle]] forbids and what [[survey-response-model]] would score as a response effect |

## Limits

- Only Patterson & Forgatch (1985) and Glynn & Moyers (2010) are causal, and
  both are small-N analogues — family-therapy single subjects, and graduate
  students interviewing undergraduates about drinking. The field trial
  (Miller et al. 1993, n = 42) randomised the style but the
  resistance→outcome link inside it is correlational.
- The coding systems classify whole utterances into a handful of behaviour
  types. The evidence therefore supports "reflect rather than confront" and
  supports nothing about *which words*: it is a coarser instrument than
  [[survey-response-model]]'s split ballot.
- Magill et al. (2018) is the reason not to state the chain as a theorem. The
  half of it that would justify evocation on outcome grounds did not hold for
  change talk; only the sustain-talk path did.
- Every subject is a human with a stake in the answer. Nothing here has been
  measured on an LLM answerer, where the analogue of sycophancy under
  pressure is a different literature.
