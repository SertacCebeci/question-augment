# INTENT

The only output is the **wiki** — and the wiki grows **on its own**.

A multi-disciplinary scientific knowledge base that takes in **everything**
on the road to **asking good questions** and **giving good answers**,
task-agnostically. The two are one transformation seen from its two sides:
the asker opens slots for constraints, the answerer supplies them — from
many possible worlds to one. The wiki lives in `wiki/`.

The **ability** to ask and answer well and the **effort of gathering** what
that ability rests on are separate steps. This repository does the second
one. No skill, no procedure, no method is a deliverable here — the wiki
alone is a sufficient output.

## Scope

Not a list of topics. One question decides what enters:

> *Does this knowledge change how a question is generated, selected,
> formulated, answered or evaluated?*

From any field that has something to say about it. Where the
transformation can stall is a useful map, not a fence: on the asker's
side the axes, selection, formulation, source, answer, consistency,
stopping and distillation; on the answerer's side comprehension and
commitment.

## Two verbs

**Grow.** The wiki finds works itself, decides itself which to read,
reads each one whole, and writes what it establishes — grounded in the
work's own text. No owner in the loop; the owner reads the history.

**Chat.** The wiki explains concepts and topics from what it holds,
citing its notes. A question it cannot answer is not a failure of the
chat — it is a **gap found by a source that does not share the wiki's
blind spots** — and it becomes something the wiki searches for.

## Constraints

**Material, not conclusion.** The wiki does not spell out a ready-made
method; it carries the concepts a method could be derived from. A specific
practice of asking does not go inside — the concepts leading to it do. The
measure: someone reading the wiki should be able to build a strong
structure out of it without being told to.

**A gap that is pointed at is not filled directly.** If the owner names a
missing piece, writing that piece on demand breaks the test — the wiki's
own sufficiency can only be measured by what comes out unprompted. A named
gap is handled as a **revision of the scope**, not as a note to write.
Gaps found by *chat* are different: they were not named, they were
stumbled on. Those become searches.

**No fixed end.** What is being looked for has no definite stop, and the
value of the unknown cannot be measured in advance. The threshold is the
owner's satisfaction — declared **in advance and in writing**, so that it
can be measured against rather than felt.

## Growth

The wiki does not close: it only grows, in two ways — **more notes and
better notes**. A reading that establishes a concept the wiki lacks
creates a note; one that bears on a concept the wiki holds adds to that
note — a claim, a limit, a relation, a correction. Nothing the wiki has
accepted is taken away by its own growth.

Recent work on *agents that ask* — LLMs, compound and multi-agent systems
that ask better questions — is read month by month from arXiv, 24 months
back from 2026-04.
