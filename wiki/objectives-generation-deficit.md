# Objectives generation deficit

> Asked to list what matters to them in a decision they are actually making,
> people write down about half of the objectives they endorse minutes later
> from a checklist — and the half they missed is rated nearly as important as
> the half they wrote.

## Finding (Bond, Carlson & Keeney, *Management Science* 2008)

One procedure, three studies. The participant lists every objective relevant[^s1][^s2]
to a real decision of personal consequence; is then shown a **master list** of
~30 candidate objectives and checks each relevant one; maps their own items
onto the list. Checked items that map back are *self-generated*; checked items
that do not are *recognized* — relevant, and omitted at the generation stage.
Importance is rated last.

| Study | n | Decision | Self-generated | Recognized (omitted) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 62 | MBA program, recent | 6.0 | 7.6 |
| 2 | 70 | MBA program; 1-week delay before rating | 6.6 | 7.7 |
| 3 | 28 | summer internship, *impending*; a week to ruminate, with a reminder | 6.8 | 7.9 |

The omitted ones are not filler. In Study 3 (9-point importance scale)
recognized objectives averaged 6.8 against 7.4 for self-generated, while a
planted bogus objective drew 2.9 and a weak baseline objective 4.7. In Study 1,
71% of participants overlooked at least one of their own top five and 10%
failed to generate their single most important objective; in Study 2, after a
one-week delay, 35% failed to generate their most important one.

**Time, motivation and expertise do not close it.** In the paper's field case,
Keeney elicited objectives from 12 Seagate Software executives in hour-long
value-focused discussions with follow-up and revision. Pooling the twelve
lists gave 39 specific objectives; each individual had named 14.17 on average
— **36% of that pool** — and only one of the twelve named more than half.
Bond, Carlson & Keeney (2010) later found that 92% of a separate pretest
sample (N = 94) acknowledged their own list was incomplete, attributing it to
thinking too narrowly (49%) or too shallowly (36%).

## Why it matters

Stall 8's executable procedures ([[information-bottleneck]],
[[agglomerative-information-bottleneck]]) consume a **relevance variable**:
the thing the distillate must predict. Stall 1 asks where the dimensions of
$W$ come from. Neither stall has only one way in — stall 1's map lists
generators that do not work by asking anyone what matters
([[metamorphic-probes]], [[premortem]], [[typical-set]],
[[goal-refinement-graph]], [[problem-solution-coevolution]]). What this
finding prices is the narrower method that the bottleneck family's relevance
variable and the informant-facing stall-1 answers ([[grand-tour-question]],
[[context-free-questions]]) do rest on: **unaided elicitation from one
person**. Its yield is roughly half, with the missing half as important as
the present half, on decisions the respondent is currently making.

Three consequences for a derived procedure:

1. **An elicited objective list is short by construction, not by noise.**
   Giving the same respondent more time does not fix it: Study 3's week of
   rumination with a reminder left the gap at 6.8 generated against 7.9
   recognized.
2. **Recognition measures the gap; it is not what repaired it.** The deficit
   is exposed by a recognition instrument — the same person checks off what
   they could not produce. But the repair that was actually measured
   (bond-2010) is a *request* that asserts the list is incomplete and names a
   target number ([[quantified-challenge]]); handing the respondent sample
   candidate answers made coverage **worse** (10.8 recognized-missed against
   7.9 in the standard condition). Reading "recognition exposes it" as
   "recognition repairs it" is the inference this note refuses.
3. **Spreading the elicitation across people reaches what one long session
   does not** — this wiki's inference, not the authors' claim. No executive at
   Seagate, however senior and however long the discussion, named more than
   half of what the twelve between them named; the 39-item pool is the *union*
   of their lists, so it certifies only that each list omitted items others
   volunteered, never that the union is complete. What transfers is the
   direction: informant count is a lever distinct from session length.

| Qualitative research | thematic/theoretical saturation | **not this** — [[qualitative-saturation]] stops when *new* items cease arriving from new informants; here the stream stops early from one informant who then recognises what was missing, so a saturation signal would have declared this list complete |

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home field** | an individual listing objectives for their own consequential decision, on paper, with a domain master list available to score against |
| **Here** | an asker eliciting from one respondent the axes of $W$, or the relevance variable a distillate must serve |
| **What survives** | the direction and rough size of the shortfall; that the omitted items are as important as the listed ones; that added deliberation time does not remove it; that a recognition instrument exposes it |
| **What does not** | the master list. The instrument was built by domain experts before the session; an asker who had one would already hold the axes it is trying to elicit. And the ~50% is measured against that list, so it is a completeness figure relative to a known candidate set, not against the truth |

## Limits

- **The measuring instrument is the master list, and there is no ground
  truth.** "Half" means half of a 28–34 item list the experimenters
  brainstormed. A different list moves the number; nothing here bounds how
  far.
- **The Seagate 36% is arithmetic on a union.** The 39-objective set is
  assembled from the twelve individual lists, so a per-person share below
  100% follows from any disagreement between informants and is not evidence
  that the pooled set is complete. Consequence 3 above rests on the *spread*
  between individuals, not on the pool's completeness, and is this wiki's
  reading rather than a result the authors state.
- **The figures quoted here were not re-derived in verification.** Both Bond
  et al. papers are confirmed to exist, to match their citations and to
  support the ~50% omission result and the intervention comparison in
  structure and magnitude; but the publisher pages returned 403 and the PDFs
  could not be parsed, so every individual digit above (6.0/6.6/6.8 against
  7.6/7.7/7.9; 6.8 vs 7.4; 71%/10%/35%; 14.17 of 39; 92%/49%/36%) comes from
  this note's reading of secondary renderings, not from the typeset text.
- **The authors call their own estimate conservative, on inspection rather
  than by test.** They report that participants mapped their items onto
  master-list entries liberally, which inflates the self-generated count.
  That inflation is argued from packet inspection, not measured.
- **Studies 1 and 2 are retrospective** — the decision had been made. Study 3
  is prospective but has n = 28.
- **Population.** MBA and doctoral students at US universities; one field case
  in one firm, with no control condition.
- **No source here elicits objectives from a machine asker, or from a
  respondent answering an agent.** Applying the number to an agent's
  elicitation is this wiki's extrapolation, not a measured result.
