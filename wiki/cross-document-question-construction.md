# Cross-document question construction

A question that can only be answered by combining two articles is hard to get by asking for it directly. The XRAG pipeline builds such questions in stages, and each stage is there to block a specific failure. The first stage writes a summary of each article that is accurate, covers the key points, and has little lexical overlap with the source. The reason for the overlap constraint is that questions written from the article's own wording are easy to answer by string matching, and "direct question generation from articles can focus on trivial details".[^c1] Questions drawn from summaries instead "tends to produce questions about the main points of the articles."[^c2]

The second stage does not ask for the two-source question at once. The authors write that "generating such questions in one step is" difficult, because "LLMs often create questions that simply" link two separate questions with "and".[^c3][^c4] So the pipeline first produces simple question-answer pairs from each summary, each answerable with one step of reasoning. The third stage works on these pairs: it looks for "connections between the two sets of Q&A pairs", keeps related pairs with at least one from each source, and writes a new question that needs both.[^c5]

The mechanism is that a two-source question is composed from shallow facts that are each individually easy, and the composition is what makes the question cross-document rather than a conjunction. Because the selected pairs "originate from different source articles", answering the composed question "necessitates integrating information from both sources".[^c6] The paper keeps four types: "aggregation, comparison, multi-hop, and set questions."[^c7]

Construction is then checked on two fronts. Annotators keep examples where "the question is natural and answerable and the answer is" correct or correctable.[^c8] And the difficulty is checked separately: all LLMs score low without retrieval, "with accuracy rates falling below 16%."[^c9]

## Limits

- The generator is GPT-4o and the evaluation is partly the same family of models. The authors note that "QA pairs are generated by GPT-4o, and evaluation" "may be biased in its favor."[^c10]
- The claim that one-step generation fails is given as a finding ("we find"), with no count or ablation in the parts of the text read. Whether linking shallow facts is the source of the difficulty, rather than the reading load of two articles, is not separated here (my inference).
- Quality control removes items, so what remains is a survivor set. In a manual review of answers judged incorrect, the authors report that "8% involve noisy or ambiguous questions".[^c11]
- Only four composition types are covered. Other two-source relations, such as contradiction or change over time, are not tested by this pipeline.
- The construction says nothing about whether a model that answers these questions is reasoning or matching; that needs the separate evidence the paper gives for the human gap.
