# Cross-generator, cross-corpus split

When a synthetic question set is used both to fine-tune a model and to evaluate it, the same LLM and the same documents produce the supervision and the reference answers. The paper names the pitfall: this creates "a train/test correlation that confounds genuine domain adaptation with pipeline-specific style mimicry."[^c1]

## The mechanism

Two independent separations are applied at once.

1. Cross-generator: the training split is generated by Claude Sonnet and the test split by GPT-4o, both under the same style templates and quality controls.[^c2]
2. Cross-corpus: the two splits come from disjoint seed documents (40 defense documents split into two 20-document corpora), with no document-level overlap, checked by passage-level n-gram and embedding-similarity tests.[^c2][^c3]
3. Judge separation: construction-time judges come from a third family (Gemma), distinct from both the generators and the baselines, to limit family-level coupling.[^c2]

The intended consequence is that a fine-tuned model "cannot win on the test split by imitating GPT-4o's writing style or by memorizing test-corpus content."[^c2] In the case study a LoRA-adapted Llama3.1-8B trained on the training split still improved on the test split and under alternative retrievers, which the authors read as not explained by style matching or passage memorization.[^c4]

## A side effect on metrics

The separation makes train and test reference answers differ systematically in length (Claude answers are longer). Token-overlap metrics must therefore be read with that difference in mind.[^c2]

## The same disjointness at the level of source

Outside synthetic QA, the same idea appears with a producer in place of a generator. A ChEMBL study argues that the identity of who made a compound can carry the label, and it recommends that evaluation consider "source-aware splits (e.g., author-disjoint, lab-disjoint, or site-disjoint)".[^c6] That paper proposes these splits and does not run them, so it shows the leak under scaffold splits only; see [[provenance-cue-leakage]].

## Limits

- One domain (defense), one generator pair, one fine-tuned model; transfer to other corpora is "argued rather than demonstrated."[^c5]
- The splits still share a construction pipeline (same prompts, same NLI prefilter, same quality controls), so within-pipeline metrics may reflect pipeline-level style correlation; the authors mitigate with retrieval-conditioned analysis and an independently built expert-curated check set but "cannot fully rule out residual correlation."[^c5]
- My inference: the design removes two named leakage routes, not all; shared prompt templates mean the question-form distribution is the same on both sides, so gains could still partly be format familiarity.
