Abstract
Answer verification methods are widely employed in language model training
pipelines spanning data curation, evaluation, and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). While prior work focus on developing unified verifiers
applicable across multiple reasoning scenarios, significant challenges remain in
computation-oriented scientific domains, such as algebraic equivalence checking
and physical constant substitution. In this paper, we introduce CoSineVerifier, a
tool-augmented verifier that leverages external executors to perform precise computations and symbolic simplifications. CoSineVerifier enables robust verification that
goes beyond simple semantic matching. We propose a novel two-stage pipeline,
which begin with cold-start fine-tuning and followed by multi-turn reinforcement
learning with tool integration. Extensive experiments conducted on STEM subjects,
general QA, and long-form reasoning tasks demonstrates strong generalization of
CoSineVerifier. The results shows that the CoSineVerifier achieves state-of-theart performance on VerifyBench-Hard and SCI-Bench. And we also employ our
CoSineVerifier in RLVR as a reward model, the results show that it consistently outperforms both rubric-based and model-based verifiers on AIME’24 and AIME’25,
demonstrating strong potential to enhance reasoning capabilities of LLM. Our
model is released at https://huggingface.co/Nanbeige/CoSineVerifier-Tool-4B.

Introduction

The evolution of large language models (LLMs) training—from relying solely on human-annotated
data to enabling models to self-evolve—has imposed increasingly stringent demands on how we
evaluate the quality of LLM outputs. In this context, answer verification, which compares a model
output against a reference, has become a critical component throughout data curation, evaluation, and
model training pipelines. In the phase of data curation, answer verification methods are extensively
employed to guarantee the consistency, fidelity, and accuracy of synthetic corpora by many works [1,
2, 3, 4]. At the evaluation stage, these methods provide a robust alternative to assess correctness
beyond exact match (EM), enabling semantic-level comparison and format-robust scoring [5, 6, 7, 8].
For model training, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) relies on verifiers
to produce reward signals, imposing stringent requirements on verifier accuracy, calibration, and
latency/throughput [9, 10, 11].

Existing verification methods primarily rely on direct semantic comparisons between model outputs
and reference answers. Rubric-based verifiers [12], which depend on structured outputs (e.g.,
Math-Verify evaluating equivalence in Markdown or step-by-step formats), can fail even on simple
computation-heavy cases such as comparing 2*2 and 4. Alternatively, many studies [13, 14, 15]
employ LLM-based verifiers that assess answers through chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning or implicit
reasoning encoded in model parameters. However, these verifiers are still prone to hallucination and
inconsistencies in numerical or symbolic reasoning. For instance, when comparing matrices with
hundreds of entries or substituting physical constants with specific values, LLM-based verifiers often
produce incorrect judgments. The lack of robust computation skills prevents reasoning models from
receiving accurate rewards in RLVR tasks within complex scientific domains, thereby limiting their
overall potential.
We present CoSineVerifier, a tool-augmented LLM-based verifier designed for checking answers to
computation-heavy scientific questions. As shown in Figure 1, reference answers and model outputs
often express the same numerical value using different formulas. To enable robust verification, a
reliable verifier must be capable of performing arithmetic transformations before making a judgment.
In this paper, we propose to equip the verifier with external tools such as a Python interpreter.
Therefore, our proposed CoSineVerifier can perform precise computations on both formulas to derive
aligned numerical results, thereby ensuring accurate verification.
However, foundation models are not well-tuned on precise tool-augmented verification, which may
issue inaccurate and redundant tool calls during the process. To address this challenge, we propose
a novel verifier training methodology. We begin by collecting a diverse set of questions spanning
multiple domains, including mathematics, natural sciences, and logical reasoning. We first conduct a
comprehensive analysis of how common verification methods fail on these questions, identifying
typical failure modes in such settings. As illustrated in Figure 2, we identify two major challenges:
(i) Calculation inaccuracy represents the largest proportion of verification failures, highlighting
the limitations of existing verifiers in computation-intensive contexts. (ii) Error types are diverse
and follow a long-tail distribution, making it difficult for previous data augmentation approaches
to achieve comprehensive coverage. Based on these insights, we design an automatic method for
constructing verifier training data that synthesizes challenging computation-heavy instances. This
approach increases both the density of queries prone to verification errors and the proportion of cases
that require computational tool-augmented reasoning.
After constructing this diverse and challenging training dataset, we propose a two-stage training
strategy to equip the verifier with effective tool-use capabilities in computation-heavy scenarios: (i)
cold-start supervised fine-tuning, followed by (ii) reinforcement learning with tool-call encourage2

• We propose CoSineVerifier, a tool-augmented answer verification model. By invoking
external tools (e.g., Python interpreter), it performs precise computation and algebraic

• We develop a two-stage training framework consisting of cold-start fine-tuning followed by

the correctness of tool-invoked verifications on computation-heavy questions.
• Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of CoSineVerifier in terms of both
verification performance and downstream RLVR applications. CoSineVerifier achieves
state-of-the-art results on VerifyBench and Sci-Bench. As a fast and accurate reward model
in RLVR, it also consistently outperforms rubric-based and CoT-only verifiers on both
AIME’24 and AIME’25.

Related Works

2.1

Answer Verification

Answer verification is the automated process of deciding whether a candidate response is factually
correct to the provided reference answer. Significant progress has been made in the development of
LLM-output verification[16, 17], which can broadly be divided into two main classes: rule-based
verification and model-based verification.Early rule-based methods like Math-Verify[12] extract the
final answer via heuristics and compare it against the ground truth using predefined rules. However,
these approaches struggle with unstructured model outputs and tend to incur a high false-negative
rate[18]. To address these shortcomings, model-based verifiers exploit the flexibility of LLMs to interpret and evaluate unpredictable output. Xverify[13] and CompasVerifier[14] are designed to provide
efficient and robust answer verification by carefully constructing training examples. While effective,
they are constrained by the verifier’s inherent reasoning and calculation competency—particularly in
computation-intensive math and unit-sensitive STEM problems.
In this paper, we concentrate on the model-verifiaction reward setting , notably in calculate-intensive
and science domains where existing verifiers usually fail. To overcome these limitations, we propose
CosineVerifier, which endows the model with external tool capabilities (e.g., a Python interpreter and
a unit-conversion toolkit) to reliably validate diverse LLM outputs while delivering efficient, robust
and trustworthy verdicts.
3

2.2

Tool Integrated Reasoning

A growing line of work investigates improving LLM reasoning by augmenting models with external
tools, yielding consistent gains in accuracy, calibration, and sample efficiency across math, program,
and multi-turn QA tasks[19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24]. ReTool[25], ToRL[26] and SimpleTIR[27] train
models to interleave real-time code execution with language reasoning and to learn when/how to
call tools via reward signals, yielding strong gains on math-reasoning benchmarks. VTool-R1[28]
integrates Python-based visual editing tools (box, mask, highlight) into the RFT loop so the model
learns to interleave intermediate visual steps with textual chain-of-thought. Extending beyond
code, Search-R1[29] casts web search as an action space and trains query generation/revision via
outcome rewards for multi-turn retrieval-augmented reasoning. Taken together, these approaches
recast reasoning over a unified tool-action space, spanning code, vision, and search—where outcomelevel rewards teach models when to plan, execute, and reflect, yielding stronger generalization and
reliability beyond base models.
To the best of our knowledge, CosineVerifier is the first outcome answer verifier that augments LLMbased judgments with executable tools, enabling complex unit-consistent calculations, toleranceaware numeric checks and robust string format equivalence for general verification process.

4.1

Tool-augmented Verification

To facilitate the reliable outcome verification, we equip our verification model with external tools that
can dynamically execute computations and norliaze units, enabling precise judgments on calculationintensive and science tasks. Specifically, as shown in Figure 3 (a), when facing a verification problem,
our CoSineVerifier-4B-Tool first decides whether to use an external tool. If external tool call is
needed, we parse the tool arguments(e.g. python code) and send them to sandbox environments. After
the sandbox environment completes the execution and returns the output, CoSineVerifier-4B-Tool
gives its final judgement based on the sandbox output. Full case study can be found in Appendix D.
4.2

Data Curation

Data Collection Answer verification in real-world applications is challenging for two reasons: (i)
the diversity of questions encountered in practice, and (ii) the variability of model outputs (format,
length, and reasoning style). To address these challenges, we construct a large-scale candidate
training data, comprising 1.14 million samples. These samples were collected from 15 datasets
using 14 large language models (LLMs), spanning mathematics, science, logical reasoning, and
commonsense knowledge domains. This provides broad coverage for training robust verification
models. In addition, to strengthen CosineVerifier on practical verification tasks, we deliberately
go beyond static benchmarks (in contrast to prior collections such as CompassVerifier[14] and
Xverify[13]). A substantial portion of our data is sampled from competition-level math and science
questions, where verification often fails in practice. During the construction of of our data, we also
ensure cross-domain coverage, and to further increase robustness, we also prepend different prompt
prefixes(e.g. Let’s think step by step) on a subset of training data, promoting generalization to diverse
prompting styles.
Data Annotation After constructing a large and diverse training data, we introduce an iterative
annotation pipeline that produces reliable verification labels. We first employ a set of models
(Compass-Verifier-32B, qwen3-4b-2507, and Qwen3-32B), with each model providing three independent annotations for a total of nine per sample. We then retain 63,714 samples that show
any disagreement among these nine annotations. For samples that showcase high disagreement
rate(defined as more than three out of nine annotations differing), we further send them to a more
powerful model(GPT-o3) with majority voting, ensuring high label fidelity on harder questions.
4.3

Data augmentation

Long-tail data augmentation To understand when the verifier performs poorly, we conducted a
discrepancy analysis over 63,374 disagreement cases. We first prompted Qwen3-32B to produce
an error category for each sample and then leverage GPT-o3 to summarize these categories into
15 error types. As shown in Figure 2, all error types reveal a pronounced skew: the top five
categories—dominated by Calculation Inaccuracy and Exact Match Failure, account for over 85%
of errors, while the remaining ten categories are sparsely represented. This distribution makes
low-frequency errors harder to learn. To counter this imbalance, we use gpt-o3 to synthesize targeted
long-tail verification examples under constrained generation settings, yielding 10k long-tail synthesize
instances. We further incorporate these samples into training for both the labeling verifier and the
tool-augmented verifier, improving coverage of rare error modes. All training data statistic can be
found in Appendix C.
Difficulty-oriented data augmentation After cold-start, our model acquired foundational tool-use
and verification skills. To further amplify its performance, we focused on curating a high-difficulty
post-training dataset using an iterative self-instruct framework. In each round, GPT-o3 synthesized
new questions conditioned on the current hard cases. Our cold-start model then attempted these
questions. We only retrained incorrect instances, and merged these instances with the reference set
for the next iteration. After four such iterations, the procedure produced a reinforcement-learning
corpus of 9,456 examples. A detailed algorithm is depicted in 1.

4.4

Post Training

4.4.1

Cold-Start

To ensure verification accuracy and robustness, we equip CosineVerifier with two executable tools: a
Python interpreter and a unit-conversion utility. During the preliminary experiment of CoSineVerifier,
we find that directly apply reinforcement learning on base model yields performance degradation,
due to the base model’s limited tool-calling capabilities. We therefore implement a cold-start stage
to equip the model with foundational tool-use proficiency. Specifically, we first collect real-world
tool-use traces from our training data and retain only those cases that (i) produce a correct final
answer and (ii) contain explicit tool-call trajectories. To boost the efficiency of CoSineVerifier, we
discard model internal <think>. . . </think> content, and further filter out responses longer than 200
tokens to keep the cold-start corpus compact and efficient. This procedure yields 29,339 tool-usage
examples used to bootstrap training.
During training CoSineVerifier-4B-Tool, we mask the loss on tool-execution outputs (e.g., interpreter
traces and unit-conversion results), which prevents the model from merely imitating tool dumps and
improves optimization stability. We also train CoSineVerifier-Label series models, which are train
on the full 63,374 disagreement data together with 10k model-generated long-tail instances, and
providing verdict as binary classification with targets Correct and Incorrect.

5.2

Main Results

CoSineVerifier series achieve strong verification accuracy compared to baselines As shown
in Table 1, our CoSineVerifier series outperforms all baselines across three verify benchmarks.
Specifically, for CoT Verifier, our CoSineVerifier-Tool-4B surpasses baselines by 0.5%-14.7% with
noticeably less tokens, especially on harder benchmarks like VerifyBench(hard) and Sci-Bench,
where complex calculation and long-context string comparing is needed to verify the LLM-generated
answer. In addition, for labeing verifier, CoSineVerifier-4B and CoSineVerifier-32B also surpass
other labeling verifiers by 0.7%-1.1%, demonstrating stronger verification skills under single-token
scenarios.
Small models with external tools excel large models Figure 4 demonstrates the performance
comparison of our CoSineVerifier against various model across different domains. Among three
benchmarks, even close-source SOTA models like o3 still struggle with reliable verification on
compute-intensive and scientific reasoning tasks. In contrast, both CoSineVerifier-4B-Tool and
CoSineVerifier-32B demonstrate exceptional proficiency in the Math and Science domains while
achieve competitive or even superior performance on the remaining domains. Despite being much
more smaller on parameter size, the superior accuracy exhibited by CoSineVerifier-4B-Tool validates
the efficacy of integrating external calculation tools to enhance the model’s verification capabilities. These results demonstrate the feasibility of tool-augmented, small-scale models to exceed the
performance of large SOTA models in complex verification tasks.
CoSineVerifier-4B-Tool shows superior efficiency while maintaining high accuracy We further
evaluate the efficiency of CoSineVerifier-4B-Tool against several baselines in Figure 4. To ensure
a fair comparison, all models were benchmarked on 4xNVIDIA A800 GPUs. We report the mean
inference latency computed over 1000 randomly sampled prompts. While CoSineVerifier-4B-Tool
achieves high accuracy, incorporating external tools does not incur significant inference latency. We
attribute this efficiency to two primary factors: (1) our efficiecy-driven cold-start data construction
methodology, which explicitly optimizes for short and precise tool-calling trajectories, resulting in
2.4x-19x times less token usage compared to other models. (2) the computational efficiency afforded
by the model’s compact 4B parameter count, which minimizes the overhead of the backbone model
during inference.

Application in RLVR

The primary objective of our CoSineVerifier series is to establish a reliable and efficient outcome
reward verifier tailored for real-world data processing and reinforcement learning. To avoid potential
biases inherent in existing benchmarks and validate the efficacy of CoSineVerifier as a reward model
with RL training, we further evaluate answer-verification methods in RLVR settings. Specifically,
utilizing an on-policy GRPO algorithm, we train Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on competition-math
problems with 42K training data drawn from DAPO-Math-17k[30], OpenR1-Math-220k[42], and
DeepScaleR-Preview[43]. We compare our approach against various verifiers, including both rulebased and model-based methods, under identical training configurations. We report mean@32

details are provided in Appendix C.

contrast, weaker verifier like Math-Verify perform worse than CoSineVerifier series, demonstrating
a direct correlation between a verifier’s capability and base model’s reasoning performance during
reinforcement learning. As recent research increasingly utilizes challenging datasets that elicit highly
complex model responses and reference answers[44, 45], this trend naturally motivates us to develop
a more reliable verification system that ensures robust and accurate reward signaling to boost the
performance of base models.

7

Conclusion

In this work, we introduce the CoSineVerifier series, a family of answer verifiers designed for
computation-oriented scientific questions. Our core contribution, CoSineVerifier-Tool-4B, demon10

strates that a compact 4B model, when augmented with external tools like a Python interpreter, can
significantly outperform much larger models on challenging verification benchmarks. This toolintegrated approach enables the precise evaluation of algebraic equivalences and physical constant
alignments, addressing a key weakness of existing LLM-based verifiers. Furthermore, when applied
as a reward model in RLVR tasks, CoSineVerifier series yields clear performance improvements on
AIME24 and AIME25, highlighting the importance of accurate, tool-enhanced reward signals for
training more capable models.
