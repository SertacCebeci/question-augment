# Round-wise regret conformal acceptance

> [[conformal-prediction|Conformal prediction]] gives a finite-sample coverage guarantee for one
> i.i.d. test point; this result gives the same kind of guarantee for the
> regret of a controller's action proposal at round $t$ of an ongoing
> multi-agent discussion, conditional only on the discussion having
> reached round $t$.

## Definition / Mechanism

For a fixed controller $m$ proposing action $a$ at state $s_t$, define the
non-conformity score as the one-step regret: the gap between the best
achievable drop in the surrogate discussion loss $J$ and the drop $a$
actually achieves, computed by counterfactually evaluating every
admissible action from the same frozen state. [[calibration|Calibration]] trajectories are
run offline with the controller, router, model and prompts held fixed;
only trajectories that reach round $t$ contribute their round-$t$ score to
that round's calibration set, and the threshold $\epsilon_t$ is its
$\lceil(n_t+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest value.

"Proposition 1 (Round-Wise Marginal Regret Coverage). Fix a controller $m$
and round $t$. ... $\mathbb{P}(Q_t^m(s_{test}) \le \epsilon_t^m \mid$ test
reaches $t$, $N_t=n_t) \ge 1-\alpha$."[^c1]

At test time: "non-conformity score (or regret) for the controller's
proposed action is computed, and if it is within the conformal threshold
$\epsilon_t^m$, then it is passed to the router for stage 4. Otherwise, the
near-optimal conformal action set $\Gamma_t^m(s)$ is generated by executing
all possible actions" and the fallback selects a minimum-regret member of
that set.[^c2]

The paper states the guarantee holds "conditional on the realized
calibration-cohort size and on the test trajectory reaching round $t$",
requiring the pooled calibration and test scores to be exchangeable and
the controller and scoring procedure to be fixed independently of the
calibration sample.[^c3]

## Why it matters

It gives the action a group takes at each turn a certified regret bound
instead of an ad hoc heuristic, and it does so per round rather than only
in aggregate over a whole session — the failure mode a fixed schedule or
round-robin protocol cannot even express. It is the mechanism that lets
[[consilience-communication-control]] replace an untrustworthy proposal
instead of merely reporting that it might be wrong.

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| **In its home field** | Split conformal prediction gives distribution-free, finite-sample marginal coverage for one i.i.d. test point given an exchangeable calibration set (Vovk, Gammerman & Shafer, 2005) |
| **Here** | Applied not to a label but to the one-step regret of a multi-agent controller's proposed action, calibrated separately per discussion round on trajectories that reached that round |
| **What survives** | The distribution-free, finite-sample coverage guarantee, and the exact-probability refinement $k_t/(n_t+1)$ when scores are almost surely distinct |
| **What does not** | Marginal validity across rounds jointly — each round's guarantee is separate, not simultaneous over a whole trajectory — and exchangeability requires the controller, router, model and prompts to stay fixed between calibration and test; a controller update, model swap, or prompt change at deployment breaks it |

## Limits

- The guarantee is conditional on reaching round $t$ and on the calibration
  cohort size $n_t$ at that round, not an unconditional guarantee over an
  entire session.
- Requires exchangeability of calibration and test non-conformity scores
  under a fixed scoring procedure; the note does not claim the guarantee
  survives any change to controller, router, model, or prompts between
  calibration and deployment.
- Distinct from [[sequence-level-conformal-prediction]]'s guarantee: that
  result covers a whole multi-step scenario's prediction set from a
  min-over-timesteps score; this one covers one round's action-regret score
  conditional on having reached that round — the literature measures these
  as different objects.
