# Context cost-accuracy decoupling

Padding a question with unrelated text was measured on two axes that moved apart. Each question was "preceded by a large, irrelevant context block"[^c1] of 4,096, 10,000 or 15,000 words, over a set of "200 diverse general knowledge questions"[^c2], served by vLLM on eight A100 GPUs. The cost axis rose steeply. The authors report that "the time required to process the input is dominated by" the prefill stage[^c3]. At 15,000 words the 70B model's latency rose by 719.64%, and the explanation given is that "even a silent or irrelevant token consumes the same memory bandwidth as a critical one".[^c4] The mechanism offered is that the attention step "must perform pairwise comparisons over the entire sequence", so the bill is paid per token whether or not the token bears on the question.[^c5]

The accuracy axis moved little under the same padding. For Llama-3.1-70B, "accuracy declined only slightly from the 98.5% baseline to 98% at 15,000 words".[^c6] Qwen1.5-14B "shift[ed] only from 99% to 97.5%" over the same range.[^c7] The needle-retrieval test, in which a single planted fact sat at the start, middle or end of the padding, also held: the models found the needle "irrespective of the context length distraction/noise".[^c8]

So the two readings come apart. Input length is expensive in time and memory whether or not it is relevant, while the answer, in these tests, hardly changes. The authors name the practice "context discipline" and call "sending more data than is necessary" a bottleneck "that cannot be addressed by increasing computational power alone".[^c9] For asking and answering, this is my reading and not a tested result: a long padded prompt can look costly on a latency measure and harmless on an accuracy measure, and each measure answers a different question. Neither one shows the other.

## Limits

- The accuracy evidence is small. It is 200 general-knowledge questions, generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro.[^c10] The reported gaps are 0.5 to 1.5 points, which is a few questions. The text reports no confidence interval and no significance test, so "barely moved" is not shown to be a difference of zero.
- The authors say the question is open: "powerful GPUs, and varying context lengths is required to investigate this further".[^c11] Only two dense models and one MoE model were run.
- Latency was measured with caching off, since "prefix caching was disabled, and a tensor-parallel size".[^c12] With prefix caching, repeated padding may cost far less, so the percentages describe this setup and are not a general price of context. This caveat is mine.
- The padding is described only as "irrelevant". The text does not say how similar it was to the questions. Near-miss distractors, which could mislead, were not tested here.
- The memory-wall account is the authors' explanation. The text contains no profiling that isolates it.
- The Mixtral latency "hump" at 4,096 words is not part of this note. The authors call it a hypothesis: "The anomalous slowdown observed in the 4,096-word regime suggests a complex interplay".[^c13]
