# Reference-keyword LCS reward

Open-ended generation has no single answer to check against, so this reward is built from a reference answer written for the same prompt. An LLM first lists the key points the answer must address, then, for each key point, extracts a short list of keywords (each under three words) that "encode the core facts, concepts, or entities required to assess the correctness and relevance of the response."[^c1] The paper reports that this two-level extraction beats a single-step extraction by 0.9 points, and that the extracted keywords come to about 15% of the reference.[^c2][^c3]

Scoring a rollout is a regular-expression job, not a judgment. For each key point, the keywords matched in the rollout and in the reference are kept with their frequency and order, and the two sequences are compared by their longest common subsequence. The per-point score is that LCS length divided by the longer of the two sequences, and the content reward is the mean over key points.[^c4] The authors chose LCS because "it inherently captures keyword ordering and repetition."[^c5] With several references, the highest alignment per key point is kept, with up to three references used.[^c6]

A second, style component runs beside it. An LLM writes short Python checks for measurable properties such as length, markdown structure and formatting, and assigns each check a weight "reflecting its relative importance."[^c7] The final reward averages the content and style parts. Both are computed by rule, which the paper sets against reward models and judges: the reward is "computed via reference-grounded rule-based reward," so no model is loaded during RL.[^c8]

The point of the normalization shows in the ablation. Replacing LCS with a plain percentage of reference keywords present in the rollout is reported to fail badly, because that version "disregards keyword ordering" and invites "verbose outputs to artificially inflate keyword coverage."[^c9][^c10] Dividing by the longer sequence means that extra matched words in a rollout lower the per-point score rather than raise it; the paper does not spell out that consequence, so this reading is mine. Removing the content reward cost 13.0 points of average score, removing the style reward 2.8, and dropping the LLM-derived style weights 1.2, all on Qwen2.5-3B-Base.[^c11][^c12]

## Limits

- The reward measures coverage of one reference's key points, in order. It does not establish that an answer is correct. An answer that is right in other words scores low, and a fluent answer that uses the reference's words in the right order scores high. The paper's claim that the scheme "preserves output diversity" is measured at one decoding temperature (1.0), not across settings.[^c13]
- The key points, keywords and style checks are all LLM outputs, and the only filter is a cross-check against the reference itself: cases "where both content and style rewards of the reference fall below 0.7" are dropped.[^c14] A wrong key point that the reference happens to satisfy passes this check. Nothing here tests the extracted components against an independent source.
- Style is limited to what code can check. The paper says "semantic aspects such as tone are implicitly captured through the content reward."[^c15] That is an assumption the paper does not test separately.
- The training references are one model's output: "All responses are regenerated by GPT-4o-mini to maintain consistency." The reward therefore learns to match one generator's phrasing and structure, which the paper does not separate from quality.[^c16]
- The ablation on multiple references is small: a single reference drops the average only "from 31.1 to 30.7." So the robustness the paper credits to multiple references is a small effect in this setup.[^c17]
- The headline benchmarks are judged by a model (MT-Bench uses "GPT-4.1-mini as the evaluation judge"), even though the training reward is not. The reward is rule-based; its evidence of quality is not.[^c18]
- The padding result is shown for the bag-count variant, not for the LCS variant under adversarial pressure. Whether LCS normalization closes the padding route fully is not tested.
