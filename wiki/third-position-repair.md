# Third-position repair

> The comprehension check that happens *after* the answer: the answer itself
> displays the reading it was given, and the asker's next turn is the last
> position the structure of talk provides for correcting that reading.

## Definition / Mechanism

**The response is the display.** Sacks, Schegloff & Jefferson (1974) observed[^s1][^s2][^s3]
that the turn-taking system has, as a by-product of its design, a *proof
procedure*: a next turn is produced on the basis of what its speaker took the
prior turn to be doing, so it exhibits that understanding publicly. Nothing
extra is uttered to make the understanding visible; answering is what makes it
visible.

**Repair is positionally ordered.** Schegloff, Jefferson & Sacks (1977)
describe repair — the handling of trouble in speaking, hearing and
understanding — as organised by *who initiates* and *where*. Opportunities for
self-initiation precede opportunities for other-initiation, and the paper's
result is a strong empirical skew toward self-repair. The positions run: within
the trouble-source turn itself, its transition space, the next turn (where a
recipient initiates repair — the clarification request of
[[clarification-request]] lives here), and then the turn after that.

**Third position.** Schegloff (1992) analyses the position after next: the
speaker of the trouble source, having now seen the recipient's response, finds
in it an understanding it did not intend, and repairs its own earlier turn.
This is self-initiated self-repair occasioned by evidence that could not exist
earlier — the evidence *is* the response. Schegloff describes such a turn as
built from a repair initiator ("No…"), a component accepting what was right in
the response, a component rejecting the understanding the response displayed
("I don't mean…"), and the repair proper.

**Why last.** Before third position, no misunderstanding has been evidenced;
after it, the conversation has moved on and the divergence propagates. Hence
Schegloff's title: repair after next turn is *the last structurally provided
defense of intersubjectivity in conversation*. The same paper names a rarer
fourth position, so "last" means last routinely provided, not last possible.

## Why it matters

1. **The stall-9 check does not close before the answer.** The wiki's other
   stall-9 notes place comprehension work ahead of commitment — a confidence
   scale and a threshold ([[clarification-request]]), an accommodated question
   ([[question-under-discussion]]). Schegloff's ordering says the decisive
   check is unavailable until the answerer has committed, because its channel
   is the answer.
2. **The channel is narrow and depends on the answer's shape.** Only the part
   of the reading that the response *enacts* is exposed. An answer that
   delivers a value and enacts nothing about which question it took itself to
   be answering gives the asker nothing to check against.
3. **The move belongs to the asker, and it is self-repair.** At third position
   the asker corrects its own prior question, not the answer. That is a
   different action from rejecting an answer as wrong, and the wiki's answer
   evaluation notes ([[conclusive-answer]]) do not contain it.
4. **The defense is finite.** There is a position after which the structure
   supplies no further correction, so an asking protocol that never occupies it
   loses the misreading permanently rather than later.

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home field** | conversation analysis: recorded, naturally occurring talk between co-present or telephone participants, analysed turn by turn under a turn-taking system with transition-relevance places |
| **Here** | any asker–answerer exchange — including an agent asking a human, or a human asking a model — where a question is followed by an answer and then by the asker's next move |
| **What survives** | the response as the only public display of the reading the question received; the ordering of repair opportunities, with the post-answer position the last routinely provided one; the fact that the post-answer move is the asker repairing its own question |
| **What does not** | the transition space and same-turn positions, which have no analogue in written or asynchronous exchange; the preference for self-repair, which is a measured property of human conversation and is not shown for a machine answerer; any frequency figure — CA reports none for how often third position actually catches a misreading |

## Limits

- Schegloff's positions are defined relative to a turn-taking system that a
  written, batched or single-shot question–answer exchange does not
  instantiate. That such an exchange *has* a third position at all is this
  note's mapping, not a claim of the source.
- The four-component format of the third-position turn is reported here from
  descriptions of Schegloff (1992); the original was not read this pass, so the
  component inventory is the weakest claim in the note.
- Conversation analysis is a single-case, transcript-based method: it shows
  that the position exists and how turns in it are built, not what proportion
  of misunderstandings it catches. Point 4's "loses the misreading
  permanently" is therefore structural, not measured.
- Point 2 — that an answer should be shaped so its assumed reading is
  displayed — is this wiki's inference from the proof procedure, not a
  recommendation Schegloff makes.
