Abstract

arXiv:2605.28354v1 [cs.AI] 27 May 2026

Training large language models as retrievalaugmented reasoning agents typically combines reinforcement learning with an SFT cold
start distilled from a stronger model. However, this paradigm overlooks two fundamental
factors: the dependency structure among subskills, and the possibility that distillation is not
the only route to capability acquisition. We
study this through Plan, a structured agentic
behavior for multi-hop retrieval that decomposes a question into ordered sub-questions before any retrieval is performed, so that each
search step can be anchored to a pre-designed
sub-question instead of drifting under the influence of partially relevant documents retrieved
earlier. However, across three model families spanning 3B to 14B parameters, we find
that an identical reward signal induces qualitatively different RL failure modes. This
phenomenon indicates that successful training
hinges not only on reward design but also on
model-specific feasibility conditions: sufficient
initial entropy, training stability, and prerequisite sub-skills. Motivated by this, we propose a self-bootstrapping paradigm in which a
small-scale seed model generates filtered trajectories that activate Plan in any target model,
eliminating the need for distillation from an external stronger model. Our pipeline activates
Plan across every tested model and consistently
outperforms competitive baselines on multihop QA benchmarks. Our code is available at
https://github.com/qzp2018/PL-Search.

Introduction

Recent work has demonstrated that reinforcement
learning can effectively train large language models to acquire search-augmented reasoning behaviors. Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025) pioneers training
models via RL to interleave thinking and retrieval
within a single trajectory, using outcome-based rewards, and AutoRefine (Shi et al., 2025) extends
this framework by introducing explicit document
1

the agent to drift away from the original question.
Figure 1 contrasts this reactive baseline (b) with our
plan-based approach (a). A global plan, formulated
before any retrieval is performed, anchors each
subsequent reasoning step to a fixed sub-question,
preventing such drift. We formalize plan as a structured agentic behavior and design a plan-aware
reward function that supervises both its structural
validity and the alignment between each reasoning step and its planned sub-question. Through
systematic experiments across three model families (Qwen2.5, Llama3.2, and Qwen3) and scales
ranging from 3B to 14B, we report two findings.
First, direct RL training of plan fails in qualitatively different ways across models, despite
identical reward designs. Models with low initial
entropy lack sufficient exploration room, and the
policy cannot acquire new behaviors regardless of
the reward signal. In addition, some models train
stably at first but collapse abruptly. As entropy
rises, output format breaks down, the reward signal vanishes, and training destabilizes irreversibly.
Models that lack the refine sub-skill also fail: Plan
demands that pre-formulated sub-questions remain
consistent with subsequently retrieved evidence.
Without the ability to integrate information across
this boundary, both forcing the model to acquire
plan and refine simultaneously and forcing it to
learn plan first lead to training collapse. Although
these failures appear different on the surface, they
share a single root cause—the initial model does
not satisfy the implicit conditions required for plan
RL: room to explore, training stability, and the prerequisite sub-skills. A single SFT pass addresses
all three conditions at once, which is why it is effective across all the tested models.
Second, we propose a self-bootstrapping training paradigm. Prior work constructs cold-start
SFT data by distilling trajectories from a larger and
more capable model. We identify an alternative
and effective approach: A small seed model that
satisfies the feasibility conditions identified above
can acquire a plan through direct RL and generate trajectories that activate a plan in any other
target model, and the self-bootstrapped data even
outperform strong-model distillation in our experiments. Specifically, some models, exemplified by
Qwen2.5-3B-Base, can autonomously acquire the
plan through pure RL by first learning refinement
and then learning plan on top of it. We exploit
such a model as a seed that generates plan trajectories, which are then filtered through a quality-aware

pipeline and used as SFT data to activate the plan
capability in any target model. Through this procedure, every target model, regardless of family
or scale, successfully acquires plan and achieves
consistent performance gains on multi-hop benchmarks, without the need for an external stronger
model. We refer to the resulting method as PLSearch. In summary, our contributions are as follows.
• We formulate plan as a structured agentic behavior that prevents trajectory drift by anchoring each retrieval step to a pre-designed subquestion and name it PL-Search, a method
trained with a plan-aware reward that supervises step-level sub-question alignment.
• We identify three feasibility conditions for direct plan RL—sufficient initial entropy, training stability, and the presence of prerequisite sub-skills—and show that the violation
of these conditions manifests as qualitatively
distinct failure modes across model families.
• We propose a self-bootstrapping paradigm in
which a small-scale seed model generates filtered trajectories to activate plan in any target
model, eliminating the need for strong-model
distillation while outperforming it.

failure modes that arise when each is violated

as a universal remedy (§4.4.2), motivating the selfbootstrapping pipeline in Section 3.3.

Condition 3:prerequisite sub-skills. To test
whether the R→P order reflects a genuine dependency between sub-skills, we ablate the training
order on Qwen2.5-3B-Base—the only model on
which R→P succeeds— thereby isolating order effects from model-intrinsic properties. The P→R
curve in Figure 4 exhibits volatile entropy, with
peaks at 1.0 (step 10) and 0.9 (step 120); the critic
score briefly reaches 0.55 before collapsing to 0.10.
Without prior training on refine, each plan step
must operate on raw retrieved documents; the plan
reward can be satisfied superficially at first, but
the lack of evidence integration destabilizes training over time. The R→P order succeeds because
refine provides reliable evidence integration first,
lowering the exploration burden and keeping the
RL gradients coherent.

5

Conclusion

We study the dependency structure among subskills in RL training of search-augmented LLMs,
using plan as a research vehicle. Through experiments across three model families and scales from
3B to 14B, we identify two findings. First, direct
RL training of plan exhibits heterogeneous failure
modes across models, revealing that the bottleneck
of RL-driven acquisition lies in sub-skill dependencies rather than reward design. Second, a selfbootstrapping training paradigm, in which a single
seed model generates plan trajectories to activate
plan capability in other models of comparable scale,
can eliminate the reliance on external strong-model
distillation that current cold-start practices depend
on. We hope these findings motivate further investigation into the structural properties of multi-skill
agentic behaviors in retrieval and related settings.

Implementation Details

Retrieval setup. We use the December 2018
Wikipedia dump (Karpukhin et al., 2020) as the
external knowledge corpus and E5-base-v2 (Wang
et al., 2022) as the retriever, consistent with SearchR1 and AutoRefine. The retriever returns the top
three most relevant documents for each query.
Seed model construction. The seed model is
obtained by applying a two-stage RL procedure
to Qwen2.5-3B-Base. Stage 1 trains the model
with AutoRefine-style RL for 200 steps. Stage 2
initializes from the Stage 1 checkpoint and trains
the model on the plan task using our full plan-aware
reward function for an additional 200 steps. The
resulting checkpoint is used solely as a trajectory
generator for the self-bootstrapping pipeline.

Trajectory filtering pipeline. The filtering
pipeline processes seed-generated trajectories
through four stages: (i) hard filter on format validity (enforcing plan → (think → search →
refine)+ → answer structure), (ii) hard filter
on answer correctness via cover-EM against the
ground-truth answer, (iii) soft scoring via a quality score in [0, 1] that linearly combines three normalized signals: search rounds (weight 0.40), a
log-scaled count of search calls that saturates
at four rounds; query diversity (0.35), the mean
pairwise bigram Jaccard distance across search

Scaling the self-bootstrapped SFT data.

We vary the number of correctness-filtered seedmodel trajectories used for SFT from 1,500 to
4,500, keeping all other components fixed. As

sharpens the harder multi-hop behaviors rather than
uniformly lifting all tasks. So we choose 4500 as
our default SFT data number.

C.1

Prompt Template

Figure 7 shows the prompt template used in
our method. The template enforces a two-phase
structure. In the planning phase, the model
produces a <plan> block listing all ordered
sub-questions before any retrieval, preventing
later search steps from drifting under partially relevant documents. In the execution phase,
the model iterates over each sub-question through a
<think> → <search> → <documents> → <refine>
cycle: <think> grounds reasoning in the planned
sub-question, <search> issues a focused query,
<documents> receives retrieved content (masked
from the training loss), and <refine> distills
evidence for subsequent steps. Finally, the
trajectory ends with <answer>.

Ablation on the Plan-Triggering
Threshold

C.2

The plan reward threshold δ controls the boundary of the Rplan reward shaping: when the plan–
think alignment score Salign exceeds δ, the model
receives full reward (Rplan = 1); otherwise it receives the raw alignment score (Rplan = Salign ).
To assess its sensitivity, we compare our default
setting (δ = 0.25) against fixed thresholds of 0.5
and 0.75, holding the base model, SFT-then-RL
pipeline, and RL recipe constant. As shown in
Table 8, the default threshold attains the best average performance, outperforming the 0.5 and 0.75
variants by 0.010 and 0.014 on EM, respectively,
with consistent per-dataset gains on 6 of 7 benchmarks; the only exception is Bamboogle, whose
small evaluation set makes per-dataset numbers
noisier. The improvement is most pronounced on
the multi-hop benchmarks, consistent with the role
of plan behavior in coordinating multi-step reasoning. Overall, the performance proves that 0.25 is a
suitable choice of threshold.

Token Budget Analysis

To address the concern that the multi-hop gains
reported in Table 3 might stem from a larger generation budget rather than from explicit planning,
we analyze the per-component token distribution
across all training rollouts. Token counts are computed with the model’s native tokenizer over the
full rollout corpus, and the retrieved <documents>
spans are reported separately since they are inserted
by the retrieval system rather than generated by the
policy. As shown in Figure 8, retrieved documents
account for the overwhelming majority of the token
budget (76.1%), whereas the <plan> block adds
only 2.96%. Even when documents are excluded
and we consider only model-generated reasoning
blocks, planning remains one of the lightest components (15.9%), below refine, think, and search.
The planning action therefore introduces negligible
generation overhead, and the observed improvements cannot be attributed to a substantially longer
generation budget.
