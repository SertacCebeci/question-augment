# Theory-ladenness of observation

What an observer takes a datum to be depends on the theory or expectation held when looking. Brewer and Lambert (2001) review cognitive psychology and the history of science and conclude that perception is theory-laden, but with a gradient: the influence is strong only under particular conditions.[^c1]

## The gradient

The authors' summary is that perception is "only strongly theory-laden when the perceptual evidence is ambiguous or degraded, or when it requires a difficult perceptual judgment".[^c2] Clear, well-resolved evidence leaves less room for the held theory to decide what is seen. For a questioner or answerer this places the risk in the quality of the evidence, not in perception in general (my inference).

## Beyond perception

The paper argues that the debate has been framed too narrowly, around perceptual experience. A full account needs theory-ladenness in "attention, perception, data interpretation, data production, memory, and scientific communication".[^c3] Each is a separate point where a prior theory can steer what enters, survives or is reported from an inquiry. The reference list points to the evidence used (the N-ray affair, the Millikan-Ehrenhaft dispute, inattentional blindness, reconstructive remembering of the literature), but only the abstract and references were available, so the specifics are not reported here.

## Not relativism

The authors state that the evidence "does not lead to a relativist account of scientific knowledge".[^c4] Theory shapes observation without making all observation equally theory-determined, so evidence can still discriminate between theories, especially when it is unambiguous (my reading).

## Limits

- The archived text is the abstract plus the reference list; the experiments, effect sizes and case studies are not in it. Mechanisms for each stage are listed but not described.
- The gradient (ambiguous, degraded, hard judgment) is a stated conclusion; how sharply it separates strong from weak cases was not seen.
- Nothing here tests whether knowing about theory-ladenness reduces it in an asker or answerer; that implication is mine and unestablished.
