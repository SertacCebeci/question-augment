# Unfaithfulness propagation kernel

> One fabricated sentence does not stay contained — every sentence after
> it was generated conditioned on it, so its unfaithfulness should count
> against them too, just less and less as the distance grows.

## Definition / Mechanism

Fan, Duan & Xu (2026) start from a gap in ordinary
inter-sample consistency scoring: a claim's consistency score $S(c_i)$
(the fraction of resampled responses that entail it) treats every claim
in a response as independent, but a claim generated by next-token
prediction is conditioned on everything the model already said —
including any earlier claim its own [[claim-decoupled-reinterrogation|context-free
reinterrogation]] found unfaithful. To carry that dependence forward,
they convolve the sequence of per-claim unfaithfulness $1-F(c_i)$ with a
kernel and add the result to each later claim's score: "we propose to
utilize the sequential property of ci ∈ R to model the influence of
unfaithfulness 1 − F (ci ). Specifically, we convolve the sequence
[1−F (c1 ), 1−F (c2 ), . . . , 1−F (ck )] with a influence kernel E to
propagate the impact of unfaithful claims to subsequent claims."
[^c1]

The kernel they settle on is exponential decay over claim distance, and
the final per-claim uncertainty is the ordinary consistency score scaled
by this accumulated, decaying unfaithfulness weight: "We choose the
kernel E as the exponential decay function E(i) = e−λi for claim indices
i = 0, 1, . . . , k. Different choices of kernels are evaluted in Table.
4. The uncertainty score for claim ci is then S scaled by the influence
of claim unfaithfulness U (ci ) = S(ci ) · W (ci )."
[^c2]

A claim right after a fabrication is weighted almost as unfaithful as the
fabrication itself; a claim ten sentences later inherits almost none of
it — the kernel is what turns a single-point faithfulness signal into a
document-shaped one.

The choice of kernel is not incidental: the authors ablate several
propagation functions and report exponential decay as the strongest.
"The influence kernel E serves to propagate the impact of an inconsistent
claim to subsequent claims. In this section, we investigate the
influence of different kernels as propagation functions on the
uncertainty estimation performance."
[^c3]

"we show that the exponential-decay weighting is the most effective
approach to estimate uncertainty in long-form generations."
[^c4]

## Why it matters

Most claim-level uncertainty scores treat a long-form response as a bag
of independent claims. This kernel is a concrete, checkable answer to
"how far does one bad claim's damage travel" — it converts the
observation that generation is sequential (a fact about how the text was
produced) into a scoring rule (a fact about how much to trust each part
of it), and it generalizes past this one paper: "The weighting W can also be
applied to other consistency-based methods to consider the intra-sample
claims dependence."
[^c5]

Any resample-based consistency score can be composed with it.

## Limits

The kernel only propagates forward — a claim earlier in the response is
never re-weighted by a later, unfaithful one, even though a reader
encountering the fabrication would also doubt what came before it. The
decay rate $\lambda$ and the choice of exponential decay over
alternatives are set by ablation on two datasets (FActScore, LongFact)
and five model families, not derived from a model of how conditioning
strength actually falls off with distance, so the specific rate is an
empirical fit, not a mechanism, and may not transfer to responses with
very different claim density or structure.
