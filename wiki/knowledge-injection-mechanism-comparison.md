# Knowledge-injection mechanism comparison

Yang et al. (2026) treat three ways of giving a model the knowledge a question needs as distinct mechanisms that differ in when and how knowledge becomes available: unsupervised fine-tuning (continual pretraining on raw text), supervised fine-tuning on question-answer pairs, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) at inference time.[^c1] They compare them on three 7B models (Mistral-7B, Llama2-7B, Llama2-7B-Instruct), all with pretraining cutoffs before 2024, on QASC (8 options, a 17M-sentence corpus) and a new set of over 10,000 multi-hop questions built from 2024 Wikipedia events (4 options).[^c2]

## Measured pattern

Base accuracy on 2024 Events is 0.276 to 0.326. Continual pretraining lifts it to 0.320 to 0.392, RAG to 0.654 to 0.753, and supervised fine-tuning to 0.813 to 0.884 (Table II).[^c3] On QASC, base accuracy is about 0.35 to 0.46, RAG adds roughly 30 points, and supervised fine-tuning reaches about 0.79 to 0.87.[^c4] The authors read the weak continual-pretraining gain as evidence that "continual pretraining alone provides limited benefits for multi-hop reasoning".[^c5] Chance is 1/8 on QASC and 1/4 on 2024 Events. Base models beat chance, so they retain some knowledge, and the instruction-tuned model beats chance on novel events without the underlying text.[^c6]

## Why the comparison is only partly controlled

The arms are not scored the same way. Supervised models use a classification head over concatenated question and option. Continual-pretrained and RAG models are scored by next-token log-probability of the option letter, with three in-context examples in the RAG prompt.[^c7] The supervised arm is trained on questions of the evaluation's own form: for 2024 Events, DeepSeek-generated questions for training and GPT-generated ones for evaluation, from the same corpus.[^c8] My reading is that the supervised arm's lead therefore mixes knowledge with option-format and question-style learning, the confound described in [[format-familiarity-confound-in-fine-tuned-gains]]. The paper itself says only that supervised fine-tuning shows "the strong effect of taskspecific supervision".[^c9] The RAG arm uses a retrieve-20, rerank-to-4 pipeline with 1000-token chunks, so its number depends on retrieval quality, which was not varied.[^c10]

## What it means for asking and answering

For questions whose facts post-date the model, supplying text at answer time is what moves accuracy without task-specific labels. Exposing the model to the same text in training, without questions, barely does. This is a measured pattern on one setup, not a rule.

## Limits

- Only 7B models, LoRA on query and value projections, one RAG configuration, and multiple-choice log-probability scoring; open-ended answers were not tested.
- The supervised arm uses a different head and in-distribution training questions, so its ranking against RAG is not a clean comparison of knowledge injection (my inference).
- Training questions were generated by LLMs from the same event chunks as the test questions, so overlap between train and test facts is possible; the paper does not audit it.
- No ablation separates retrieval failure from integration failure on multi-hop items, though the introduction motivates both. The "multi-hop reasoning" interpretation is the authors', and accuracy alone cannot exclude memorization or retrieval gains.[^c11]
- Continual pretraining ran at one learning rate (1e-5) with LoRA for 20 epochs; weak gains may reflect that setup.
- Single run per cell, no variance reported.
