# Generator-rendered question items

Image-based benchmark items have usually been built in pieces: the picture came from a generator or a camera, and the question text was written by a person or by a language model, so the generator supplied only the visual part. The paper's alternative puts the generator on the whole composite item. Its key distinction is that "the VQA creation process is carried out by the image generation model, with humans intervening solely for verification and prompt refinement."[^g1] The earlier practice is described as one where the generator "played only a supplementary role, producing just the visual part while the question text still had to be created separately by humans or by an LMM, incurring additional cost."[^g2]

## What the generator is given

The content is not invented by the generator. Each item already exists in JMMMU, and the generator receives "both the question image and the question text" with a prompt "to integrate them into a single composite image".[^g3] Ground for this is the paper's own statement that the 1,320 items have "contents identical to those in the original JMMMU".[^g4] So the method is a rendering pipeline over supplied questions, not a generator of new questions.

The prompt is the unit of revision. The paper varied "six components as parameters to generate a diverse set of images": background, background colour, font, margin, capture state and aspect ratio.[^g5] Human effort goes into the prompt, not the image: the authors note that "humans cannot easily edit content directly inside the image in the same way as text-based QA".[^g6] Some controls failed: "we found that Nano Banana Pro does not allow control over these image tags", and an explicit instruction to keep them "significantly degraded the quality of the generated images".[^g7]

## Checking as regeneration, not filtering

Review was by the authors, with a custom annotation tool. "In the first review round, 71% of the questions passed."[^g8] The failures were of several kinds: an unrelated image, unreadable text, missing or wrong question text, or an unnatural image. "For these failed cases, we regenerated the images with the same prompt or prompts with minor prompt adjustments."[^g9] The contrast the paper draws is with a closely related method: "LogicOCR performs manual verification and discards a portion of the generated samples."[^g10]

## Where generation stops

Some items were written by hand. "We manually created 67 samples that Nano Banana Pro had difficulty generating."[^g11] The listed causes were long question text, "small or difficult-to-render text within the question image (36 samples)", extreme aspect ratios, domains such as chemical formulas or musical notation, and policy rejections. The paper reports that "approximately 95% of all the questions in JMMMU-Pro were generated by Nano Banana Pro."[^g12]

## Limits

- One generator (an image model used through its API), one language, one benchmark. The abstract says the benchmark was built "at low cost"[^g13], but the text gives no cost figure to compare with a manual or LMM-written pipeline.
- The pass rate is for the authors' own review. Agreement between reviewers is not reported; a final crosscheck was done "to eliminate inconsistencies in the authors' evaluation standards," which removes drift but does not measure it.
- Acceptance is judged on whether the composite matches the original, not on whether the item is harder, cleaner or more valid as a measure. The paper's claim that the approach is a "guideline" for future benchmarks rests on the generator's current capability, which the authors expect to change; they do not test that.
- The generator's rendering limits set which content can enter the benchmark, so the item set is biased toward short text and common layouts. The paper's manual fallback is the sign of this bias, not a removal of it.
- The concept here is the division of labour, not the particular generator. Whether a different generator would fall into the same division is not shown.
