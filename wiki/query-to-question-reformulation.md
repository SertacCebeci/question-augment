# Query-to-question reformulation

An agent that searches does not ask the way a person asks. Its queries tend to follow web-search style, with keywords, phrases and quotation marks for exact matching.[^c1] Many neural rankers are trained on natural-language questions such as MS MARCO, so the query the ranker sees at use time differs in form from the query it learned on.[^c2] The paper calls this a training-inference query mismatch and tests it on BrowseComp-Plus with the gpt-oss-20b agent.

## Mechanism

Query-to-question (Q2Q) puts a reformulator between the agent and the ranker. It maps the agent's query q_t to a natural-language question, and the question is what the ranker receives. A second variant also feeds in the agent's recent reasoning trace.[^c3] The trace matters because a bare keyword query is ambiguous. For the query "61,880" football attendance, the query-only rewrite became "What is the football attendance number 61,880?". That shifts the focus from finding matches to explaining the number. With the trace, the rewrite was "What football match had an attendance of 61,880?", which keeps the intent.[^c4]

## What was measured

With SPLADE-v3 and Qwen3-Embed-8B, the query-plus-reasoning variant (Q+R) consistently and significantly beat raw agent queries. With SPLADE-v3 the relative gains were 7.34% in recall and 7.95% in accuracy.[^c5] The query-only variant gave little to no improvement.[^c6] The same mismatch affects re-ranking. Rank1, a reasoning re-ranker trained on MS MARCO, often misread keyword-rich queries. In one failure it treated the independent keywords "radiation" and "protein" as one semantic unit.[^c7] Feeding Rank1 the Q+R questions gave relative gains of 3.40% in recall and 5.69% in accuracy over raw queries.[^c8]

The effect depends on the ranker. For BM25, Q2Q questions hurt, so keyword-style queries suit lexical matching better than questions do.[^c9] Rewriting toward the training distribution is therefore a fix for rankers trained on questions, not a general improvement. The authors also report that learned sparse and multi-vector retrievers with millions of parameters generalise better to web-search-style queries than 7B/8B single-vector dense models.[^c10]

DrugRAG reaches the query side from the opposite end. It does not rewrite a short query into a question; it reduces a full clinical question to a few concepts, and the reasoning model's output is what the retriever receives: "to generate a reproducible and semantically focused retrieval query."[^c12] The authors concede that this extraction step may carry part of the gain, since the reasoning model's framing could change retrieval independently of the evidence source.[^c13] So the two papers agree that the form of the query matters, but they disagree about the direction of the fix, and neither separates the reformulator's contribution from the retriever's.

## Limits

One agent (gpt-oss-20b) was used here, because the authors dropped GLM-4.7-Flash for lack of GPU.[^c11] One dataset with a fixed corpus was used, BrowseComp-Plus, and one reformulator model. The reformulation adds an LLM call per search, and its cost was not weighed against the gain in what I read. The causal story, that mismatch rather than some other effect of rewriting explains the gain, is the authors' reading. The query-only variant's failure shows that adding the reasoning context may carry some of the benefit. Whether the gain holds for trained-on-keywords rankers or other tasks is untested.
