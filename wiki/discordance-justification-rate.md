# Discordance justification rate

> When a protocol licenses an exception to its own commitment rule, how often
> the licence is actually **named in the output** is a separate measurable
> quantity from how often the exception is taken — and across bodies running
> the same protocol it runs from 0% (Chong et al. 2023) to 76% (Hershenfeld et > al. 2026), inside a reported literature range of 16%–68%.

## Definition / Mechanism

GRADE permits a strong recommendation on low-certainty evidence only in five[^s1][^s2][^s3]
enumerated paradigmatic situations ([[discordant-recommendation]]). The
**discordance justification rate** is the proportion of an issuing body's
*discordant* recommendations whose text explicitly cites one of those
situations. Its denominator is the discordant recommendations, not all
recommendations, and it is measured by coding the published artifact — not by
asking the panel.

Three audits of bodies all declaring GRADE:

| Body, audit | Discordant recs | Licence named in text |
|---|---|---|
| Eight national guidelines — Chong et al. (2023) | 96 of 151 strong (63.6%) | **0 of 96** — no guideline invoked a paradigmatic situation |
| 22 ASH guidelines 2018–2026 — Hershenfeld et al. (2026) | 33 of 77 strong (43%) | **25 of 33 (76%)**; 4 gave a non-paradigmatic reason, 4 gave none |
| 33 WHO guidelines — Alexander et al. (2016) | 160 | see below — a *different* measurement |

Hershenfeld et al. place their own 43% inside a literature range of 12%–64%
for discordance and 16%–68% for justification frequency in other societies,
and report a time trend *within* their one body: 62% of ASH discordant
recommendations in guidelines published 2018–2021 carried an explicit
paradigmatic justification against 100% of the 2023–2026 subgroup (P = .03),
the two subgroups summing to the body's overall 76%. The rate is therefore not
a property of the protocol but of a body's editorial habit at a date.

Alexander, Brito, Neumann et al. (2016, *J Clin Epidemiol* 72:98–106) measured
something else on the same objects: whether each of 160 WHO discordant
recommendations *fit* a paradigm in the coders' judgement. Only 25 (15.6%)
did; 33 (21%) rested on a misclassification of evidence quality, 29 (18%) were
good practice statements, and 73 (46%) should in the coders' view have been
conditional rather than strong. Fit and citation are different numbers over
the same denominator: a body may name a licence it does not have, or hold one
it never names.

## Known as

| Field | Name there | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| Guideline meta-research (Hershenfeld et al. 2026) | explicit paradigmatic justification | exact |
| Guideline meta-research | documented justification for discordant recommendations | close — descriptive phrasing used here; no source names it so |

**Not this concept.** A **paradigm citation rate** as used informally in
clinical guideline methodology counts the same numerator against no fixed
denominator; since the denominator (discordant recommendations, not all
recommendations) is what fixes this object, that name is not an alias of it.
The **discordance rate** itself (12%–64%; 63.6% in Chong, 43% in ASH) is a
different number over a different denominator — strong recommendations, not
discordant ones — and moves independently of this one: ASH has the lower
discordance rate *and* the higher justification rate. **Consistency with GRADE
guidance** (Alexander et al. 2016) is a third quantity: a coder's judgement of
whether the situation obtained, not a property of the text. None is an alias.

## Why it matters

Stall 10 has, in [[discordant-recommendation]], the only deployed protocol that
lets an answerer commit hardest where its certainty is lowest — on a named
licence. This note measures what happens to that licence in the field: in the
largest audit that coded for it, it was cited **zero** times across eight
guidelines, while another body naming the same protocol reached 76%. A
commitment rule with a declared exception clause therefore has two distinct
compliance quantities, and only the second is observable from the answer
alone. For a derived procedure this makes *the licence a required field of the
answer*, and its naming rate an audit statistic separate from the exception
rate — the ASH time trend shows the field is cheap to enforce once a body
decides to.

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| **In its home field** | a published guideline document, a fixed enumerated licence list, two independent coders reading the artifact retrospectively, a denominator fixed by another coded variable (discordance) |
| **Here** | any answerer whose protocol declares conditions under which it may commit beyond its evidence; the rate at which its answers state which condition applies |
| **What survives** | the split into three separately measurable quantities — exception taken, licence cited, licence actually held; the finding that the second is not implied by the first; the audit design (code the artifact, not the deliberation) |
| **What does not** | the five-item licence list, which is specific to interventions with a comparator and a population; the coders' fit judgement, which required clinical expertise per recommendation and has no cheap analogue; and any inference from citation rate to answer quality — no audit here links either rate to whether the recommendation was later overturned |

## Limits

- The claim that these are two quantities and not one rests on **three audits
  with different coding schemes**, not on one instrument applied to several
  bodies. Chong et al. coded explicit invocation, Hershenfeld et al. coded
  explicit justification including non-paradigmatic reasons, Alexander et al.
  coded fit. The 0%–76% spread between bodies is therefore partly a spread in
  measurement, and this note may not be used to rank bodies against each
  other.
- The 12%–64% and 16%–68% ranges are Hershenfeld et al.'s summary of five
  prior studies; the endpoints were not read in their own papers here.
- The ASH temporal trend (62% → 100%) is a within-body subgroup split of 33
  discordant recommendations and observational; the 100% is that subgroup, not
  any body's rate, and the trend does not establish that anything was
  enforced.
- No source relates either rate to outcomes. Whether a named licence makes a
  discordant commitment better calibrated is untested.
