# Information-gathering interview style

> A field whose respondents routinely have an incentive to withhold or
> falsify has measured a contrast between two whole interview styles: the
> one whose questions are open and rapport-led yields more true and fewer
> false confessions than the one that opens with an assertion of guilt and
> closed confirmatory questions. What it did not measure is a question order.

## Definition / Mechanism

Investigative-interviewing research names two suspect-interview styles as a[^s1][^s2]
package of co-varying moves, not a single manipulated question type. The
**information-gathering** style establishes rapport and uses open-ended,
exploratory questions to obtain a full account; any narrowing or probing
comes after that free account. The **accusatorial** style opens by asserting
the suspect's guilt and uses closed, confirmatory questions aimed directly
at a confession — it is closed and confirmatory throughout, not "closed
first, then open" (Vrij, Hope & Fisher 2014).

Meissner et al. (2014) report two meta-analyses over this contrast. The
**field** meta-analysis (actual suspects, veracity unknown) shows that both
styles raise the confession rate relative to direct questioning without a
method; it cannot say which confessions were true. The **experimental**
meta-analysis (laboratory paradigms with known ground truth) is the only one
that scores veracity: there the information-gathering style increases true
confessions and reduces false confessions, while the accusatorial style
increases both true and false confessions (Meissner et al. 2014). The
veracity claim in this note rests on the experimental subset alone.

What this changes on stall 2: it gives the practitioner's "which axis first"
cell an outcome-grounded contrast — a style that is open and rapport-led
throughout its opening beats one that is closed and accusatory throughout,
scored by confession veracity rather than by a computed value of
information. Reading this as an *ordering* rule — "open before closed" — is
this wiki's inference from the information-gathering style's internal
structure (rapport, then free account, then probing); no study in the pool
takes one question set and permutes it into two orders. As an inference it
sits alongside the divergent-then-convergent order in human ideation
([[divergent-convergent]]) and Huffman's frequency-first order
([[huffman-coding]]) already on the map, with a different warrant: a
behavioral outcome under an adversarial respondent, not [[entropy]] or
frequency.

## Why it matters

- It gives map-selection's open "interview methodology (funnel, inverted
  funnel)" cell a first outcome-grounded entry, distinct in kind from the
  information-theoretic rows already on the map: the style is justified by
  what actually came back (true vs. false confessions), not by a prior
  model of $W$.
- It is measured under the least cooperative respondent the wiki's selection
  map has yet: someone with an active reason to conceal or mislead. That the
  open, rapport-led style still wins there is stronger evidence for it than
  any result obtained from a compliant answerer.
- It names a mechanism the wiki's other selection criteria do not model at
  all: the answerer's incentive to distort. [[expected-information-gain]]
  and [[value-of-information]] both assume the answerer's distribution is
  the one being queried about, not a party who might steer it.

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home field** | a criminal-justice interview of a suspect who may be guilty and has an incentive to withhold, deny or fabricate; success is scored by confession veracity and by account completeness/accuracy |
| **Here** | a general procedure choosing which kind of question to open with when the respondent's cooperation cannot be assumed |
| **What survives** | the style contrast — an opening that is rapport-led and open-ended, with narrowing only after a free account, measurably improves both what comes back and its truthfulness relative to an opening that asserts a conclusion and asks closed confirmatory questions, even against an incentive to conceal |
| **What does not** | the ordering reading ("open before closed") as a *measured* result — it is inferred from the style's structure, not tested as a permuted order; the confession-specific success measure (most tasks have no analogue of a "true confession"); the legal and ethical constraints on which techniques are admissible; and the internal composition of the "style" — the meta-analysis scores rapport-building, minimization/maximization tactics and question type as one bundle, so which component inside it does the work is not established by this evidence |

## Limits

- The measured object is a contrast between two whole styles. No study in
  Meissner et al.'s pool varies question order within one style; "open
  before closed" is this note's reading of the information-gathering
  style's structure (rapport, free account, then probing), laid over an
  empirical style contrast, and is not itself a measured ordering effect.
- Only the experimental (ground-truth) meta-analysis separates true from
  false confessions. The field meta-analysis shows both styles raise
  confession rates over direct questioning and cannot score veracity, so
  the veracity advantage is a laboratory result whose field generalization
  is not established by this evidence.
- The advantage is measured on respondents with an active interest in
  concealment or deception (criminal suspects, real and simulated). Whether
  the same style helps with a cooperative respondent who has no motive to
  withhold is not shown by this evidence, and the size of any advantage
  there is unknown.
- "Information-gathering" and "accusatorial" are each a bundle of
  co-varying techniques, not one manipulated variable; Meissner et al.
  (2014) report the bundle's effect on the confession outcome, not a
  decomposition of which move inside the bundle carries it.
- This note is built from vrij-2014's verified abstract (archived in
  `wiki/meta/sources/vrij-2014.html`); repeated attempts to retrieve the
  paper's full body text (an 8-page policy-insights review) were blocked by
  the publisher and the author's repository, so the mechanism detail above
  is drawn from the meta-analysis it summarizes (meissner-2014) rather than
  from Vrij, Hope & Fisher's own elaboration.
