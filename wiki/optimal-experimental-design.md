# Optimal experimental design

Classical optimal experimental design (OED) picks experiments before they are run, by how much information they would carry about a parameter. The information is the [[fisher-information|Fisher information matrix]] the experiments would produce, and the choice is made by a scalar function of that matrix. The paper's summary of the field is that OED provides principles for choosing experiments to maximise information, "often by optimizing scalar criteria of the" Fisher information matrix.[^c1]

## Mechanism

Experiments contribute additively to the information matrix, so choosing the best K out of many candidates is a combinatorial problem. The standard move is a relaxation: instead of a fixed set, choose a distribution over experiments (a design measure), then realise it by sampling. The paper states the move directly: the approach "is to relax this problem by optimizing over a distribution of" experiments.[^c2] The optimum of the relaxed problem is a measure, not a list, and it is only later turned into concrete trials.

A matrix cannot be ranked directly, so a scalar must be chosen. A-design weights all parameter components equally; V-design minimises error on a chosen projection. These "are called A- or V-designs, respectively, and result in" a scalarisation of the matrix.[^c3] The D criterion, the log-determinant of the information matrix, is the objective of the paper's REINFORCE experiment.[^c4] The design goal is then stated as: "Theorem 4.1 motivates maximizing the FIM" in order to reduce estimation error.[^c5]

What makes the relaxation useful is convexity. When the scalar is concave in the matrix and the matrix is linear in the design, the relaxed problem is a concave maximisation over a convex set, so a local method reaches the global optimum. The paper proves this for its tabular setting: "In tabular settings, we prove that our objective is concave and global optimal" solution can be reached.[^c6] Its solver is Frank–Wolfe, a method known to "converge to maximum on concave objectives defined on convex sets".[^c7]

## Transfer to sequential queries

Classical OED ranks single experiments. The paper's variant ranks policies. Earlier work applied OED to active exploration by optimising the visitation measure of one policy; "Our work adapts OED" to preference queries by designing K policies that each generate candidate responses, so the design variable is a state visitation measure.[^c8] The choice model is a multinomial logit over the K options, and "This model is a generalization of the Bradley-Terry model" for K = 2.[^c9]

| | |
|---|---|
| **In its home field** | a set of experiments, each with a parametric likelihood whose Fisher matrix can be written down, and a scalar criterion over the summed matrix |
| **Here** | queries are generated by policies; the design is a measure over states; the likelihood is a choice model assumed for the human |
| **What survives** | ranking designs before running them; the relaxation from sets to measures; concavity over a polytope of visitation measures, which gives a global optimum for the design |
| **What does not** | the optimum for a θ-free design, which is only a surrogate (see Limits); any optimality claim for a generator whose state space is not finite |

The result is a design rule for which questions to put to a person: a ranking by expected information about a parameter, computed before the answers arrive. It does not say which questions a person would find easy or useful.

## Limits

- **Local and θ-dependent.** The criterion depends on the unknown parameter. The paper replaces the choice probability by its average under an uninformative prior, roughly 1/K. For K = 2 this is exact under a Gaussian prior; for larger K the authors call it "for K>2 it is a reasonable approximation when alternatives are diverse/symmetric".[^c10] In the sections read I did not find an error bound for this step, so for K > 2 the surrogate is an approximation whose size is unknown.
- **Guarantees are for discrete states.** The global convergence result is stated under "Assuming a generative model on discrete space, we" provide convergence guarantees.[^c11] The vocabulary-free REINFORCE extension is listed as "Non-convex" in the paper's comparison table, so the optimum guarantee does not carry over to it.[^c12]
- **Static design.** The main algorithm fixes the design before any answer. "The static version does not use partial" feedback from earlier queries; the adaptive variant is only sketched.[^c13]
- **Simulated people.** The experiments use a synthetic ground-truth linear model and an LLM as the preference oracle. The authors say that a human study would be "a scale prohibitively expensive with" human participants.[^c14] Fewer queries for real people are therefore not shown here.
- **Likelihood is assumed.** Everything above needs a written choice model. For questions answered in free text there is none, and the criterion cannot be computed without first choosing one.
- The Fisher criterion is local: it ranks designs by curvature around the current parameter estimate, not by the posterior mass the Bayesian criteria use (see [[bayesian-experimental-design]]).
