# Information-gain guarded probing

> When a robot's belief over an object's pose is multimodal, greedily
> picking the next physical probe by expected [[entropy]] drop resolves the
> [[efe-planning-as-variational-inference|ambiguity]] better than probing the current best guess.

## Definition / Mechanism

BayesContact keeps a particle belief $b_k(x) = \sum_i w_k^{(i)}\delta(x -
x_k^{(i)})$ over an object's pose, formed by fusing rendered-depth and
simulated-contact likelihoods in a Sequential Monte Carlo filter
(Kamireddypalli et al. (2026)). To choose the next
**guarded contact probe** — a bounded vertical dip that stops on a
force/torque threshold — from a finite candidate set $A_{dip}$, the paper's
Algorithm 1 computes the prior entropy $H_0$ of the particle weights, then
for each candidate action simulates the contact and depth evidence each
particle would produce, predicts the resulting posterior weights and their
entropy $H_{k+1}(a)$, and selects
$a^* = \arg\max_a \big(H_0 - \mathbb{E}_i[H_{k+1}(a)]\big)$: the action
whose *simulated* outcome is expected to shrink the belief's entropy the
most.[^c1]

Compared against two other probe-selection policies on the same particle
belief — MAP probing (probe where the highest-weight particle says) and
[[thompson-sampling|Thompson sampling]] (probe according to a particle drawn from the current
weights) — the information-gain rule and MAP show similar convergence on
overall pose error (ADD-S) and position error from the same vision-only
prior, but the information-gain rule handles orientation multimodality
better: it selects actions that reduce expected posterior entropy rather
than committing to the current best hypothesis, and across the simulated
peg geometries it reaches a 2 mm ADD threshold in fewer probes and with
higher insertion success than MAP and Thompson sampling in several
geometries.[^c2]

Fusing force/torque-derived contact evidence with vision (of any of the
three probing policies) over vision-only inference improves pose
observability and insertion success by 30% overall.[^c3]

## Why it matters

This is a physical instance of "which axis first" (stall 2): the axis is a
discrete probing location/orientation, not a question in language, and the
candidate answers are not enumerated in advance but generated by
simulating each hypothesis under each candidate action — the same
one-step, entropy-reduction selection rule as [[expected-information-gain]],
computed over a Monte Carlo posterior instead of an analytic one.

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home field** | [[bayesian-experimental-design|Bayesian experimental design]] ([[expected-information-gain|EIG]], Lindley 1956): a parameter with an explicit prior/likelihood, a design $q$ chosen to maximise expected entropy drop over its possible answers |
| **Here** | a discrete set of physical guarded-contact actions on a robot, scored by simulating each action's contact/depth outcome for every belief particle and averaging the resulting predicted entropy |
| **What survives** | the one-step, expectation-over-answers selection logic, and the qualitative result that entropy-reduction outperforms "probe the mode" (MAP) when the belief is multimodal |
| **What does not** | any guarantee of the EIG sign result or of optimality — the entropy here is over a finite, resampled particle set, not the true posterior, and the paper reports only a one-scenario qualitative comparison (Fig. 5) plus per-geometry counts (Fig. 6), not a significance test |

## Limits

The claim rests on a small, geometry-specific simulation study (five peg
geometries, 20 simulated poses each, 5 seeds) plus limited real-robot
trials, and the information-gain policy is compared against only two other
policies (MAP, Thompson sampling) and one parametric baseline (Man-UKF);
the paper itself states the results "support contact-driven improvement
most strongly" while calling information-gain probing merely "a promising
mechanism," not a demonstrated general advantage.[^c4]
