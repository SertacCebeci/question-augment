# Visual robustness score

V-FAT separates two sources of text bias in multimodal models: internal corpus bias (pretraining statistics override an atypical image) and external instruction bias (alignment-induced sycophancy to a misleading prompt).[^c1] It tests them in three levels: L1 atypical images, L2 a misleading instruction on a normal image, L3 both together.[^c2]

**The metric.** Each response is correct, trap-conforming (matches the suggested wrong answer), or another error. Mean textual dominance (mTDS) is the share of predictions equal to the trap; resistance is R = 1 - mTDS.[^c3] The score per level is the harmonic mean of accuracy and resistance, VRS = 2·mAcc·R / (mAcc + R).[^c4] The authors argue the harmonic mean penalizes models that reach accuracy by guessing along with text priors, and models accurate in neutral settings that give in to misleading instructions.[^c5]

**Why this matters for asking and answering.** Accuracy alone cannot tell weak perception from strong language priors from compliance. Resistance is measured separately from correctness, so a wrong answer that is not the trap (perception failure) counts differently from a wrong answer that follows the prompt (compliance). That reading is mine; the paper's claim is that existing benchmarks cannot decouple the sources.[^c6]

**Headline finding.** Across 12 frontier models, the authors report strong results on existing benchmarks but significant visual collapse under high linguistic dominance.[^c7]

**A scene-level version of the same split.** A synthetic test keeps indoor layouts and changes object scale. On the abnormal set, Gemini's size accuracy falls "from 54.7 to 28.3," and its distance accuracy falls too, while human evaluators show "performance shows only a marginal degradation on these" tasks.[^c8][^c9] The authors read the size collapse as models answering from class-level size priors: "simply output canonical object sizes, a strategy that fails" in abnormal scenes.[^c10] This is the L1 idea at scene scale: an atypical input separates what a model expects from what it sees. The work reports the size and distance pattern on one pair of tasks. It does not establish that size is generally more prior-bound than distance (my reading).

## Limits

- Questions and distractors were generated by MLLMs and checked by a critic model, so the traps may reflect those generators' idea of a plausible lie.
- Resistance only counts the single designated trap; other bias-driven errors fall under "other error".
- Harmonic mean weights accuracy and resistance equally, an unjustified-by-test choice; I did not read ablations of it.
- Per-model numbers and open-ended judging were not read in detail. The text states 4,026 instances in the abstract but 4,020 in the introduction.
