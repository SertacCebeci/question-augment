# Warmth-induced agreement under uncertainty

In a between-subjects manipulation (warm versus neutral chatbot), the warm condition was perceived as warmer (M = 5.66 against the neutral condition).[^c1] But "Warmth did not predict any verification behavior, nor did it significantly influence accuracy."[^c2] The effect appeared only in agreement: participants agreed more with the warm chatbot when its answer was incorrect; the increase in the correct condition was not significant.[^c3]

## Mechanism

When the chatbot's first answer was wrong and conflicted with other sources, participants faced uncertainty. In the presence of AI, uncertainty does not prompt more information seeking; agreeing under uncertainty was read as a decision to trust the AI rather than invest more effort.[^c4] Warmth is proposed to act subconsciously: only 7 participants mentioned it, and they disagreed on whether it helped or reduced credibility.[^c5] The authors' conclusion is that warmth works as an indirect nudge at the final decision, after conscious checking is done (their interpretation; the mediation is not tested as a separate causal chain).

## Limits

- The hypothesis was only partially supported: significance held for incorrect answers alone.
- Seven open-ended mentions are too few to say how warmth is perceived across people.
- One warm/neutral operationalization, one question set, a browser-extension sample.
- Direction of "uncertainty" is inferred from conflict between sources, not measured per answer.
