# At-issueness and projection

Projection is the survival of an implication when its trigger sits under an entailment-cancelling operator (negation, a question, a conditional antecedent, an epistemic modal).[^c1] "Carla stopped smoking" implies Carla smoked before; "Did Carla stop?", "Carla didn't stop" and "If Carla stopped" all keep that implication but drop "she stopped now". Classically this was credited to presupposition. Simons, Tonhauser, Beaver and Roberts argue that it is not: non-restrictive relative clauses, expressives and honorifics project too, and common-ground, anaphoric and multi-dimensional theories do not cover all triggers. Their replacement is one claim: "Meanings project IFF they are not at-issue,"[^c2] with at-issueness defined from the discourse, not from the trigger.

## Mechanism

Two hypotheses carry the account. Only not-at-issue implications can project, and operators target at-issue content.[^c3] An operator's scope is roughly the main point of the utterance; whatever is not the main point is left outside the scope and so survives.

At-issueness is defined from the [[question-under-discussion]]. An assertion is relevant to a question under discussion (QUD) if it contextually entails a partial or complete answer to it.[^c4] A proposition p is then at-issue relative to Q iff the yes/no question ?p is relevant to Q.[^c5] The test is run on the question that p would raise, not on p's truth.

Worked case: Q "What most surprised you about the first graders?", A "They didn't know that you can eat raw vegetables." The factive complement "you can eat raw vegetables" has a yes/no question none of whose answers helps answer Q, so it is not-at-issue and projects. The knowledge claim "they knew it" is relevant, so negation can target it.[^c6]

Consequences claimed:

- One explanation for classical presuppositions, conventional implicatures and other triggers (the paper names the prejacent of "only"), because they share the discourse property, not the lexical class.[^c7]
- Intonationally backgrounded material projects while focused material is what negation associates with, because focus signals the QUD and backgrounded material is independent of it (a sketch, which the authors say needs further fleshing out).[^c8]
- Projection is not fixed by the trigger. A normally projective trigger that is made at-issue should fail to project. "Does France have a king?" answered with "the king of France didn't attend the opening of Parliament" yields a non-projecting reading, since the existence implication is at-issue.[^c9]

Apparent counterexamples, such as answering "Are there any boys in your class?" with "I don't like the boys in my class", are bad out of context. The paper's reading is that neither the projecting nor the non-projecting interpretation is appropriate. They become acceptable when the hearer can reconstruct a higher implicit question (why are no boys invited?) relative to which the existence implication is not-at-issue.[^c10] So the QUD a hearer assigns, not the words alone, decides what the answerer is taken to commit to.

## For asking and answering (this wiki's inference)

This is my inference, not a claim of the paper. If a question's wording puts an assumption into not-at-issue position, a negative or hedged reply will not deny it, so the assumption passes unchallenged. That gives a scope-based account of why [[presupposition-accommodation]] is silent. Conversely, an answerer who wants to deny a presupposed element must make it at-issue by changing the question being answered.

## Limits

- The relevance definition is acknowledged as "overly restrictive" and needs weakening to cover moves that merely raise the probability of an answer (e.g. "It's cloudy" to "Is it going to rain?").[^c11]
- The paper is theoretical and uses constructed judgments; it reports no experiments or corpus rates.
- QUDs are usually implicit, so applying the test requires the analyst to reconstruct one; the paper gives no procedure for doing this and concedes that building clean test cases is delicate.
- Defaults, the role of the projective reading as a preference, and the sketch for focus are left open by the authors.
- Nothing here was tested on machine agents; the section above on asking and answering is the wiki's extrapolation.
