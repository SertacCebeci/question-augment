# Presentation modality shapes question intention, not surface form

Park et al. analysed spoken learner questions to an embodied virtual agent along six dimensions (who, when, where, what, why, how: timing, topic, type, grammatical form, intention, volume and length). They compared an augmented-reality agent placed in the physical room with a screen-video agent during CPR instruction.[^c1]

## What differed

The AR agent produced higher spatial and social presence, and more frequent and longer questions focused on clarification and understanding. The video agent drew questions about procedural refinement.[^c1]

The difference sat in intention and intensity, not in the visible shape of the question. Timing and content (topic, type, form) were broadly comparable across modalities.[^c2] In AR, learners asked to deepen understanding or clarify ambiguity in the agent's answers. In video, they narrowed to specific factual uncertainties.[^c3] The authors' summary is that modality shaped "not the surface characteristics of questions, but rather the underlying intentions and the intensity".[^c4]

## Presence is a filter, not a universal mediator

Presence did not carry the effect of modality onto every aspect of questioning. It suppressed questions during the action stage, when learners watched the demonstration, and encouraged reflective questions at the ending stage. Spatial presence also narrowed topics to core CPR procedures.[^c5] It did not significantly alter question type, form, intention, or overall depth.[^c6] So presence alone does not explain the modality differences.

## Learner traits moderate

Motivation changed direction and focus. Extrinsic goal orientation raised ending-stage questioning, and task value widened topics to legal and ethical context. Motivation did not change question intensity or depth.[^c7] Learning strategies changed execution: critical thinking moderated timing and grammatical form, and time and environment management predicted longer questions.[^c8]

## Outcome

Declarative quiz scores did not differ. AR learners outperformed video learners on the practice task, which the authors read as an advantage for procedural skill.[^c9]

## Mechanism for asking and answering (my inference)

Because the same surface question can carry different intentions depending on the medium, a respondent that classifies questions only by type or form will misjudge what is wanted. A question's intention has to be inferred from context. The authors' design implications follow this: reinforce each medium's natural inquiry mode and prompt the one it under-elicits ("Why" in video, procedural accuracy in AR).[^c10]

## Limits

- One task (CPR), one pair of media, one lab study; generality to other domains or to text-only asking is not shown.
- The "presence as filter" reading rests on which mediation paths were significant. Absent paths are weak evidence of absence.
- The design implications are the authors' proposals and were not tested here.
- My inference about intention-aware answering is not something the study measured.
