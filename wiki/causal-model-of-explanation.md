# Causal model of explanation

On the causal model, we explain phenomena by giving their causes, or, where the phenomenon is itself a causal regularity, by giving the mechanism linking cause and effect.[^c1] Explaining why smoking causes cancer does not cite a cause of the causal link; it cites the mechanism that makes it.[^c1] The extract (an abstract only) argues the model is better placed than three rivals: explanation as a reason to believe, as making familiar, and as a Deductive-Nomological argument.

## What the model buys

**Understanding is more knowledge, not super-knowledge.** The model distinguishes understanding why a phenomenon occurs from merely knowing that it does: understanding is knowledge of the phenomenon plus knowledge of its causal history.[^c2] For an asker this means a "why" question is not answered by further evidence that the thing happened; it needs a cause.

**No regress of whys.** Something can explain without itself being explained, because one can know a cause without knowing the cause of the cause.[^c3] An answer is therefore complete enough at one causal step; the asker need not be given the whole chain.

**Self-evidencing explanations are legitimate.** The speed of recession of a star explains its red-shift although the shift is part of the evidence for that speed. The explanation gives no non-circular reason to believe the phenomenon, yet nothing bars knowing a cause through its effects.[^c4] So an answer may be good even when the asker's only reason to accept it is the very fact asked about.

**Familiar is not understood.** The blueness of the sky is perfectly familiar yet its cause may be unknown, so familiarity cannot be what explanation supplies.[^c5]

**No law is needed.** One does not need to give a law to give a cause, nor know a law to have good reason to believe a cause is a cause.[^c6] The asymmetry that recession explains red-shift but not conversely follows from causes explaining effects and not the reverse; a conjunction of laws fails to explain its conjuncts because conjunctions do not cause them.[^c7]

## Limits

Only the abstract is in the archived text, so the arguments themselves, the treatment of contrast (the paper's title topic), and its objections are not available; the claims here are the abstract's assertions, not demonstrated results. The model is philosophical, with no measurement of how people ask or answer. It leaves open non-causal why-questions (mathematical, normative). Whether the abstract's contrast with rivals is decisive is contested in the literature, not tested here.
