# AI-structured elicitation: clearer, not lighter

> A structured, AI-supported elicitation workflow raised participants'
> self-reported clarity about the process without lowering how much mental
> effort they reported it took.

## Definition / Mechanism

In the same controlled study as [[collaborative-transcript-ai-synthesis]],
the two collaborative conditions differed in whether the elicitation
activities ran through a structured AI platform (Condition B) or were
conducted unaided (Condition A, Nominal Group Technique-style discussion).
Participants in both completed a post-activity questionnaire. Self-reported
process clarity and ease of execution rose with the structured platform:
"Process clarity increased from 3.3 in Condition A to 5.0 in Condition B,
while ease of execution increased from 4.3 to 5.0."[^c1]

Reported cognitive effort did not move with it: "Consequently, although
participants perceived the AI-supported process as clearer and easier to
follow, the results do not suggest a meaningful reduction in the mental
effort associated with requirements elicitation."[^c2]

## Why it matters

This separates two things a structuring intervention on the asking side
could do — make the process *feel* more legible, versus make it *cost*
less to go through — and reports that this platform did the first without
the second. A design that optimizes for reported clarity of an elicitation
workflow is not thereby reducing the participant's effort budget for it.

## Limits

- Self-report from six participants across two groups, one scenario, no
  significance testing — a perception measure, not a validated cognitive
  workload instrument.
- The platform (structured stages, guided applet) and the absence of AI
  are confounded with each other and with group identity; the clarity gain
  cannot be attributed to AI support specifically versus structure alone.
- Effort was reported as "relatively similar" in the source's own words,
  not shown statistically indistinguishable.
