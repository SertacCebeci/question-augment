# Saturation

> One word, four objects. They stop at different points on the *same*
> transcripts, so "we reached saturation" does not say what was reached — and
> only one of the four is about the dimensions of the space.

## The name and what it covers

Saunders et al. (2018) separate four uses that had been circulating as one.[^s1][^s2][^s3][^s4][^s5][^s6][^s7][^s8]
This note holds the bare word; the last column says which note holds each
object.

| Model | What saturates | Logic | Stage | Member note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Theoretical saturation** | properties of a developing category — Glaser & Strauss's original: "no additional data are being found whereby the sociologist can develop properties of the category" | inductive | sampling | none yet — queued as the stall-1 theoretical-sampling gap |
| **Inductive thematic saturation** | appearance of *new* codes or themes (existing ones keep accumulating) | inductive | analysis | [[qualitative-saturation]] |
| **A priori thematic saturation** | exemplification of themes fixed before collection | deductive | sampling | none — named here only as a failure mode |
| **Data saturation** | informational redundancy — new data repeat old data | neither | collection | [[qualitative-saturation]] |

Hennink, Kaiser & Marconi's (2017) **code saturation** and **meaning
saturation** are not a fifth and sixth model. They are two *counting units*
inside inductive thematic saturation: codes are the labels in the frame,
meaning is the understanding of each label. [[qualitative-saturation]] claims
`exact` only for the operational novelty-count rule *once a unit is declared*;
its `close` rows for code, meaning and theoretical saturation are that note
marking what the count is not.

## The differences are measured, not verbal

Hennink, Kaiser & Marconi (2017) ran the two units against one another on a
single 25-interview dataset: **code saturation at 9 interviews, meaning
saturation at 16–24**. Their gloss — code saturation says you have "heard it
all", meaning saturation says you "understand it all". Guest, Bunce & Johnson (2006), 60 interviews across two countries, put saturation inside the first
12 with the metathemes present by 6. Guest, Namey & Chen (2020) give the only
operational rule with parameters — a base of 4 interviews, a run of 2–3, and
a new-information threshold of ≤5% — under which their datasets crossed at
6+2 and 8–9+2. Hennink & Kaiser's (2022) review of 23 empirical tests puts
the range at 9–17 interviews (4–8 focus groups) for homogeneous samples with
narrow aims, and **larger for theoretical saturation than for the code
counts**.

So the spread between the earliest and the latest of the four on the same
material is roughly a factor of two.

## Why it matters

Stall 1 asks what the dimensions of $W$ are. Only **theoretical saturation**
claims anything about that: it is defined over the *properties* of a
category, i.e. over the axes, and it is the latest of the four to arrive
(hennink-2022). The number that travels under the bare word "saturation" is
the widely quoted 9–12 interviews; *this wiki reads that figure as inductive
thematic saturation*, because what guest-2006 and guest-2020 count is the
arrival of new theme labels per interview, not the development of a
category's properties. No cited source classifies the quoted figure that way
— the reading is ours, and it is the inference the rest of this section rests
on. Anyone porting "saturation" as a stopping rule for axis discovery imports
the earliest object under the latest object's name.

**A priori thematic saturation cannot discover an axis at all.** Its theme
set is fixed before collection; it measures only whether each pre-declared
theme has been exemplified. It is the deductive member sharing the inductive
members' name, and it is the substitution the [[efe-planning-as-variational-inference|ambiguity]] permits.

Two positions reject the family rather than sorting it. Malterud, Siersma & Guassora (2016) replace the count with **information power** — five
judgements (study aim, sample specificity, use of established theory, quality
of dialogue, analysis strategy) rather than a novelty count. Braun & Clarke (2021) argue that information redundancy presupposes meaning sitting in the
data waiting to be exhausted; under reflexive thematic analysis interpretation
never exhausts, so no novelty count can terminate anything.

## Limits

- Saunders et al.'s four models are a reading of how the word is used, not a
  tested partition; a large share of published uses cite no model at all and
  cannot be assigned to one.
- **The assignment of the quoted 9–12 figure to inductive thematic saturation
  is this wiki's inference, not a sourced claim.** guest-2006 is filed in the
  literature under "data saturation"; the reading above turns on what was
  counted (new theme labels) rather than on the label the authors used. If
  that reading is wrong, the "earliest object under the latest object's name"
  argument loses its example but not its structure.
- The 9 vs 16–24 figure is one dataset, one study, one coding team. What
  transfers is the **ordering** — codes before meaning, meaning before
  category properties — not the integers. Hennink & Kaiser's 9–17 range is
  conditioned on homogeneous populations and narrow research aims and says
  nothing about heterogeneous ones.
- All four objects are defined over a corpus of many informants sampled in
  sequence. This wiki's usual setting is one informant in one session, where
  "no new codes in the last two interviews" has no counterpart. The
  distinction survives the move; the counts do not.
- Glaser & Strauss's theoretical saturation has no operational test — which
  is why the countable members displaced it, and why the displacement is
  invisible in the citation record.
