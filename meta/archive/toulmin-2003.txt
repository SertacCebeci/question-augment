Information Contents Metrics Accessibility Book description A central theme throughout the
impressive series of philosophical books and articles Stephen Toulmin has published since 1948 is
the way in which assertions and opinions concerning all sorts of topics, brought up in everyday life
or in academic research, can be rationally justified. Is there one universal system of norms, by
which all sorts of arguments in all sorts of fields must be judged, or must each sort of argument be
judged according to its own norms? In The Uses of Argument (1958) Toulmin sets out his views on
these questions for the first time. In spite of initial criticisms from logicians and fellow
philosophers, The Uses of Argument has been an enduring source of inspiration and discussion to
students of argumentation from all kinds of disciplinary background for more than forty years.
