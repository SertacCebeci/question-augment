# Differential visual reasoning triplet

Outcome-reward training of vision-language models rewards only the final textual answer, so the model may treat the image as optional context and answer from linguistic shortcuts. The paper names this the perception-reasoning decoupling: current methods "treat visual inputs as optional context rather than necessary evidence".[^c1]

## Mechanism

For each training instance three views of the same question are built: the original image (anchor), a decremental view with random visual regions masked (counterfactual, evidence lost), and an incremental view with non-semantic diffusion noise added (semantics preserved).[^c2] Two signals follow. Visual sensitivity: the policy on the original input should diverge from the policy on the masked input. Visual robustness: it should stay close to the policy on the noised input.[^c3]

The objective adds to the GRPO reward a term that maximises the KL between original and masked policies, subtracts a term for the KL between original and noised policies, and subtracts an entropy penalty on the masked and noised policies.[^c4] The entropy term exists because constraining consistency directly can be satisfied trivially: the policy collapses into "a high-entropy uniform distribution to trivially minimize the KL divergence".[^c5]

## What was measured

- Ablation on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with GRPO as baseline: adding sensitivity alone gave a substantial improvement, robustness alone also helped, and the full objective was best overall, though single components sometimes won on specific tasks.[^c6]
- The best perturbation strength depends on domain: general reasoning such as maths favoured aggressive masking and noise, while medical reasoning needed milder regularisation.[^c7]
- Structured diffusion noise beat unstructured Gaussian noise in the appendix comparison.[^c8]

## Limits

- Evidence is from RLVR on Qwen2.5-VL 3B and 7B; no transfer to other model families or to text-only grounding is shown.
- Divergence from the masked view is a proxy for using the image. Random masking may remove irrelevant regions, in which case a divergence reward would be spurious (my inference; the paper reports no test of mask relevance).
- The unified model does not win every task, so the sensitivity/robustness complementarity is an average effect.
- It is a training-time objective; it does not say how a questioner should ask, only that an answerer's dependence on evidence can be forced by contrast with evidence-removed inputs.
