# Audience-epistemic mismatch in correction

The claim: whether a correct answer helps depends on the receiver's epistemic orientation (how much they trust institutions, how they validate knowledge), not only on the answer's accuracy. Müller and Holzinger describe credibility as relational: "A correction deemed authoritative by one epistemic community is experienced as confirmation of elite manipulation by another."[^c1] Their framework, Phase-Aware Coherence Detection (PACD), scores each person on a continuous coordinate phi in [0, pi] from pre-intervention questionnaires on four dimensions: institutional trust, scientific epistemology, conspiracy and anti-elite openness, and experiential or alternative epistemology.[^c2] Participants fall into three clusters, from anti-institutional (C1) to pro-institutional (C3).

## What was measured

A pilot (N = 45, students at one Austrian university) randomised people to traditional fact-checking, phase-aware coherence analysis (which shows coherence profiles across several epistemic communities instead of one institutional verdict), or control, on three claims: 5G health effects, urban trees and air quality, mRNA vaccine mechanism.[^c3]

- Traditional fact-checking moved belief away from truth on average (M = -0.39), with a backfire rate of 39.3% (50.0% in the moderate cluster). The phase-aware condition averaged 0.00 with a 13.6% backfire rate, and its effectiveness slope across clusters was near flat (+0.08, against -0.79 for fact-checking).[^c4]
- Fact-checking also raised confidence (+1.32) while accuracy fell. The authors call this confident ignorance: "participants becoming more certain while moving further from truth".[^c5] Phase-aware framing changed confidence least. They conclude that calibration should be scored alongside accuracy.[^c6]
- The harm did not fall where the theory predicted. The largest negative effect of fact-checking was on the pro-institutional cluster (M = -0.79), and the anti-institutional cluster showed 0.00. The authors explain this post hoc as redundancy: re-engaging with a false claim the person already rejects may raise its fluency.[^c7] That explanation was not tested.

## Claim type decides which instrument works

The pilot found a reversal. On the 5G claim, where the evidence is near-unanimous, fact-checking beat the phase-aware condition (M = +1.00 vs -0.12). The authors suspect that presenting several "perspectives" where none legitimately exist adds spurious doubt. On the identity-laden mRNA claim, fact-checking gave the worst mean in the data (-1.42) and phase-aware framing about 0 (-0.18).[^c8] From this they derive a complementarity principle: "epistemic unanimity and low identity-load; phase-aware coherence analysis should be preferred for claims that are contested, nuanced, or identity-adjacent."[^c9]

For asking and answering, the point is that the same true reply has a different expected effect on different audiences. Averaging over audiences hides it: opposite effects cancel, and a mean near zero can sit on top of real harm to some subgroup. The paper pre-registered exactly that prediction (H1).

## Limits

- Tiny and narrow: N = 45, one Austrian student sample, data in German. The anti-institutional cluster had only five people, and the authors say its estimates "are highly unstable", the "most important statistical limitation".[^c10] So the claim about sceptical audiences, the one that motivates the framework, is the least supported.
- The authors call the work an existence proof and pilot, and claim-level comparisons are underpowered.[^c11] All three claims had clear ground truths; genuinely contested claims were untested.
- The phase-aware condition did not beat fact-checking on average (it averaged 0.00). Its advantage is lower variance across audiences and fewer backfires, not better belief change.
- The "phase" and "interference" language is borrowed from quantum-probability models of cognition. In this paper phi is a questionnaire-derived score on one axis. Nothing here tests wave-like interference, so only the analogy (orientation matters, mismatch cancels or inverts signal) transfers; the mathematics does not. This is my reading.
- The broader backfire literature is contested, and the paper itself notes replications questioning its universality; what it treats as robust is the moderation by source-recipient gap.[^c12]
- Mechanism claims (identity threat, redundancy, spurious uncertainty) are the authors' interpretations, not mediation tests.
