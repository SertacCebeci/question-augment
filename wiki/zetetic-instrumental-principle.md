# Zetetic instrumental principle (ZIP)

> Adopting a question obliges you to take the means to answering it — and the
> means include **refusing information you could have had for free**.

## Definition / Mechanism

Jane Friedman (2020, *The Epistemic and the Zetetic*) writes the instrumental[^s1]
norm of inquiry as

> **ZIP** If one wants to figure out $Q^?$, then one ought to take the
> necessary means to figuring out $Q^?$.

It is an ordinary means–end norm with a *question* as the end. Friedman keeps
it deliberately weak: necessary means rather than best means, the `ought`
narrow-scope (having the end is what triggers the obligation), and verdicts
that are "all zetetic things considered" — a non-zetetic reason can override
them. The discussion is restricted to **temporally urgent** inquiries: ones
whose goal is to figure out $Q^?$ by some time $t$ in the near future.

The mechanism is that the means to resolving a question include *staying
focused*, and staying focused means declining what does not serve it.
Friedman's case: counting the windows of the Chrysler Building for an hour
outside Grand Central. Over that hour there is an enormous amount the
inquirer could come to know by perception, and much more it could infer from
evidence it already carries; succeeding at the count requires ignoring almost
all of it. "I succeed at my inquiry then by failing to respect my evidence
for some stretch of time."

That collides with two permissive epistemic norms she states as

> **EP$_a$** If one has excellent evidence for $p$ at $t$, then one is
> permitted to judge $p$ at $t$.
> **KP$_a$** If one is in a position to come to know $p$ at $t$, then one is
> permitted to come to know $p$ at $t$.

ZIP declares impermissible what these always permit. Friedman is explicit
that the trouble is not *violation* — a permission may be left unused — but
**normative coherence**: if ZIP is itself an epistemic norm, then epistemic
normativity issues a permission and a prohibition for the same act, and does
so pervasively, over the course of any ordinary inquiry.

## Known as

| Field | Name there | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| Zetetic epistemology | ZIP | exact — Friedman's own label for this principle |
| Practical reason / instrumental rationality | instrumental principle, means–end principle | close — the general schema "take the means to your ends"; ZIP is the instance whose end is figuring out a question, and it leaves the schema's open questions (necessary vs. best means, wide vs. narrow scope) open |
| Zetetic epistemology | *zetetic norms*, *norms of inquiry* | **not this concept** — the whole class of norms governing inquiry. ZIP is one member; the class also contains norms about which questions to take up, which this note says nothing about |

## Why it matters

**Stall 1.** The criterion- and informant-based rows on Axes explain a
missing axis by a failure or an in-principle blindness: the criterion is
undefined off its parameter space ([[expected-information-gain]]), the model
has no typical set for an unmodelled axis ([[typical-set]]), the informant
does not say it ([[tacit-knowledge]]), the state cannot be asked for at all
([[unknown-unknowns]]), exhaustiveness is unprovable ([[issue-tree]]). ZIP
adds a cause that is not a failure — an agent doing its job *correctly* is
required not to attend to what its current question does not need. That is
also what separates it from the nearest row, [[problem-framing]], which
already denies that narrowness is a failure: framing gives a *prospective
warrant* for choosing an axis set (adopting this working principle will
create the value sought), while ZIP gives a *prohibition on attending* to the
axes the adopted question does not need, which bites after the choice and
against axes the agent can see for free. The narrowness of an inquiry's
working axis set is partly normative, not only epistemic.

**Stall 2.** Every adaptive criterion on Selection — EIG
([[expected-information-gain]]), GBS ([[generalized-binary-search]]),
[[adaptive-submodularity]], [[value-of-information]] — re-scores the
candidate set at each step; the offline rows ([[huffman-coding]]) fix an
order in advance. Neither kind says what an *adopted* question forbids
attending to. ZIP describes selection as a standing commitment instead:
having taken up $Q^?$ under a deadline, the agent owes it the means until it
is resolved, and the permissive norms that would license taking any available
information give no advice at all about what to ignore.

## Transfer

| | |
|---|---|
| **In its home field** | A norm of normative epistemology: what a human inquirer is permitted and required to do while trying to figure something out, tested against intuitions about cases. |
| **Here** | A constraint on a questioning agent's control loop: once a question is adopted under a budget, the agent owes it the means, and free-but-irrelevant information is charged against that budget. |
| **What survives** | The structural claim, which needs no intuitions: an agent that both pursues a question under a bound and accepts every available observation has no coherent policy, because the second consumes what the first requires. The narrow scope survives too — drop the question and the obligation vanishes. |
| **What does not** | The deontic force. Friedman's norms are adjudicated by cases; nothing here gives an exchange rate between a unit of distraction and a unit of progress, a threshold, or a test for what counts as a *necessary* means. And "all zetetic things considered" means ZIP never settles what to do: it is silent on competing ends, which Friedman assumes away. |

## Limits

- The stall-2 claim of this note — commitment rather than per-step scoring —
  rests on Friedman's restriction to **temporally urgent** inquiries. Without
  a deadline the conflict softens; Friedman says ZIP is still in force there
  but that the issues are more complex, so the exclusion this note leans on
  is not established for open-ended inquiry.
- Friedman *argues*, and does not establish, that ZIP is an epistemic rather
  than a practical norm; the opposite case is made in `zetetic-epistemic-separation` (draft dropped).
  Nothing in this note's two consequences depends on which way that goes —
  they need only that both standards bind the same agent.
- Nothing here is measured. The Chrysler Building case is a constructed
  example, and no study cited here compares a focused inquirer with a
  distractible one.
- Read from the author's own preprint (dated 07/2020, marked forthcoming in
  *The Philosophical Review*); wording is quoted from that copy, and no
  section or page of the published version is claimed. Independent
  verification on 2026-09-11 reached only part of it: the ZIP statement, the
  EP$_a$/KP$_a$ statements and the Chrysler Building case are corroborated by
  secondary sources, while the "normative coherence" (rather than violation)
  framing, the "all zetetic things considered" qualifier and the exclusion of
  competing zetetic ends rest on the preprint copy alone and were not
  independently confirmed — and the last of these is what the **what does
  not** row of ## Transfer rests on.
