Introduction

A strong NDCG@10 on Chinese Legal Case Retrieval (LCR) is naturally read as legal-reasoning
ability. The leading benchmarks (LeCaRDv2 (Li
et al., 2024), LeCaRDv1 (Ma et al., 2021), and
CAIL2022 stage-2 (China AI and Law Challenge,
2022)) rank reference judgments for a given fact
pattern and score systems with NDCG@10 over
graded relevance, and recent strong systems reach
NDCG@10 up to ≈ 0.85–0.88: KELLER (Deng
et al., 2024) on the SAILER backbone, Qwen38B-Reranker (Zhang et al., 2025), BGE-M3 (Chen
et al., 2024), and SAILER itself (Li et al., 2023)
(GLIER (Li et al., 2026) reports a higher MAP but
not NDCG@10). Yet it is unclear how much of
that number reflects within-charge legal-reasoning
capacity rather than across-charge separation that
any charge classifier could provide, a distinction a
single leaderboard cannot draw.
Existing LCR evaluation does not separate two
interpretations of strong NDCG@10. Charge-asanchor: a fact pattern about an intentional-injury
crime should retrieve intentional-injury cases, so
leveraging charge identity is a legitimate retrieval
signal. Charge-as-label-leak: if relevance grades

Benchmark validity and dataset artifacts in NLP.
Annotation artifacts repeatedly undermine leaderboard interpretation. Partial-input and hypothesisonly baselines (Gururangan et al., 2018; Poliak
et al., 2018; McCoy et al., 2019; Niven and
Kao, 2019; Kaushik and Lipton, 2018) show that
benchmark labels are often recoverable from a
label-correlated subset of the input without the
target competence. Underpowered comparisons
can themselves manufacture apparent leaderboard
gains (Card et al., 2020), which motivates the
charge-cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals and
explicit power caveats we report throughout. Bowman and Dahl (2021), Raji et al. (2021), and
Freiesleben and Zezulka (2025) formalize benchmark construct validity (with Bean et al. (2025)
documenting validity breaches across hundreds of
recent benchmarks), and Shao (2026) packages
metadata-only screening, an evidence-intervention
statistic, and reader-strength calibration into a general weak-label audit. Shao (2026) also cautions
that metadata predictability alone does not establish evidence dependence; consistent with that
caution we do not infer system-level evidenceindependence from charge predictability, and our
charge-name occlusion (§5.7) and within-charge
residual (§5.1) are instances of the evidenceintervention and non-definitional-remainder checks
his protocol recommends, showing the score is
mostly but not entirely charge by construction.
Construct-validity audits of retrieval benchmarks’
relevance labels, with corrected protocols, also exist (Rodriguez et al., 2023), and behavioral probes
characterize what neural rankers actually rely
on (MacAvaney et al., 2022). We contribute a legalIR instance of this lineage: a model-free chargeoverlap baseline on graded ranking (NDCG@10)
rather than classification accuracy, asking whether
Chinese LCR labels reward charge identity above
legal-reasoning signal. We do not claim the audit
machinery is new; our delta is the ranking-metric
instantiation, the cross-benchmark heterogeneity,
and the legal-domain specialization.

Prior work uses charge as a model-side signal; we
ask whether NDCG@10 on existing Chinese LCR
benchmarks measures predominantly that signal.
Charge labels are routinely available for adjudicated Chinese criminal judgments and have been
widely used as supervisory signal (Deng et al.,
2024; Li et al., 2026), graph structure (Tang et al.,
2024), and ontological hierarchy (Hei et al., 2024).
The question is therefore not whether charge information should be available to a retrieval system (it manifestly helps real downstream tasks)
but whether the metric leaves room for the withincharge legal reasoning that distinguishes a strong
retriever from a charge-only ranking rule. This
anchor-versus-reasoning split is the benchmark’s
own: LeCaRDv2’s Characterization rubric (Li
et al., 2024) factorizes relevance into matching
the crime’s key constitutive elements (which encode charge) and matching key circumstances (the
within-charge facts), so the question we pose is
which of the rubric’s two factors NDCG@10 ends
up rewarding.
Our cross-benchmark evidence (Section 5)
shows the answer depends on the benchmark. On
LeCaRDv2, P(r ≥ 2 | cq =cd ) = 0.97 versus
P(r ≥ 2 | cq ̸=cd ) = 0.22 (lift 4.49), and a
model-free charge-overlap oracle recovers 99.2%
of the BM25-to-KELLER NDCG@10 gap. On
CAIL2022 stage-2 the same oracle falls below
Qwen3-8B-Reranker’s NDCG@10, indicating substantial retrieval-specific headroom beyond the
charge-primary oracle.
We therefore frame CCE as a correction-versusover-control test, not an anti-charge protocol.
Charge-stratified evaluation quantifies how much
of a system’s headline NDCG@10 comes from
cross-charge ranking (where a charge-only rule
explains much of the separation) versus withincharge ranking (where legal-reasoning signal lives).

Construction probe

The construction probe quantifies how chargepredictable the relevance labels are by pool construction, using two label-only statistics. For each
benchmark, we estimate P(r≥2 | cq =cd ) and
P(r≥2 | cq ̸=cd ) over the released qrels. The
lift is the ratio of the first to the second; macroAUC is the area under the binary ROC curve
1[cq =cd ] → 1[r≥2] macro-averaged over queries.
Macro-AUC is undefined for all-positive pools (no
relevance contrast); on LeCaRDv2 this leaves coverage 112/160 (47 all-positive plus 1 all-negative
pool), which formally fails a pre-registered 0.9 coverage gate (144/160). We report macro-AUC regardless, with full disclosure: the excluded pools
are the most charge-homogeneous, so 0.871 is a
conservative estimate (an argument from the excluded pools’ homogeneity, not a formal bound);
the 47/160 all-positive top-heaviness is itself a construction finding; and a coverage-free pooled-AUC
(0.924) corroborates the same direction.

CCE protocol

Definition (3 sentences). CCE evaluates case retrieval under explicit charge control by reporting
(i) charge-stratified NDCG@10 (macro-averaged
across charge strata, N F ) and (ii) counterfactual
occlusion drop ∆drop (baseline N F minus occluded
N F ) per system. CCE uses a cluster bootstrap over
queries grouped by charge (Cameron and Miller,

starting from a two-list reciprocal-rank fusion of
BM25 and a dense retriever, we read the charges

Summary of cross-benchmark verdicts

The four measurements give heterogeneous perbenchmark verdicts (Fig. 1). LeCaRDv2 shows
the strongest charge-confounding pattern (oracle
≈ best-trained, lift 4.49, cascade 76.6%, KELLER
occlusion drop +4.05pp). LeCaRDv1 is partial
(oracle 84.3%, lift 2.00, poor cascade transfer).
CAIL2022 is narrow (oracle OUT OF SPEC, lift
1.76, cascade non-viable) but the occlusion test
still surfaces a single exploratory @10-only signal
on CAIL2022; we do not treat it as confirmatory,
and the LeCaRDv2 conclusion does not depend on
it.
Evidence weight. The LeCaRDv2 conclusion
rests on the model-free oracle and construction
macro-AUC, anchored on KELLER/Qwen3 rather
than the contaminated BGE-M3 (the over-recovery
is anchor-invariant; the v2-vs-v1 heterogeneity follows from the model-free macro-AUC 0.871 vs.
0.759, §4). The cascade corroborates the oracle
without clearing its locked gate; the KELLER occlusion drop is an external diagnostic. The conclusion does not rest on the stratified rank alert or
the exploratory CAIL occlusion cell. CCE surfaces
when charge control changes leaderboard interpretation; it does not by itself adjudicate anchor-vsleak, which needs benchmark-author and statutecoverage context.
